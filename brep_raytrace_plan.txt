IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: FEATURE-TARGETED CHORDS (2026-08-09)
----------------------------------------------------------------

The initial imported comparisons used three centered axis chords and fixed-
seed random sphere chords.  They established reproducibility, full partition
comparison, and reversal checks, but they did not establish feature coverage.
A random line has measure zero probability of passing exactly through a trim,
seam, pole, or surface-tangent configuration.  A high miss fraction can
therefore make a large audit look reassuring while systematically omitting the
events most likely to exercise crack tolerance and grazing cleanup.

`brep-audit --ray-depth-compare` now accepts

  --feature-ray-count=N

and appends up to N deterministic feature chord pairs after the existing axis
and random pairs.  Candidate features are divided into three categories and
selected at stratified indices across each available category:

  * one chord through the underlying surface-domain midpoint of a face, along
    its evaluated oriented normal;
  * one chord through a topological edge midpoint, along an incident face
    normal; and
  * one chord through the same edge point along the face tangent-plane
    conormal, perpendicular to the edge tangent.  This is locally grazing to
    that face and deliberately stresses seam/contact behavior.

The generator evaluates edge points and tangents from the trim and its owning
surface, not from a tessellation.  Thus drawing failures cannot hide a ray
test.  It uses the object bounding sphere to place both endpoints outside the
object envelope, shoots both directions, and records `sample_kind`, target
face, target edge, exact origin, direction, chord length, and global pair
index.  `--ray-pair` replays any generated global pair.  If a singular
derivative, invalid domain, or underlying face midpoint lies outside the
bounding sphere, that candidate is skipped rather than synthesized from
invalid data.

These lines are coverage probes, not geometric oracles.  An underlying face
domain midpoint may lie outside a trimmed face, and an exact edge-conormal
line may correctly be a contact-only miss.  The audit still asks only the
properties it can prove independently on an imported model: finite oriented
solid partitions, equality between fresh requested tree depths, and exact
forward/reverse line invariance.  Analytic implicit-to-BREP and Cauchy-Crofton
gates remain the truth authority.

The first 24-feature replay used the 186-face
`Product_step18_item.s` in
`non_grabcad/0353/0353-02-57.g`.  It added eight face-normal, eight edge-normal,
and eight edge-grazing pairs to the three axis controls.  The preceding random
audit had passed, but these 54 directed rays initially found five depth-
dependent results, five depth-three reversal failures, and seven depth-eight
reversal failures.  Two general cleanup defects explained the complete set.

First, the near-miss stage still contained an older odd-count heuristic that
dropped a NEAR_MISS at the front or back before same-event coalescence and the
new symmetric balanced-run matcher.  A seam root represented twice as
NEAR_HIT at one depth and as NEAR_HIT/NEAR_MISS at the other therefore changed
which distant boundary was discarded.  This heuristic is now removed from
both fixed and allocating paths.  Local coincidence resolution happens first;
the reversal-invariant matcher, not raw parity, decides which observations are
unmatched.  A C++ stream ratchet varies the coincident representative between
NEAR_HIT and NEAR_MISS and requires the same outer interval in both ray
directions.

Second, an exact edge-normal line lay in planar face 11.  At adaptive depth
three that face contributed only its endpoints, while depth eight returned a
130-root sampled continuum along the coplanar face.  The dense cleanup retained
one arbitrary zero-normal sample beside a transverse entry, producing a
0.025-unit interval instead of the real 0.8-unit interval and selecting the
opposite end after reversal.  Two policies combined to cause this:

  * erasing the first invalid/grazing list element advanced over the new first
    element, and the fixed path deliberately copied that historical skip; and
  * `brep_resolved_grazing_pair` checked separation and ENTER/LEAVE direction
    but did not verify its own theorem premise that both endpoint normals are
    grazing.

Both cleanup containers now re-evaluate the element returned by erase.  A
resolved affine-grazing interval requires both endpoint normal dots to be
inside `BREP_GRAZING_DOT_TOL`, in addition to the existing ordered direction,
valid trim, finite separation, and minimum-material-size checks.  A single
zero-normal coplanar sample cannot borrow material-boundary authority from a
transverse hit.  A new C++ continuum stream places three zero-normal samples
between ordinary entry and exit events and requires their complete removal.
This does not weaken the qualified anisotropic grazing case: its two actual
material endpoints are both grazing.

After these repairs all 27 chord pairs on the STEP object pass with zero depth-
partition or reversal differences; the maximum depth-three/depth-eight
endpoint delta is
`5.3813607792108087e-8`.  Exact pair 11 now returns
`[19.511658187986882,20.31165818798695]` at both depths and maps exactly to the
reverse interval.  The dense path still observes 130 raw roots and explicitly
removes the entire coplanar continuum; tree density no longer changes the
solid result.

One non-semantic face-identity difference remains explicitly recorded.  Pair
9 crosses several coincident faces 151--154.  At the reverse entry, depth three
retains face 151 while depth eight retains face 152, whose root is about
`5.7e-14` earlier.  Both have the same oriented normal and material endpoint,
and both reverse to the face-151 forward exit.  This is not a partition
difference or an analytic indication that one face owns the physical event.
The audit now emits `face_difference_ray_indices` and includes those exact
pairs in diagnostic records so future prepared topological ownership work can
ratchet them separately from solid occupancy.

The converted-sphere CTest permanently exercises all three feature categories;
an exact selected-pair replay checks its metadata.  The focused 78-ray affine-
grazing trend and the complete 18-shard non-seam optimized suite pass.  The
Cobb seam gate now
ratchets zero local partition regressions and zero ambiguous changes.  Its old
two-promotion minimum is obsolete: the general legacy cleanup itself now
returns those two correct partitions, leaving nothing for the prepared shadow
comparison to promote.  Retain the independent 16/16 below-envelope crack
repair, reversal, prepared selection, and seam-certificate gates.  The final
416-ray seam run passes with 280 prepared selections, 136 conservative
fallbacks, 24 certified seam pairs, zero leaks, and zero reversal failures.
Rebuilt ASAN/UBSAN cleanup and feature-audit gates pass with no diagnostic.

Feature rays should now accompany every imported stratum, with requested and
actual category populations recorded separately.  They do not replace random
chords, analytic directed partitions, grazing clearance ladders, or Crofton
convergence; they close a distinct coverage hole.  Any new discrepancy must be
replayed by exact global pair and reduced into the C/C++ difficult-event corpus
before production defaults or performance work change.


IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: LOCAL CLEANUP AND REVERSAL INVARIANCE (2026-08-09)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The last object in the initial 14-database ray stratum exposed a second
ordered-hit cleanup defect.  The witness is the 848-face order-six object
`IBEX_SOLID_item.s` in
`original/SC_solid_stp_20260429.g`.  Deterministic pair 22 has origin

  (95.848591855513888, -55.740815264982629, -11.3876265194396)

direction

  (-0.58941604087522914, 0.74193704918254422,
   0.31955929936283961)

and chord length `255.39858798473995`.  Fresh adaptive-depth-three and
depth-eight trees agreed on all nine surface roots.  Before this repair the
forward legacy solve returned no interval, while the reversed solve returned
two.  This was neither a tree-density difference nor a root-corrector
failure.  It was a direction-dependent interpretation of the same boundary
observations.

There were two independent causes.

  1. Historical NEAR_MISS cleanup treated sorted adjacency as event identity.
     It could erase or relabel a near miss next to a clean or near hit with
     the same oriented direction even when their ray parameters were several
     model units apart.  On the forward SC ray, a leaving near miss at about
     `71.71510755` interacted with a clean entry at about `75.263`, although
     they plainly represented different boundaries.  Reversing the ray
     changed their ordering and therefore changed the cleanup result.

  2. The solid-run cleanup ignored leading unmatched exits but discarded the
     complete suffix after any unmatched trailing entry.  A retained
     direction stream equivalent to ENTER, ENTER, ENTER, LEAVE, LEAVE
     therefore lost a valid balanced interior run.  After reversal the
     unmatched event was at the opposite end and the corresponding material
     interval survived.  Prefix/suffix deletion is not invariant under the
     required map that reverses event order and exchanges ENTER with LEAVE.

The repaired legacy fallback uses two general rules.

First, every NEAR_MISS interaction is local.  Near-miss versus non-miss
same-direction removal, consecutive near-miss merging, and near-miss versus
clean/near-hit relabeling all require
`brep_solid_events_coincident`.  Its parameter envelope is the existing
model-derived solid-event tolerance: model distance divided by ray-direction
magnitude plus the established floating-point floor.  Adjacency in a sorted
stream is only a search ordering; it is never by itself geometric identity.
The fixed production workspace and allocating equivalence implementation use
the identical test and allocate no new shot-time storage.

Second, incomplete solid streams retain their maximal balanced observations
symmetrically.  A reverse scan matches every ENTER to an available later
LEAVE and removes only unmatched ENTER events.  A forward scan then matches
every LEAVE to an available earlier ENTER and removes only unmatched LEAVE
events.  This is fixed-storage parenthesis matching.  The surviving balanced
stream is compacted with the existing nonzero-winding union rule: the first
zero-to-one entry and the corresponding one-to-zero exit bound material.
A lone original observation is still retained for the separately qualified
seam/closure transaction.  Under ray reversal, the two scans exchange roles,
so the retained material intervals map exactly under
`t_reverse = chord_length - t_forward`.

This tolerant rule remains legacy fallback behavior.  It must not become
prepared authority.  The prepared event ledger still requires complete
source-box ownership, event identity, orientation, trim/seam disposition,
and zero final winding; an unresolved or incomplete ledger fails the whole
ray back to the legacy path.  Parameter coincidence can prevent a known
local duplicate or crack observation from corrupting a valid fallback, but
it cannot prove that two arbitrary BREP roots are the same topological event.

The C++ cleanup corpus now has two reduced permanent ratchets in addition to
the earlier nested-shell, odd-stream, overlap, and tolerance-cluster cases.
One supplies ENTER/ENTER/ENTER/LEAVE/LEAVE and its exact reversed stream and
requires the same maximal balanced interval.  The other reproduces the SC
ordering with a genuinely coincident near-hit/near-miss pair and a later
noncoincident near-miss/clean pair.  The first pair may be resolved locally;
the second must remain independent.  Both compare the complete retained
partition after reversal, rather than testing a single hit count.

Exact imported replay now returns, in both directions and at both depths,
the two SC pair-22 intervals

  [58.107629653..., 71.715107548...]
  [93.746252051..., 184.158146005...]

under the reversal map.  The complete 70-ray legacy-only SC audit passes with
16 hit rays, 54 misses, 26 material segments, zero invalid partitions, zero
orientation failures, and zero depth or reversal differences.  Its maximum
depth-three/depth-eight endpoint difference is
`3.3064736726373667e-8`.  The post-classifier trees contain 270,839 nodes and
172,669 leaves at adaptive depth three versus 3,494,794 nodes and 1,787,007
leaves at depth eight, plus 60,527 CurveTree leaves.  Peak resident memory was
about 973 MB in the depth-eight comparison.

The full prepared-plus-legacy trace also passes all 70 rays and both depths.
At each depth it reports 35 prepared-eligible rays and 35 attempts, but every
ray falls back: 31 are uncertified, two exhaust the surface-box workspace,
and two fail event classification.  Across those attempts it visits 2,498
candidate spans, constructs 18,336 subdivision boxes, retains 1,235 isolated
boxes, makes 213 fold attempts with eight fold certificates, and records 225
unresolved physical events.  The run took about 7 minutes 44 seconds wall,
559 seconds of CPU, and 950 MB peak RSS on this host.  Trace mode deliberately
executes the legacy solver after a prepared selection, but there were no
selections here; this workload principally measures prepared candidate and
proof work plus dense fallback.

These figures make the later performance ordering more concrete.  Once the
correctness gates are sufficiently broad, profile normal production without
trace duplication and attack object/face/span candidate traversal and proof
volume before micro-optimizing Newton iteration.  A compact immutable
face/span BVH, conservative vectorized coefficient/box rejection, and batched
`vshot` can reduce work for arbitrary BREP volumes.  Revive Abert-style
surface-evaluation or corrector optimizations only where profiles show them
hot.  Matching Abert exactly is not a goal, and neither this one model nor a
shallow special case justifies reducing the production depth from eight.

The optimized broad suite passes 18/18 non-seam shards, the complete seam
suite passes, and the rebuilt ASAN/UBSAN cleanup shard reports no diagnostic.
The seam matrix still has 280 prepared selections, 136 conservative
fallbacks, 24 validated seam pairs, zero leaks, and 14/14 known crack repairs.
The formerly reported two experimental local-cleanup ambiguities are now
zero, because distant events can no longer alter one another merely through
sorted adjacency.  This improvement is an incidental confirmation of the
general locality rule, not authority to weaken seam ownership.

The initial 14-database preparation stratum is now paired with sampled ray
comparisons for all of its selected representative objects, including the
largest SC case.  Imported agreement supplies regression and invariance
evidence, not analytic ground truth.  Before changing the production density
or declaring the solver generally correct, continue the directed
implicit-CSG-to-BREP partition corpus, paired implicit/BREP Cauchy-Crofton
surface-area and volume convergence, signed grazing ladders approaching a
single-point tangent, Cobb seam/crack perturbations, similarity and reversal
checks, and feature-targeted imported rays.  Every difficult solve becomes a
small C/C++ ratchet with its geometry, tolerance, root stream, event stream,
and expected complete partition.


IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: ORIENTED SOLID HIT-RUN ASSEMBLY (2026-08-09)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The next stratified imported audit found a solid-assembly defect rather than
a SurfaceTree-density or numerical-root defect.  The witness is
`second/bearings/UCP-206.g`, `UCP_206_item.s`: a valid, solid 127-face BREP
with three topological face components.  The same deterministic rays were
shot with freshly built depth-three and depth-eight trees.  Both depths
produced the same raw roots, including under reversal, but the historical
ordered-hit cleanup selected different final intervals.  This is important:
making the initial boxes denser cannot repair a direction-dependent fill rule.

Three exact ray-pair witnesses now cover the distinct failure modes:

  * pair 3 exposed an odd stream.  Forward cleanup saw ENTER, LEAVE, ENTER;
    the reversed line saw LEAVE, ENTER, LEAVE.  The old tail deletion chose a
    different interval depending on traversal direction;
  * axis pair 0 exposed an overlapping-boundary run.  Its accepted directions
    were LEAVE, ENTER, ENTER, LEAVE, LEAVE, ENTER.  The historical rule
    retained the last entry and first exit, which is intersection semantics,
    and published two intervals with inverted endpoint normals; and
  * pair 26 exposed the distinction between a true nested shell and multiple
    observations of one seam event.  Its same-direction roots differed by
    about `2.4e-5`, below the model tolerance `5e-4`.  Counting those as two
    independent shell crossings made one ray direction lose the interval.

There was also a concrete initialization bug in both allocating and fixed
cleanup implementations: the code intended to establish the entering sign at
the first processed hit, but tested an iterator/index state that could never
be true.  Correcting that line alone was necessary but insufficient.  The raw
root trace proved that the governing behavioral error was the fill contract.

`brep-audit --ray-depth-compare` now has two diagnostic controls used to make
these cases cheap and reproducible:

  brep-audit --ray-depth-compare --ray-count=0 --ray-pair=N \
    --legacy-only file.g brep

`--ray-pair` replays one indexed deterministic chord pair, and
`--legacy-only` retains direct fresh depth-three/depth-eight SurfaceTree prep
while omitting the prepared local-box solve.  Failure records include every
stored raw root, face component, UV, trim class and distance, normal dot,
direction, adjacency, cleanup-stage count, solver call, and final interval.
The pass gate now treats a nonnegative entry normal or nonpositive exit normal
as an invalid solid partition instead of merely reporting an orientation
counter.  These controls are diagnostic only and do not alter production
shooting.

The legacy solid fallback now applies the following allocation-free rule after
the existing near-miss, near-hit, grazing, and exact-duplicate stages:

  1. Sort retained observations by ray parameter.  Direction is the oriented
     surface result: outward normal dot ray below zero is ENTER and above zero
     is LEAVE.

  2. Define a same-event parameter envelope as

       max(BREP_SAME_POINT_TOLERANCE, rti_tol.dist / |D|)

     plus a 128-epsilon parameter-scale floor.  Consecutive observations with
     the same direction inside this envelope represent one boundary event at
     BRL-CAD model resolution.  Retain the first ENTER and last LEAVE.  Those
     choices exchange under ray reversal and are the outward extrema for
     union occupancy.  Never coalesce opposite directions: they may be a real
     minimum-size grazing interval.

  3. Scan the remaining oriented events with a nonnegative winding depth.
     The first ENTER taking the depth from zero to one is a material entry.
     Nested ENTER events raise the depth.  The LEAVE returning depth from one
     to zero is the material exit.  Thus disjoint shells produce separate
     intervals, overlapping outward shells produce their union envelope, and
     a correctly inward-oriented cavity opens and closes a void.

  4. Remove only provably unmatched observations, symmetrically: a reverse
     scan removes ENTER events with no later LEAVE, then a forward scan removes
     LEAVE events with no earlier retained ENTER.  This retains the maximal
     balanced observations without bridging a gap, and the scans exchange
     under reversal.  Preserve a sole unmatched observation for the separately
     certified seam/closure transaction; the Cobb crack resolver needs that
     boundary witness.  This rule supersedes the original prefix/suffix
     deletion described when the UCP checkpoint was first implemented.
     A prepared result does not use this heuristic: incomplete event ownership
     or final winding still causes atomic whole-ray fallback.

  5. Compact the bounded `brep_hit_workspace` in place.  The allocating list
     path implements the identical event choices for equivalence checking,
     but no new allocation was introduced into the fixed production cleanup.
     Plate and non-solid contracts retain their historical first-hit behavior
     and do not use solid winding.

The independent analytic oracle is deliberately stronger than replaying an
imported model.  The C++ hard corpus converts two radius-five implicit spheres
centered at x=-3 and x=+3 to BREP, appends both closed shells into one BREP
primitive, and compares it with the implicit CSG union.  Along the x axis the
oracle interval is `[12,28]`.  The old cleanup returned only the intersection
`[18,22]` in both directions; the nonzero-winding rule returns `[12,28]` in
both.  A separate disjoint-sphere union continues to return multiple
intervals.  Pure cleanup tests ratchet nested, odd, leading/trailing,
tolerance-clustered, reversed, and singleton-closure streams.

The tolerance/healing boundary is explicit.  Same-direction observations
inside model resolution are one event; true shell crossings farther apart are
counted.  This is the best available legacy fallback policy, not permission to
erase uncertain topology.  The prepared pipeline should ultimately replace
parameter-only coalescence with certified root/event identity from face,
edge, trim, and source-box ownership, then apply the same nonzero-winding
occupancy contract.  Any unsupported identity remains whole-ray fallback.

The complete Cobb seam gate initially caught an over-aggressive version that
discarded a singleton.  Retaining it restored all 14 below-envelope repairs,
both prepared partition promotions, classifier/similarity invariance,
ambiguous-correspondence fallback, and the 416-ray bowed-seam matrix.  The
final optimized gate before the trim-endpoint addition passed all 18
registered ray shards.  The standalone
seam shard completed in 383 seconds on this host; the other 17 shards passed
in 157 seconds when run concurrently.

The final 70-ray UCP audit has zero depth differences, reversal mismatches,
invalid rays, or orientation anomalies at both requested depths.  It returns
20 material segments on 16 hit rays.  Eight rays select the prepared path;
six fall back for surface-box capacity and 56 for incomplete certification.
Depth three builds 7,620 nodes/5,139 leaves, while depth eight builds 170,816
nodes/84,360 leaves.  The prepared trace visits 821,770 subdivision boxes,
isolates 105,414 boxes, attempts 104,323 folds, and records 103,100 box
overflow attempts.  Those figures are now the performance baseline, not a
reason to weaken the correctness gate.

Final ASAN/UBSAN replay passes the pure cleanup corpus, the hard analytic and
Cauchy-Crofton corpus, and imported pairs 0, 3, and 26 without a diagnostic.
The three parallel instrumented UCP replays each peak near 1.13 GB, so keep
them as separate sanitizer shards when memory is constrained.  Leak scanning
remains disabled because of the host ptrace wrapper.

Correctness remains the immediate authority.  Once the event/root corpus and
prepared fallback boundaries are sufficiently locked down, follow through on
the Abert-informed performance phase in Section 10: profile first, eliminate
global face/span work and dense seed trees, compact immutable data, add
conservative SIMD and real batched traversal, and optimize the corrector only
if it remains hot.  The UCP workload supplies a concrete before/after target.


IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: TRIM-ENDPOINT CLASSIFICATION (2026-08-09)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The next imported comparison demonstrated why depth eight must be treated as
a continuity reference rather than a truth oracle.  In
`nist/NIST_MBE_PMI_11.g`, `Document_item.s`, deterministic axis pair 1 is

  origin=(-7.1054273576010019e-15, -58.828516522601504,
          -0.075000000000000622)
  direction=(0, 0.99999999999999989, 0)
  chord=117.65703304520302

Before repair, adaptive depths zero through seven returned only
`[74.828516522601504,90.328516522601504]`; depth eight also returned the
physically symmetric first interval
`[27.328516522601504,42.828516522601504]`.  Both results were independently
stable under ray reversal.  The discrepancy was therefore not detectable by
the reversal invariant alone, and the dense answer was not automatically
entitled to win.

Raw-root tracing localized the difference to face 7 at
`t=27.328516522601504`, UV approximately
`(4.71238898038469,1.695)`, with normal dot `-1`.  Every tree depth found the
same surface root.  At depths zero through seven the leaf and full CurveTree
called it a clean trimmed miss.  At depth eight a leaf marked
`m_checkTrim=false,m_trimmed=false` accepted it without a point query, while
the full CurveTree still called it outside.  Thus the deep tree happened to
mask a trim-classifier defect; it did not improve the nonlinear solve.

Topology and geometry establish the intended result.  Face 7 is a cylindrical
patch with outer loop 9 and UV domain `u=[0,2*pi], v=[0,2.04]`.  Its loop
covers approximately `u=[pi,2*pi], v=[0.27,1.77]`, placing the root plainly
inside.  The upper boundary is split between trims 39 and 40.  Trim 39 ends
only about `2.2e-8` in u beyond the query, while trim 40 begins about `1.6e-4`
on the other side.  This is a real imported endpoint discrepancy of the sort
the solid classifier must tolerate without turning arbitrary outside points
into hits.

The specific implementation bug was a contradictory two-stage screen.
`CurveTree::isTrimmed` admitted trim leaves whose u bounding boxes were within
`BREP_UV_DIST_FUZZ` (`1e-6`) of the query.  `BRNode::isTrimmed` then repeated
the u test with exact bounds.  Trim 39 was therefore counted as a candidate
but silently converted to an under-trim result rather than evaluated at its
near endpoint.  Only lower crossings remained, producing the wrong winding.

`BRNode::isTrimmed` now uses the same `BREP_UV_DIST_FUZZ` envelope as candidate
selection and clamps the admitted u value to the leaf endpoint before curve
evaluation.  This is deliberately a narrow structural-UV consistency rule.
It does not widen
`BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE`, the model tolerance, or same-event hit
coalescence; points beyond the structural envelope remain outside.

A fast C++ regression, `rt_brep_ray --trim-classifier-only`, builds a planar
outer loop with the same split-upper-trim discrepancy.  It requires the
near-endpoint scanline to be inside with two candidates and a trim distance of
0.5, while a scanline more than `BREP_UV_DIST_FUZZ` beyond the endpoint must
remain outside.  This turns the imported observation into a source fixture
rather than depending indefinitely on one `.g` file.  `brep-audit` failure
records now also expose fixed-cleanup stages plus leaf/full-face trim status,
distance, candidate population, hit class, and equivalence counts.

After repair, exact pair 1 agrees at every candidate depth zero through seven
against depth eight: four hits, two material intervals, no invalid rays,
orientation anomalies, reversal mismatches, or leaf/full-face classifier
differences.  The complete depth-three/depth-eight 70-ray audit also passes:
10 hit rays, 14 segments, maximum endpoint delta `1.42e-14`, and 44 prepared
selections at each depth.  Depth three uses 2,146 nodes/1,556 leaves versus
62,790/31,932 at depth eight.  The reduced classifier test is a permanent
CTest shard.

The final optimized validation is 19/19 registered ray shards on the exact
clamped implementation.  The classifier plus comprehensive seam/crack matrix
passed 2/2 in 234.82 seconds; the other 17 shards passed concurrently in
175.53 seconds, including the exhaustive interval, Crofton, pole, grazing,
endpoint, contact, non-isoparametric, and defect gates.  The rebuilt
ASAN/UBSAN classifier fixture and exact NIST depth-three/depth-eight pair also
pass with no diagnostic.  Leak scanning remains disabled for the established
host ptrace limitation.

This result strengthens, rather than reverses, the staged performance plan.
First remove answer dependence on accidental leaf preclassification, complete
the imported and analytic correctness gates, and retain depth eight in
production.  Then profile the certified pipeline and pursue the broadly
applicable Abert-informed work: face/span acceleration, compact immutable
data, conservative batched rejection, a true `vshot`, and corrector
optimization where measurement justifies it.  No requirement exists to match
Abert's implementation exactly, and no tree-depth reduction is accepted
merely because this one classifier case now works at depth zero.


IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: LOOP-TOPOLOGY TRIM CLASSIFICATION (2026-08-09)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The NIST endpoint repair exposed a broader problem in the historical trim
classifier.  It inferred face material from the direction flags of whichever
BRNode happened to be closest above a query.  It did not evaluate each BREP
loop independently, did not subtract inner loops explicitly, and assumed a
single +V parameter-space ray even when V was periodic.  SurfaceTree prep then
copied filtered trim pointers into every BBNode leaf.  Besides making the
answer dependent on leaf density, this multiplied CurveTree reference storage
through the largest acceleration structures.

The replacement contract is ordinary point-in-loop parity with BREP topology
and periodic quotient geometry made explicit:

  1. Normalize the query into every closed surface domain.  Choose a parity
     ray along a nonperiodic parameter direction when one is available: use
     +U when V is closed, otherwise +V.  This prevents a half-infinite ray
     from repeatedly meeting periodic copies in its own direction.

  2. During CurveTree construction, collect immutable BRNode leaf references
     per ON_BrepLoop.  Generate only periodic images needed to overlap the
     native domain in the coordinate transverse to the ray.  Do not replicate
     along the ray axis.  Keep separate vectors sorted by minimum U and V so
     either ray direction can stop scanning once later leaves cannot cross.
     Cache serialization is unchanged: this index contains pointers, offsets,
     loop type, and derived endpoint flags, and is rebuilt after ordinary or
     deserialized CurveTree construction.

  3. Each monotone trim leaf implements a half-open +V crossing and a
     symmetric half-open +U crossing.  Exact shared vertices therefore have
     one owner.  Curve proximity is a separate fixed-workspace calculation;
     it supplies trim distance and adjacency for crack policy but does not
     silently substitute closest-curve orientation for parity.

  4. Count crossings separately per loop.  Material is inside at least one
     declared outer loop and inside no declared inner loop.  Unknown loops use
     parity as a conservative compatibility rule.  This remains correct when
     imported inner curves carry the same orientation as outer curves; loop
     type, not incidental curve direction, defines a hole.

  5. Spend endpoint tolerance only at a measured join displacement.  Prep
     compares each trim start with the preceding trim end and each trim end
     with the following trim start, taking the shortest periodic coordinate
     difference when appropriate.  A leaf may extend its lower half-open
     coordinate only when its corresponding topological join is displaced
     toward that lower coordinate.  Exact joins receive no extension.  The
     query must still lie within BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE; a larger gap does
     not globally enlarge the loop, and the other side of the gap does not
     acquire a second owner.

This last distinction fixed a concrete UCP failure.  On face 8 of
`second/bearings/UCP-206.g`, pair 3 produced a root at UV approximately
`(0.4934774783,0.3796082907)`.  A converged independent sampled-loop oracle
placed the point outside by about 0.01573 UV units, beyond the 0.005 edge-miss
allowance.  Production nevertheless counted three crossings.  Trim 44
already crossed the query exactly; trim 45 met it at the same endpoint to
roundoff but received a blind 0.005 extension, changing the correct even

IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: STRUCTURAL KNOTS AND A DEPTH PLATEAU (2026-08-09)

SurfaceTree construction must distinguish structural decomposition from
adaptive density.  Closed-surface seams and all interior NURBS span knots are
correctness boundaries: a leaf that crosses one may combine derivative,
parameterization, bounding, or root behavior from distinct Bezier-like
subdomains.  They therefore cannot share the optional flatness-depth budget.
Construction now exhausts seam and knot splits first, then restarts recursion
at zero for adaptive flatness/straightness refinement.  The requested
--surface-tree-depth value is an adaptive budget only; measured BBNode depth
includes structural and adaptive levels and may exceed it.

This corrects an ordering defect in the earlier controlled-depth experiment.
The common recursion limit was tested before the structural-knot branch, so a
small limit could terminate a leaf before all interior knots were split.  A
new libbrep regression constructs a smooth NURBS surface with six U spans and
five V spans at adaptive depth zero.  It requires 30 leaves and directly
checks that none crosses an interior span boundary.  This contract is
shape-independent and is a precondition for interpreting any reduced-density
ray result.  A future resource guard may bound malformed input, but it must
report invalid preparation rather than silently make a knot-crossing leaf.

The current clean-interior regular-stream theorem provides complete ownership
of independently certified regular events without taking parameter-space
hulls across faces or spans.  Each same-face/span physical component must own
all of its terminal boxes and roots, exclude its local complement with fixed

determinant enclosure.  The complete stream must alternate ENTER/LEAVE and
respect BRL-CAD's minimum material-segment size before it atomically replaces

that component complete and refuses to publish.  The sparse legacy fallback
then retains only one root.  This supplies the next precise algorithm target:

  * collect all observations in the connected component transactionally;
  * identify exactly two distinct, opposite-direction regular root groups;
  * contract two disjoint local neighborhoods with fixed workspace;
  * certify existence/uniqueness, determinant sign, and outward ray-parameter
    enclosure independently for each root;
  * split at both local boxes and use direct/rotated Bernstein bounds to
    exclude every remaining portion of the complete component hull; and
  * publish both events atomically only after every root and box has one
    proved owner.

This theorem is the regular/regular analogue of the existing joint
boundary/regular component proof.  It must retain the same stack, depth, and
visit bounds, fail conservatively, and add telemetry and a focused regression
ratchet.  Only after it moves the grazing floor downward should another
default-reduction experiment be considered.  Limit seven is a promising
intermediate measurement, not yet a production-default decision.  A cache
version change, full sanitizer gates, the complete parallel suite, and STEP
corpus profiling are mandatory before any production default changes.

The experiment also sharpens what convergence means.  As a secant approaches
one-point tangency, two physical crossings necessarily coalesce into a
zero-transition contact and then disappear.  Stable behavior is therefore
defined relative to geometry and minimum-segment tolerances: root separation
and the material interval must trend monotonically, contacts must not create
odd-hit solid leaks, and transform/scaling tests must preserve the normalized
classification.  Requiring the raw hit count to remain continuous at the
singular tangent would be mathematically impossible and is not an acceptance
criterion.

The final six material fallbacks exposed two distinctions which are now part
of the production design:

  1. An edge sector's line_before_state and line_after_state are angular,
     topological observations.  Their validity must not be vetoed by the
     legacy fixed grazing cutoff applied to a face normal at a discrepant trim
     lift.  Conditioning is checked where authority is published: every
     incident surface root must be finite and non-grazing and every relevant
     local component must pass its bounded analytic proof.

  2. A sub-T trim displacement may make the literal trim frame report contact
     even though the complete roots on both incident faces agree on one solid
     crossing direction.  Such a result is only a provisional
     tolerance-healed transition.  It requires the strict prep-time seam-gap
     authorization, an exact represented-edge witness, complete and
     conflict-free incident root/box ownership, non-grazing roots on both
     faces, and a common ENTER or LEAVE direction.  It may publish only when
     the joint boundary/regular component theorem proves exactly two local
     roots, excludes the component complement, certifies a constant
     determinant sign in the boundary root's own contraction box, applies
     m_bRev, and obtains the same direction.  Any failure preserves the
     whole-ray fallback.

This rule does not turn a geometrically near ray into an edge ray, does not
classify an exact tangent as a crossing, and does not infer orientation from
root iteration alone.  Promoted ownership boxes are boundary boxes, not
contact boxes.  Dedicated telemetry records candidate failure stages and
published tolerance transitions.

IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINT: CERTIFIED LOCAL COMPONENTS (2026-08-09)
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The first production form of the proposed local, allocation-free pipeline is
now present in src/librt/primitives/brep/brep.cpp, with trace telemetry in
src/librt/librt_private.h and its isolated Cobb ratchet in
src/librt/tests/brep_ray.cpp.  brep_current_state.txt contains exact current
counts, verification status, and continuation instructions.

Three related authorities must remain distinct in subsequent work:

  1. A regular component theorem may replace several connected coarse
     terminal boxes by one physical root only after strict Krawczyk inclusion
     over their full hull, or a local contraction plus complete Bernstein
     exclusion of the remaining hull.  It separately certifies determinant
     sign and an outward ray-parameter interval.  Numerical roots and coarse
     boxes are observations, not authority.

  2. A manifold-edge event requires an exact represented-line witness.  The
     preferred witness is the 3-D edge locus.  When that locus is discrepant,
     coincident incident trim lifts may supply the witness only if both are on
     the ray to coordinate roundoff and both lie inside the independently
     certified prep-time edge discrepancy envelope.  Model tolerance may
     authorize this topological representation choice; it may not snap a
     merely near ray onto the edge.

  3. When one exact boundary root and one nearby regular root occupy the same
     terminal boxes, a joint theorem uses two disjoint local contraction
     neighborhoods and excludes their complete component complement.  A wide
     certified neighborhood is useful for root ownership, but the published
     regular event needs the smallest nested box that independently certifies
     root existence/uniqueness, orientation, duplicate observations, and t
     enclosure.  The exact boundary event receives its own roundoff-scale t
     interval.  Reusing one box for both jobs is either numerically fragile
     when too small or topologically ambiguous when too large.

The generalized complement walk aligns boxes with certified-neighborhood
boundaries before applying direct and rotated Bernstein exclusion.  It is
bounded by the existing 64-entry fixed subdivision stack, depth 24, and 8,192
visits.  All decisions are transactional: no source-box disposition or event
is published until every observed root and box has exactly one proved role.
This checkpoint deliberately does not alter SurfaceTree density.  It first
establishes the right local authority and a regression ratchet; later prep
experiments may remove dense levels only where this authority preserves the
same directed partitions, grazing trend, seam policy, and paired
Cauchy-Crofton results.


1. PURPOSE AND SUCCESS CRITERIA
-------------------------------

BRL-CAD's BREP primitive must interpret a trimmed NURBS boundary as the
boundary of the solid the modeler intended.  This is subtly different from
reporting every mathematical intersection with each underlying surface.
Imported patches and their trims may disagree by small amounts, adjacent
surface parameterizations may have radically different scales, and a ray may
pass through the uncertainty region at a seam.  A mathematically literal
per-face answer can therefore leak through a nominally closed solid.

At the same time, the raytracer must not bridge unrelated geometry or merge
nearby, legitimate roots.  Near-tangent rays are particularly difficult:
several roots can be close in model space, ordinary Newton iteration becomes
ill-conditioned at a tangent, and a coarse starting guess can converge to the
wrong root or to no root.  The current response has been to build very dense
surface trees.  That improves the guesses but makes prep extremely expensive
in time and memory.

The target result has all of the following properties:

  * Correct solid partitions for well-formed BREPs, including rays through
    poles, surface seams, trim boundaries, vertices, and near-tangent regions.

  * Tolerance-aware closure of cracks between topologically adjacent faces.
    A sub-tolerance disagreement must not turn an intended solid ray into a
    miss.  Geometry beyond the authorized tolerance must not be silently
    healed.

  * Explicit separation of numerical convergence tolerance, UV trim
    uncertainty, model-space topology tolerance, ray-distance clustering,
    and BRL-CAD's minimum meaningful segment thickness.

  * A much smaller prepared acceleration structure.  Difficult regions are
    refined locally during a shot, using bounded, allocation-free workspace.

  * Deterministic behavior independent of thread scheduling.  Prep and shot
    must not use stale surface-specific data or random perturbations.

  * Measurable correctness against analytic implicit solids before relying on
    converted STEP geometry whose true answer is not independently known.

  * Original CSG raytracing is the primary behavioral oracle for every
    implicit-solid-to-BREP conversion.  Identical rays must produce identical
    ordered material intervals, and identical Cauchy-Crofton line samples must
    converge to the same crossing and chord statistics.  This paired test is
    the strongest promotion gate for the NURBS BREP solver, not an optional
    aggregate smoke test.

  * Predictable limiting behavior as a secant ray approaches a one-point
    graze.  The two roots must approach each other continuously, remain
    resolvable while their interval is meaningful at BRL-CAD tolerance, become
    one zero-transition contact at exact tangency, and become a miss outside
    the surface.  There must be no alternating hit/miss bands or odd-hit leaks
    near the limit.

  * Drawing behavior that does not depend on constructing the raytracer's
    dense surface tree and that remains useful for trimmed, closed, and highly
    curved surfaces.

The implementation should be staged.  The current dense-tree route remains a
correctness oracle and bounded fallback until the replacement passes directed
ray, Cauchy-Crofton, and corpus tests.


2. CURRENT IMPLEMENTATION: OBSERVATIONS AND IMPLICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Surface-tree construction and the recent split-cache change

The relevant change is commit:

  23eabb2c056fc7d0c4ccc6c5d9299f72eedbb432
  "Add libbrep changes from STEP work"

The old SurfaceTree::hasSplit implementation kept file-static surface
pointers, domains, span counts, and allocated span vectors indexed by
bu_parallel_id().  This was unsafe for two independent reasons documented in
the new code:

  * bu_parallel_id() is not a unique identity for arbitrary C++ worker
    threads used by STEP conversion.

  * A cached ON_Surface pointer survived destruction of its surface.  If the
    allocator later reused that address, stale knot data could be selected
    for a different surface.

The new implementation puts a SurfaceSplitCache on each SurfaceTree.  It owns
the two surface domains and std::vector span arrays and dies with the tree.
This is the correct ownership boundary for construction and must not be
replaced with the old static scheme.  The useful optimization is instead to
make SurfaceTree a temporary builder and release the split cache immediately
after its prepared products are transferred to brep_specific.

Current source locations:

  * include/brep/surfacetree.h
  * src/libbrep/opennurbs_ext.cpp, SurfaceSplitCache,
    SurfaceTree::SurfaceTree, SurfaceTree::~SurfaceTree, and
    SurfaceTree::hasSplit
  * include/brep/defines.h, BREP_MAX_FT_DEPTH (currently 8)

SurfaceTree first subdivides at closed seams and surface knots, then according
to sampled flatness and straightness.  Structural splits must be exhausted;
the configured maximum applies only to the subsequent adaptive phase.  The
reported physical BBNode depth includes both phases.  An adaptive maximum of
eight can produce a large number of BBNodes per face.  CurveTree data and
per-leaf trim lists add additional memory.  In
particular, BBNode::Stl contains a std::vector of children and a std::list of
trim nodes above the leaf.  Dense subdivision magnifies the cost of all of
these structures, not just bounding boxes.

Tree construction also needs an explicit failure contract.  A rational
surface can have finite, positive control weights yet be numerically singular
under the OpenNURBS normal threshold at a required local midpoint.  A failed
midpoint normal or bounding node must return an invalid SurfaceTree, not call
`bu_bomb` and not silently omit that child.  Propagate any child failure to
the root, validate every per-face tree after the parallel builders join, and
transfer no root or CurveTree unless the complete face set is valid.  If one
face is unavailable, fail preparation for the entire BREP; tracing an
incomplete solid is not a conservative fallback.  Public SurfaceTree queries
and plotting helpers must tolerate the resulting invalid builder state.

2.2 Prepared-object ownership problems

brep_build_bvh constructs one SurfaceTree per face in parallel.  It adds each
SurfaceTree root as a child of the top-level BVH, stores the SurfaceTree in
ON_BrepFace::m_face_user.p, frees only the temporary pointer array, and
contains the explicit comment that the SurfaceTrees are never destroyed.

That leak currently masks an ownership conflict:

  * SurfaceTree::~SurfaceTree deletes both m_root and m_ctree.

  * The top-level BVH also treats the transferred root as its child and
    recursively deletes BBNodes.

  * BBNodes refer to the CurveTree but do not own it.

  * brep_specific_delete deletes only the ON_Brep and top-level BVH.

  * Cache deserialization constructs one CurveTree per face and does not
    retain an owner for those trees.

No reader of m_face_user.p was found in the repository.  It should not be
used as an implicit ownership mechanism.

The fix should make brep_specific a C++ owner of:

  * the prepared ON_Brep;
  * the top-level BVH and its recursively owned BBNode roots;
  * one CurveTree per prepared face;
  * immutable Bezier-span and seam metadata proposed below; and
  * any diagnostic/prep statistics retained for reporting.

SurfaceTree should expose explicit transfer/release operations, or be
replaced internally by a builder-result object that transfers its root and
CurveTree exactly once.  The temporary builder and its SurfaceSplitCache can
then be destroyed.  Deserialization must populate the same owner containers
as ordinary prep.  Delete the BVH before deleting CurveTrees so no BBNode can
observe an already destroyed tree during teardown.

At the time of the original source study, the prep cache in
rt_brep_prep_serialize was version zero.  It writes a CurveTree followed by a
BBNode tree for every face.  The structural-knot checkpoint bumps that version
to one without changing the byte layout: version-zero trees may have stopped
before an internal knot and are therefore rejected and rebuilt.  Any later
span, tolerance, adjacency, or serialization-layout change requires another
version bump.  Old cache entries must be rejected and rebuilt, not partially
interpreted.

2.4 Current ray/surface solve

rt_brep_shot asks the master BBNode hierarchy for every intersected leaf using
std::list, then calls utah_brep_intersect for each leaf.  The intersection
solver represents the ray as the intersection of two planes perpendicular to
the ray.  For a surface S(u,v), it solves:

  f(u,v) = (S(u,v) - O) dot P1 = 0
  g(u,v) = (S(u,v) - O) dot P2 = 0

where O is the ray origin and P1/P2 span the plane normal to the ray.  The
Jacobian is:

          [ Su dot P1   Sv dot P1 ]
      J = [ Su dot P2   Sv dot P2 ]

The code explicitly inverts the 2x2 determinant.  It normally starts from the
leaf midpoint.  If the leaf midpoint normal has |normal dot ray| < 0.2, it
also tries the four corners.  It can return at most five roots from a leaf.

Important correctness and robustness issues are visible in the current code:

  * It evaluates the initial residual but does not accept an already
    converged seed before testing the Jacobian determinant.  An exact root at
    a surface singularity can therefore be rejected before success is
    considered.

  * The determinant "jitter" at a singular initial seed is
    uv += 0.1 * random * (uv0 - uv).  At the first iteration uv == uv0, so the
    perturbation is exactly zero.  It can repeat for all 100 iterations.

  * drand48 makes the solve nondeterministic and has undesirable cross-platform
    and threading properties.

  * The return value of Ev1Der is not checked.  Intermediate values and
    normals are not checked for finiteness, and the result of normal Unitize
    is not validated.

  * A fixed absolute determinant threshold of 1e-6 is not scale- or
    parameterization-invariant.

  * UV is clamped back into the leaf after every step.  Clamping can turn
    divergence into a stalled boundary point and obscure a root in the
    neighboring leaf.

  * The first-step corner direction rejection is heuristic and can discard a
    valid path on a curved or badly parameterized patch.

  * Step damping is limited to three halvings, followed by four non-improving
    iterations, for no geometric reason.

  * Duplicate detection is first performed in UV.  The same model-space root
    can have different UV coordinates on a seam, and similar UV coordinates
    do not imply similar model-space points under arbitrary parameterization.

  * A fixed five-root array assumes the dense leaves have isolated the
    surface sufficiently.  It is not a general root-count guarantee.

  * A fixed ROOT_TOL of 1e-7, BREP_INTERSECTION_ROOT_EPSILON of 1e-6,
    BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE of 5e-3, BREP_SAME_POINT_TOLERANCE of 1e-6,
    and BREP_GRAZING_DOT_TOL of 1.7453e-5 mix several unrelated meanings.

The reported sphere-pole failure is therefore plausible even when a particular
prepared sphere happens to succeed because another leaf seed approaches the
pole.  An axial test of the current local build did produce the expected
two-unit interval for one implicit-to-BREP sphere, but that does not remove
the exact-seed bug above.  It means only that the current subdivision and seed
set happened to route around it in that case.

libbrep already contains useful singularity-aware routines:

  * surface_EvNormal in include/brep/util.h and PullbackCurve.cpp
  * IsAtSingularity in include/brep/pullback.h and PullbackCurve.cpp

surface_EvNormal attempts one-sided normal evaluation at singular boundaries.
The ray solver should reuse or generalize this behavior instead of building a
normal solely as Unitize(CrossProduct(Su,Sv)).

2.5 Current trim and solid-hit repair

After Newton returns a UV point, utah_brep_intersect calls
BBNode::isTrimmed(..., BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE).  It creates:

  * CLEAN_HIT for an interior point away from a trim;
  * NEAR_HIT for an accepted point close to a trim; and
  * NEAR_MISS for a trimmed-away point close to a trim.

The associated BRNode supplies an adjacent face index when CurveTree prep can
identify one.  rt_brep_shot then runs several ordered heuristic passes:

  * remove near misses next to same-direction accepted hits;
  * turn adjacent-face pairs of same-direction near misses into CRACK_HIT;
  * remove opposite-direction near-miss pairs;
  * collapse same-direction clean hits on adjacent faces;
  * remove near misses from the ends of an odd list;
  * perform similar collapsing for NEAR_HIT;
  * discard trimmed, out-of-bounds, and grazing candidates;
  * merge same-distance candidates, deleting both if their normal signs
    disagree;
  * remove consecutive entries or exits according to an assumed sign; and
  * discard a final hit in some remaining odd-list cases.

The final solid path emits segments only when the remaining hit count is even.
This logic encodes valuable experience about real cracks, but it loses the
geometric evidence needed to make principled decisions.  It also depends on
pass order, assumes the first surviving sign identifies entry, and removes all
grazing hits before determining whether a close pair represents a thin but
valid interval.

The replacement should preserve near-trim candidates and their uncertainty,
then resolve them as local topological events.  It should not discard them
early or infer solid state from hit-count parity alone.

2.6 Tolerance-unit conflation

include/brep/defines.h describes BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE as critical to solid
raytracing and trimming and notes that it may need model-scale adaptation.  It
is currently 0.005.  The same number is used in contexts with incompatible
units:

  * as a distance from a trim in surface parameter space; and
  * as a model-space amount used to grow surface-leaf bounding boxes.

This cannot be invariant under UV rescaling or distorted parameterizations.
A surface domain of [0,1]x[0,1] and an equivalent domain of
[0,1000000]x[0,0.000001] must not get different crack behavior.

OpenNURBS already distinguishes topology tolerances:

  * ON_BrepVertex::m_tolerance is a model-space bound between the vertex and
    applicable edge or lifted trim endpoints.

  * ON_BrepEdge::m_tolerance is the maximum model-space distance from the 3D
    edge curve to the surface of any face using that edge.

  * ON_BrepTrim::m_tolerance[0] and [1] are u- and v-parameter accuracy of the
    2D trim relative to a closest surface parameter corresponding to the edge.

ON_UNSET_VALUE means the value needs to be computed; zero has a specific
near-machine-tolerance meaning.  These values must be validated and used in
their native units.

BRL-CAD's default rti_tol.dist is 0.0005 mm (BN_TOL_DIST).  rt_boolfinal
forces intervals whose entry and exit differ by this amount to zero thickness
and fuses nearby partitions.  include/rt/func.h contains an extensive Crofton
discussion showing that sub-tolerance slivers can appear or disappear by ray
angle.  BREP shot semantics must be designed with this downstream behavior in
mind; otherwise BREP may manufacture distinctions that librt later erases or
may inconsistently preserve them only at oblique angles.

2.7 Current drawing behavior

The ordinary rt_brep_plot path is primarily an edge plot.  It uses
ON_Curve_PolyLine_Approx for edges and adds special surface-tree isocurves only
for selected closed ON_SumSurface and ON_RevSurface cases.  The adaptive plot
path uses view-derived point spacing, but its edge subdivision is driven
mostly by endpoint distance rather than a complete chord-error/curvature test.

The shaded/tessellated path is separate and uses brep_facecdt_plot.  Drawing
should remain separate from the ray acceleration structure.  Requiring a
dense ray SurfaceTree to obtain a usable visual would preserve the prep cost
we are trying to remove.


3. REQUIRED SEMANTICS: FIVE DIFFERENT TOLERANCE JOBS
----------------------------------------------------

The implementation should introduce an internal BrepTolerancePolicy prepared
from rti_tol, BREP topology metadata, local surface metrics, and ray scale.
The following quantities must remain separate even if some share a numerical
lower bound.

3.1 Numerical solve tolerance

This answers: "Is the candidate sufficiently close to the mathematical ray
and surface to be a usable geometric observation?"

For the two-plane formulation use the model-space line residual:

  residual_line = hypot(f, g)

when P1 and P2 are orthonormal.  This has model-space units.  Accepting a root
also requires a suitably small model-space change in S, or a parameter change
interpreted through the local first fundamental form:

  G = [ Su.Su  Su.Sv ]
      [ Su.Sv  Sv.Sv ]

  step_model^2 = delta_uv^T G delta_uv

Numerical tolerance should be a conservative fraction of the geometric
acceptance tolerance, with a floor based on floating-point scale.  It must not
be enlarged merely to heal a seam.  Solving and seam healing are separate
operations.

3.2 UV trim-query tolerance

This answers: "Which trim segments might be relevant to this UV candidate?"
It is a broad-phase parameter-space envelope, not the final crack decision.

Use ON_BrepTrim::m_tolerance[0/1] when set.  Add a local conversion of the
model-space uncertainty through the surface Jacobian.  The natural shape is a
metric ellipse delta_uv^T G delta_uv <= T^2; an axis-aligned UV rectangle may
conservatively bound that ellipse for CurveTree lookup.  Near singular G, do
not permit the inverse metric to expand without bound.  Query a bounded UV
region and make the final decision by closest distance in 3D to the lifted
trim or edge.

3.3 Model-space seam envelope

This answers: "How far may two topologically mated representations disagree
while still representing one intended boundary?"

For each topological edge prepare an authorized envelope from:

  * rti_tol.dist as the BRL-CAD minimum model-space scale;
  * a valid ON_BrepEdge::m_tolerance;
  * valid endpoint ON_BrepVertex tolerances; and
  * the 3D lift of valid trim parameter tolerances where needed.

If a tolerance is ON_UNSET_VALUE, compute it with OpenNURBS facilities or an
adaptive edge/trim comparison during prep and mark it as inferred.  Do not
treat an explicitly set zero tolerance as missing.

Also measure the actual discrepancy g between the shared 3D edge curve and
each adjacent lifted trim/surface boundary.  The measured discrepancy is
evidence, not permission: a set tolerance authorizes closure only when g is
within it.  An inferred tolerance may be based on g because OpenNURBS
explicitly says an unset value needs computation.  Record unexpectedly large
declared or inferred envelopes for diagnostics.

Production permission must nevertheless come from a conservative bound, not
from the value used to infer the envelope.  The implemented strict policy
authorizes only when the complete edge/both-lift upper bound is below the
effective envelope.  A lower bound above it rejects repair; a bound touching
or straddling it is ambiguous.  Consequently an unset tolerance inferred as
E=g cannot self-authorize at equality.  A broader inference policy, if later
needed for imported models, must impose an independently justified maximum
closure scale rather than defining permission from the observed gap itself.

Only faces joined by the same topological edge, or the two sides of a declared
surface seam, are eligible for automatic solid closure.  Geometric proximity
alone must not join unrelated faces.  Non-manifold edges, edges with missing
adjacency, and discrepancies beyond the envelope are unresolved and use the
fallback during the staged rollout.

3.4 Ray-parameter clustering tolerance

This answers: "Could two candidate observations describe the same uncertain
physical event along this ray?"

Each candidate carries a ray-parameter interval, not just one scalar t.  For
ray direction D and model-space uncertainty T, the base conversion is:

  delta_t = T / |D|

with additional solver/bounding uncertainty included.  Candidates form a
cluster when their intervals overlap and their topology/model-space evidence
is compatible.  Do not cluster solely with BREP_SAME_POINT_TOLERANCE and do
not assume ray directions are normalized internally.

3.5 Minimum valid solid interval

This answers: "Is a resulting material interval meaningful to BRL-CAD's
Boolean evaluator?"

rti_tol.dist is authoritative.  BREP must preserve genuinely separate roots
until solid assembly, then handle intervals consistently with rt_boolweave and
rt_boolfinal.  A tangent contact produces no transition.  An interval below
the minimum solid thickness is represented according to the same zero-
thickness/fusion rules used downstream, not discarded earlier because its
normal happened to satisfy a fixed grazing threshold.


4. PROPOSED PREPARED DATA MODEL
-------------------------------

4.1 BrepPreparedFace

For every face retain:

  * face and surface indices;
  * an owned CurveTree;
  * a coarse BBNode hierarchy;
  * a list of immutable rational Bezier spans covering the surface;
  * mapping from coarse leaves to candidate spans;
  * trim/edge adjacency identifiers;
  * face orientation; and
  * prep statistics such as nodes, leaves, spans, and measured errors.

Each Bezier span stores its surface domain, order, positive-weight status,
homogeneous control points or an immutable reference to packed prepared
control points, a conservative model-space bound, and references to trims
whose UV bounds overlap it.

For ordinary positive rational weights, the dehomogenized control points obey
the rational convex-hull property.  If weights are non-positive, cross zero,
or are otherwise unsafe for conservative rational bounds, mark the span for
the existing dense-tree fallback.  Correct fallback is preferable to a false
exclusion.  Support for projective interval bounds can be added later.

4.2 BrepPreparedEdge

For every edge retain:

  * edge and endpoint vertex indices;
  * the two trim and face indices for a manifold mated edge;
  * trim orientations and face reversal state;
  * edge parameter domains and maps to each trim;
  * declared and inferred topology tolerances;
  * measured maximum edge-to-lift discrepancy and where it occurs;
  * a conservative model-space seam envelope; and
  * flags for seam, singular, boundary, non-manifold, invalid, or excessive
    discrepancy states.

Do not infer edge/trim identity from a finite parameter sample.  The first
implemented global correspondence theorem chooses the oriented edge endpoint
chord as a shared projection axis.  Exact binary64 expansions prove that every
positive-weight rational edge Bezier span has a nondecreasing projected
control polygon with at least one strict increase.  For a boundary-
isoparametric trim, expansions also prove the fixed UV coordinate exactly,
the varying rational trim coordinate monotone, and every corresponding
surface-boundary control polygon monotone with the required orientation.
This handles zero projection derivatives at sphere poles without ignoring an
uncertain interval.

For other regular trims, outward intervals bound the rational trim derivative,
the restricted rational surface partials, and their composed projected lift
derivative.  Dyadic subdivision continues until every cell is strictly
oriented.  A depth-24 or solid-wide 4,096-cell limit, unsupported arithmetic,
or any reversal leaves correspondence unsupported.  The former four-sample
projection/tangent screen remains telemetry only.  A degree-15 fixture whose
values and derivatives are forward at all four old samples but which reverses
between 0.4 and 0.6 permanently gates this distinction and the explicit depth
fallback.

Correspondence and discrepancy are separate proofs.  Production now applies
the knot-aware chord/control-hull Hausdorff subdivision to every supported
globally corresponding seam, including exact-looking records.  Refinement is
directed by the effective envelope E: strict upper < E authorizes, strict
lower > E rejects, and equality or a surviving straddle is ambiguous.  The
straddle resolution is max(roundoff, 0.01 E), maximum depth is 24, and the
solid-wide work budget is 4,096 cells.  Unsupported arithmetic/topology and
budget exhaustion are unavailable and retain whole-ray fallback.  The former
33-point authorization is exposed only as diagnostic telemetry.  Both trims
must prove inside before edge boxes, sectors, closure, or continuation acquire
authority.  All such work is prep-time, not ray-time.

4.3 BrepHitCandidate

Replace the information-poor brep_hit during solving with an internal record
containing at least:

  * face, surface, span, leaf, trim, edge, and adjacent-face indices;
  * UV point and a UV uncertainty region;
  * model-space point and normal when well-defined;
  * scalar t and [t_min,t_max] uncertainty interval;
  * line residual, last model-space step, and a conditioning measure;
  * signed model-space distance to the relevant trim/edge when available;
  * face orientation and provisional enter/leave/contact classification;
  * inside-trim, outside-trim-near-edge, clean-interior, singular, and
    out-of-domain flags; and
  * solver outcome and refinement provenance.

Do not throw away an outside-near-trim candidate.  It is essential evidence
when the neighboring face misses the ray because of a crack.

4.4 Structured solver outcomes

Use a result enum rather than a Boolean converged flag.  Required outcomes
include:

  CONVERGED_REGULAR
  CONVERGED_SINGULAR
  CONTACT_TANGENT
  OUTSIDE_TRIM
  OUTSIDE_DOMAIN
  NO_ROOT_BOUND_PROVEN
  JACOBIAN_SINGULAR
  STALLED
  DIVERGED
  NONFINITE_EVALUATION
  UNSUPPORTED_WEIGHTS
  WORKSPACE_EXHAUSTED
  FALLBACK_USED

Keep the best residual and candidate box even on failure so the local
refinement and diagnostics have useful evidence.

4.5 Per-ray completion ledger

The prepared path needs a fixed-capacity `BrepRayLedger` (the exact name is
not important) whose purpose is to prove completeness, not merely collect
hits.  It records the immutable object/prep mode used for the shot; every
coarse face/span candidate; the disposition of every retained subdivision
box; every certified mathematical root; every physical event; the final
material intervals; workspace high-water marks; and one structured fallback
reason.

Every potentially intersected span or descendant box must end in exactly one
of these states:

  * `EXCLUDED`: a conservative model-space/Bernstein bound proves no root;

  * `RESOLVED_REGULAR`: subdivision plus a strict certificate proves exactly
    one ordinary root and supplies its bounded orientation;

  * `RESOLVED_BOUNDARY`: adjacent span/face evidence proves the complete root
    set on a shared parameter or topological boundary;

  * `RESOLVED_CONTACT`: a complete proof identifies a zero-material tangent
    or a subminimum event without manufacturing a solid interval; or

  * `UNRESOLVED`: arithmetic, topology, unsupported geometry, ambiguity, or a
    fixed-work limit prevents one of the conclusions above.

`UNRESOLVED` is not a miss.  It makes the complete prepared ray ineligible and
selects the fallback before any segment is emitted.  A converged Newton point,
a small box, an even candidate count, or agreement with the legacy result is
never by itself a completion state.  The ledger must also detect missing and
duplicate accounting: the number of excluded plus candidate spans must equal
the number prepared for every fully supported face, and every terminal box
must have exactly one disposition and a compatible root/event owner.

Keep root evidence immutable as it moves through the pipeline.  Event
resolution may reference or coalesce root records, but it must not erase the
original face/span, UV box, t interval, trim class, certificate type, or
orientation proof.  This is necessary both for seam decisions and for
diagnosing whether a future failure arose in broad-phase coverage, root
isolation, trim classification, event identity, or solid assembly.


5. PROPOSED INTERSECTION ALGORITHM
----------------------------------

5.0 Production authority and stage contract

The intended production solver is a proof-producing, evidence-preserving
pipeline.  Its stages and authority are:

  1. Object dispatch and prep eligibility.  Select solid, plate, invalid, and
     unsupported contracts before solving.  A direct solid path requires a
     closed orientable BREP, complete immutable face/span records, supported
     positive rational weights and orders, and the topology records needed by
     every candidate face.  Otherwise use the appropriate fallback contract.

  2. Conservative broad phase and coverage accounting.  Traverse a coarse
     face hierarchy without allocation.  Every potentially intersected face
     reaches its prepared spans, and every span is either conservatively
     excluded or entered into the local isolation worklist.  Bounding boxes
     may add work but may never remove an unproven span.

  3. Local mathematical root isolation.  Build outward ray-plane Bernstein
     coefficients, apply contraction-only clipping and bounded subdivision,
     and use safeguarded correctors only to propose representatives.  A
     regular Krawczyk inclusion, a complete fold/boundary theorem, or another
     independently audited certificate supplies root authority.  Every
     sibling region must be excluded or resolved, so the result is a complete
     set rather than a collection of roots that happened to converge.

  4. Geometric and trim classification.  Evaluate certified roots against
     face trims, lifted 3D edges, seam envelopes, surface singularities, and
     prepared adjacency without discarding near-boundary evidence.  Produce
     clean-interior, inside-near-edge, outside-near-edge, outside-trim, or
     singular-boundary records with bounded UV/model/t uncertainty.

  5. Physical event resolution.  Establish identity from certified root
     intervals plus topology, never from t proximity alone.  Interior roots
     use the oriented surface determinant; shared surface spans use the
     boundary/fold proof; manifold edges use their incident material sector;
     authorized cracks use the same edge and bounded discrepancy evidence;
     and supported vertices use a cyclic fan.  Emit one `ENTER`, `LEAVE`, or
     `CONTACT` event, or mark the cluster unresolved.

  6. Ordered solid-state assembly.  Starting outside at negative infinity,
     process the complete event stream by outward t intervals.  A valid
     oriented manifold boundary changes winding only through resolved ENTER
     and LEAVE events; CONTACT has zero change.  Form union material intervals
     where the signed winding is positive and apply the model minimum-size
     policy with outward interval comparisons.  Negative winding, ambiguous
     event identity/order, or a nonzero final winding selects fallback.  A
     value above one is valid only when separately certified events establish
     genuinely overlapping outward shells; duplicate seam observations must
     resolve to one physical event before assembly.

  7. Atomic publication.  Only after the ledger proves complete span/box/root
     coverage, complete event resolution, valid solid state, finite endpoint
     attributes, and sufficient output capacity may it allocate librt
     segments and publish the prepared answer.  Until that point the legacy
     hit list and `seghead` remain untouched.  Any failure reruns the complete
     fallback ray; never splice an uncertified legacy hit into an otherwise
     prepared partition or publish a partial prepared result.

The current strict ordinary-root and clean-interior fold selectors are the
first production implementation of this contract.  Every terminal box stores
an explicit unresolved, resolved-regular, or resolved-boundary disposition.
An outward Bernstein determinant signs each Krawczyk-certified ordinary box.
A separate fixed-capacity event array preserves source box/root indices, UV,
ray-t interval, trim state, certificate type, determinant orientation, and
ENTER/LEAVE direction.  The same array accepts the already-certified
cross-span fold theorem.  It sorts events, validates {0,1} state, and applies
the outward minimum-segment classifier before publication.

Prepared publication now requires this ledger to be complete and emits the
already-authorized clean events directly; it no longer routes selected roots
through the legacy ordered cleanup passes.  Clean exterior roots produce no
event, and a completely excluded ray produces a certified empty partition.
Near-trim roots, unqualified contacts, unsupported topology, uncertain
orientation/order, and every capacity failure still select the whole-ray
legacy path before output mutation.  Add edge, crack, contact, and vertex
event theorems one independently qualified class at a time.  The dense
SurfaceTree remains available during rollout, but agreement with it is
diagnostic evidence rather than a mathematical correctness proof.

5.1 Broad phase

Keep a shallow surface/face hierarchy for rejecting rays far from a patch.
Initially preserve the existing depth-eight hierarchy while the new solver is
shadow-tested.  Once correctness gates pass, lower the default depth in
measured steps.

Add an allocation-free hierarchy traversal overload.  It should use a fixed
array stack and write leaf/span references into a fixed candidate array or
invoke a callback immediately.  The current std::list-based traversal and
std::list hit collection allocate during every shot and must not be part of
the final fast path.

If a fixed traversal or candidate array fills, set WORKSPACE_EXHAUSTED and
invoke the current path.  Never silently truncate candidates.

5.2 Conservative local Bezier exclusion

For each candidate rational Bezier span, choose orthonormal P1 and P2
perpendicular to D.  Express the rational surface in homogeneous coordinates
(X,Y,Z,W).  Roots of the ray-plane functions are roots of the polynomial
Bezier numerators:

  F1(u,v) = (XYZ(u,v) - O * W(u,v)) dot P1
  F2(u,v) = (XYZ(u,v) - O * W(u,v)) dot P2

The Bernstein coefficient ranges of F1 and F2 provide conservative exclusion:
if either coefficient hull is strictly positive or strictly negative beyond
the numerical interval error, the parameter box contains no ray intersection.

For boxes that cannot be excluded:

  1. Apply Bezier clipping when coefficient geometry yields a safe domain
     contraction.

     The implemented shadow contraction reduces each bivariate coefficient
     net to conservative one-dimensional envelopes.  For a selected
     direction and each ray-plane equation, the lower control value is the
     minimum interval lower bound over the other parameter and the upper
     control value is the maximum interval upper bound.  A root can occur
     only where the lower Bernstein curve can be nonpositive and the upper
     curve can be nonnegative.  Bound those parameter sets with the convex
     hulls of the control polygons.  The fixed implementation considers all
     vertices already in the requested halfspace and every pairwise control-
     polygon zero crossing; considering non-hull chords can only enlarge the
     result.  All crossing arithmetic is outward interval arithmetic.

     Intersect the lower/upper ranges for both equations and both parameter
     directions.  An inconclusive or empty clipping calculation currently
     falls through to ordinary subdivision; it is not an exclusion proof.
     Re-restrict both complete coefficient nets to a proposed clipped box and
     reject the clip if bounded restriction fails or its hull result is
     inconsistent.  Thus the first clipping deployment is contraction-only.

     Do not let a successful clip hide extra effective subdivision.  Retain
     at least half of each parameter width per clipping operation, ignore
     contractions below 1/16 of a width, and charge one or two subdivision
     levels according to the retained parameter-box area.  Cap the charged
     depth at the existing depth-24 limit.  If neither parameter midpoint is
     representably interior, retain the current box as a terminal candidate
     instead of generating duplicate children.  These rules are correctness
     and solver-resolution guards, not performance tuning constants.

  2. Otherwise split along the parameter direction with the larger predicted
     model-space extent or poorer bound.

  3. Use de Casteljau subdivision, preserving conservative coefficient bounds.

  4. Traverse near boxes first according to ray t range, but retain all boxes
     whose bounds may contain roots.

  5. Stop subdividing early only when the safeguarded corrector supplies a
     root that is independently certified as the unique root in the box.
     Small parameter/model-space bounds and numerical convergence alone are
     not stopping proofs.

This supplies a root-isolation mechanism that does not rely on one Newton
basin.  It is especially important for two close grazing roots.

For a regular candidate, use an interval Krawczyk test as the uniqueness and
existence certificate.  On a local parameter box X and a converged point x in
X, form

  K(X) = x - C F(x) + (I - C J(X)) (X - x),

where C is a nonsingular real preconditioner (normally the inverse of the
midpoint of the interval Jacobian), F is the pair of homogeneous ray-plane
Bezier numerators, and J(X) encloses their Jacobian everywhere in X.  If the
computed interval K(X) is strictly contained in the interior of X, the box
contains exactly one root and may terminate without reaching a fixed depth.
If the test fails, says nothing, encounters a singular midpoint, touches the
box boundary, or exhausts arithmetic/workspace bounds, continue subdivision;
never infer exclusion from failed certification.

The interval implementation must be auditable.  Derivative ranges come from
the convex hulls of derivative Bernstein coefficients.  Function,
restriction, derivative, and interval-operation roundoff must all be enlarged
outward; positive rational weights remain a prerequisite.  Record corrector
attempts, convergences, successful certificates, depths, and the fixed-depth
work avoided.  Exact tangencies and roots on subdivision/patch boundaries are
expected not to certify with a strict-interior test and must retain the deep
or neighboring-box path.  This is desirable: the certificate accelerates
ordinary transverse roots while leaving close grazing roots under conservative
subdivision.

Treat the first implementation as shadow evidence until its outward-rounding
and coefficient-error assumptions have received an independent numerical
audit.  `nextafter` expansion plus deliberately conservative local error
budgets are useful defenses, but production promotion requires showing that
every generated coefficient and every function/Jacobian interval encloses the
corresponding exact polynomial quantity.  Until then, a successful shadow
test may reduce comparison work but may not alter production hit publication.

The current continuation certificate deliberately does not use clipping.
That certificate must show that its bounded outside-span region isolates only
the proposed missing seam root.  Early experiments with conservative
clipping retained extra terminal ambiguity boxes and therefore weakened that
single-component proof even though the intended root remained covered.
Revisit continuation clipping only with a separate component/exclusion gate.

Audit the layers separately.  For already bounded Bernstein coefficients,
evaluate F(x) with interval de Casteljau operations and form derivative
coefficient intervals before taking their convex hulls; do not evaluate in
scalar double and attach an a posteriori cancellation estimate.  Exercise
addition, scaling, and interval multiplication with outward `nextafter`
rounding.  An independent exact-dyadic test oracle should cover every tensor
order pair through the supported maximum, positive coefficient uncertainty,
mixed signs/cancellation, and wide binary exponent ranges.  This proves the
function/Jacobian layer only.  Separately prove initial homogeneous ray-plane
coefficient construction, arbitrary sub-box restriction, rational weight and
ray-t bounds, and any reparameterization amplification before treating the
complete Krawczyk input as rigorous.

For initial construction, regard every stored control-point component,
weight, ray-origin component, ray-direction component, and ray-plane component
as an exact binary64 input.  Form each homogeneous Cartesian numerator
`cv.xyz - ray_origin * cv.w`, both ray-plane dot products, the ray-direction
squared norm, and the ray-parameter numerator with outward interval operations
at each multiply, add, and divide.  A scalar coefficient may remain as the
working center, but its declared error must cover the complete independently
formed interval rather than a magnitude-based estimate.

For a sub-box, retain the scalar de Casteljau net as a working center and run a
parallel interval de Casteljau restriction starting from every coefficient's
full `[center-error, center+error]` interval.  Enclose the local `minimum /
maximum` parameter used by a two-split restriction.  Reduce the resulting net
to one common error only after every output coefficient interval has been
formed.  Do the same independently for the homogeneous ray-parameter
numerator and positive weight net; reject a ray-t bound if any restricted
weight interval reaches zero, otherwise bound each rational coefficient with
outward interval division and take their hull.  This replaces depth-scaled
and absolute-epsilon error heuristics with operation-level enclosures.

Extend the exact-dyadic oracle with multi-split tensor restrictions at several
sub-box locations, every supported order pair, mixed signs, declared input
uncertainty, and wide binary scales.  Also gate positive-denominator interval
quotients and the initial homogeneous coefficient equations directly.  Keep
outside-span reparameterization as a distinct audit: extrapolating basis
transforms are not convex restrictions and require their own amplification
proof.

For that proof, construct outward interval matrices for the one-dimensional
Bernstein reparameterizations in u and v, then apply their tensor product
directly to each uncertain source coefficient.  Do not propagate coefficient
intervals through two sequential split nets: intermediate controls share the
same source coefficients, so ordinary interval arithmetic loses those
dependencies and can compound a needlessly large bound with degree.  A direct
tensor matrix sum retains independent source-coefficient uncertainty while
still enclosing transformation-matrix roundoff.  Derive one common result
error only after every output coefficient interval is available, and require
every resulting rational weight interval to remain strictly positive.

The independent oracle should form the same mathematical reparameterization
over exact dyadics, but obtain uncertainty amplification from exact absolute
row sums of the u and v operators.  Cover extension on either side, intervals
wholly above one (which give a negative intermediate denominator), the
alternate minimum-equals-one split branch, large extrapolation through every
supported tensor order, representative small seam-like extensions, mixed
signs, and wide binary scales.  Gate enclosure independently from efficiency;
also retain a bound on interval width relative to exact operator uncertainty
so a formally safe but dependency-exploded implementation cannot silently
make continuation certificates useless.

Qualify adaptive termination under similarities independently of physical
classifier invariance.  Apply the same translation, arbitrary rotation, and
0.01x/10,000x uniform scales to an exact implicit sphere, its converted BREP,
and every test ray; scale all model-space tolerances as lengths.  Normalize ray
parameters by the uniform scale and compare implicit, legacy-BREP, and
prepared-BREP intervals to the analytic chord.  Require the same two regular
event classes and one material interval in both ray directions, and require
both roots to receive strict-interior certificates before depth 24.  Do not
require subdivision counts to match: ray-plane basis selection, coefficient
conditioning, and conservative bounds may change work without changing the
physical answer.  Nonuniform scaling is not a similarity and needs a separate
affine/ellipsoid qualification rather than reuse of this invariant.

For that separate gate, map the exact sphere, its interior transverse rays,
and the analytic chord through A = R diag(sx, sy, sz) plus translation.  If d
is the original unit ray direction, normalize A d for shooting and scale the
expected ray parameters by |A d|.  This retains an analytic oracle without
solving a second ellipsoid equation numerically.  Construct the implicit ELL
from the three transformed radius vectors, convert the same ELL to BREP, and
compare implicit, promoted production, forced legacy, and reconstructed
prepared partitions in both directions.  A general affine map has no single
length scale; use the smallest singular value to scale the model distance
tolerance so the thinnest principal direction remains conservative, and use
the transformed direction length to normalize endpoint errors.

Sweep both overall scale and singular-value condition number.  Moderate cases
must select the prepared path.  Higher-condition cases that reach depth or
workspace limits must preserve the production partition through an exact,
observable fallback reason.  Independently ratchet prepared isolated-box and
legacy SurfaceTree-leaf capacity fallback: an ellipsoid may exceed either one
without being allowed to truncate candidates.  Keep a focused affine-only
test mode so conditioning boundaries can be iterated without rerunning the
full Crofton corpus.  This establishes ordinary transverse behavior only;
repeat the grazing trend under affine distortion before claiming a tangent or
seam classifier invariant beyond similarities.

The affine grazing gate must map the complete line construction, not merely
scale an already normalized world ray.  For a base-sphere unit direction d,
choose a perpendicular closest-point direction n and signed clearance h, form
the base line at (R-h)n, and transform both its origin and d by A.  Normalize
A d only for librt shooting.  The exact transformed parameter endpoints are
(2R +/- sqrt(R^2-(R-h)^2)) |A d|.  Report both h in units of the original
model tolerance and the physical chord in units of the transformed model
tolerance.  Sweep positive clearances logarithmically through exact contact
and mirrored negative clearances, and shoot both directions.  Translation,
rotation, anisotropic scale, and reversal then share the same analytic source
line while exercising different numerical conditioning.

This test must distinguish three stages that can otherwise look like the same
miss:

  * root absence: neither the legacy SurfaceTree solver nor the prepared local
    corrector produces the analytic roots;

  * qualification fallback: prepared boxes or roots exist but cannot be
    certified or exceed fixed capacity, after which the complete legacy result
    must be retained; and

  * post-solve classification loss: two finite, ordered roots exist but cleanup
    deletes or merges them.

In particular, a fixed normal-dot grazing threshold cannot decide whether a
solid interval exists.  Normal angle is not invariant under a general affine
map: both endpoint normals of a long, thin ellipsoid chord can be arbitrarily
close to perpendicular to the ray.  Grazing cleanup may delete an isolated
contact, invalid trimmed candidate, or out-of-bounds root, but it must retain
an adjacent ENTERING/LEAVING pair when its parameter separation represents at
least rti_tol.dist in model space.  For a non-unit ray this threshold is
rti_tol.dist/|d|.  Permit only a scale-aware floating-point evaluation slack;
do not enlarge the modeling tolerance.  Apply the same decision to fixed,
allocating, shadow-event, and prepared-publication cleanup so allocation mode
cannot change topology.

The initial implemented matrix uses conditions 13.3, 100, and 1,000; signed
h/T values 100, 1, 0.01, 1e-4, 1e-6, 1e-8, 0, and their negative mirrors; and
both directions, for 78 rays.  It found and repaired post-solve angular
deletion on 16 directed rays.  The remaining monotone capability boundary is
not yet the desired invariant: condition 100 misses a 3.08-T chord at h/T =
1e-8, and condition 1,000 misses 305-T and 30.5-T chords at h/T = 1e-6 and
1e-8.  These six cases have no stored legacy roots and no publishable prepared
local roots, so solver/isolation—not cleanup—is now the next target.  Keep
per-condition floors as one-way ratchets: improvements may reduce the miss
count, but a hit may never disappear at a larger clearance or restart closer
to tangency.  Require reversal agreement, analytic endpoint accuracy for every
retained interval, stable misses for negative h, and explicit prepared fallback
accounting.

Do not use the implicit ELL as the sole oracle exactly at transformed
tangency.  The first matrix shows direction-dependent, sub-resolution implicit
segments on three of six exact-contact shots due to discriminant cancellation.
Use the analytic zero-material contact at h=0; continue to compare the implicit
and BREP results at representably positive and negative clearances.  This does
not weaken later Cauchy-Crofton validation because exact tangencies have measure
zero, but directed boundary tests must report the implicit artifact rather than
canonize it as expected solid material.

The first production publication boundary is deliberately stricter than root
convergence.  A prepared result may replace the SurfaceTree result only when:

  * the object is a solid and is not in plate mode;

  * every face has supported positive-weight Bezier spans and every span was
    either coefficient-excluded or completely processed;

  * no subdivision, clipping/restriction, isolated-box, local-root, trim,
    cluster, or hit workspace reported failure or overflow;

  * every retained isolated box has exactly one complete ownership theorem:
    a strict-interior Krawczyk root, one of the two qualified fold parents, or
    a boundary/contact disposition owned by the narrowly certified manifold-
    seam pair described in Section 6.4.1 or the cyclic planar vertex theorem
    in Section 6.4.2; mixing these exceptions with an otherwise incomplete ray
    is not allowed;

  * every physical event group has an unambiguous entering or leaving
    direction (clean trimmed misses may be discarded, but tangencies,
    contacts, and otherwise unresolved groups may not be published); and

  * the reconstructed fixed-workspace hits survive the established cleanup
    stages as a finite, even, alternating ENTER/LEAVE partition whose segments
    are at least the model distance tolerance long.

Any failed condition runs the existing dense SurfaceTree solver unchanged.
Keep the reasons individually observable: non-solid, plate, unsupported
surface, surface workspace/restriction, isolated-box storage, uncertified
root, local workspace/trim, root coverage, event class, hit construction, hit
workspace, and invalid final partition.  Trace mode should still execute the
legacy solver after a prepared result qualifies, compare physical events and
partitions, and then return the prepared production answer.  A private test
entry point should force the legacy path so that promoted production results
can be compared against an implementation-independent baseline rather than
against themselves.

This publication checkpoint does not by itself justify reducing SurfaceTree
density.  It may increase shot time while both structures coexist, especially
for grazing rays that do the prepared work and then conservatively fall back.
Measure ordinary selection rate, fallback distribution, and end-to-end shot
time before changing the prep tree.  Retain the dense tree until the fallback
population has another correctness-preserving route.

5.3 Safeguarded corrector

Use the current two-plane equations but replace explicit determinant inversion
with a conditioned 2x2 solve (scaled pivoting or a small QR/SVD-style solve).
The algorithm is:

  1. Evaluate position and derivatives and check the return status and all
     values for finiteness.

  2. Test line residual before forming the Jacobian.  This fixes exact roots
     at poles and other singular seeds.

  3. If the regular Jacobian is usable, compute a Newton step and apply a
     trust-region/line search that requires sufficient residual decrease.

  4. Interpret step size with the local surface metric, not raw UV magnitude.

  5. Do not clamp an arbitrary step to a leaf boundary.  Restrict it to the
     active isolated parameter box; a step that wants to leave is evidence for
     subdivision or a neighboring box.

  6. If the Jacobian is rank-deficient or the normal is singular, retain the
     converged positional candidate if appropriate and use one-sided
     surface_EvNormal/IsAtSingularity logic for a normal.  Otherwise return a
     singular outcome to subdivision.

  7. Use deterministic alternate seeds: box center, corners inset by a
     representable amount, edge midpoints, and the best point predicted by
     coefficient clipping.  Do not use randomness.

  8. Deduplicate in model space and overlapping t intervals, with topology
     awareness for distinct faces.  UV is never the sole identity test.

5.4 Tangencies and close roots

Do not classify a hit as disposable merely because |N dot D| is small.  That
quantity signals conditioning; it does not by itself determine the solid
event.

The local isolator must distinguish:

  * an even-multiplicity tangent contact, which has no inside/outside change;
  * two nearby simple roots enclosing a thin interval;
  * coincident observations of one edge/vertex crossing; and
  * a gap between intended adjacent patches.

Use the isolated t intervals and a local sign/winding test on either side of
the event.  If two root intervals remain inseparable at the model tolerance,
return one ambiguous cluster to the topology resolver rather than deleting
both based on normal signs.

The expected generic limit is a fold, not a discontinuous hit/miss switch.
After a well-conditioned local change of parameter and projected-equation
basis, the poorly conditioned component has the leading form

  psi(q) = a (q - q0)^2 - epsilon + higher-order terms.

On the material side, the two simple roots approach one another with
separation proportional to sqrt(epsilon); their normal/ray dot products and
the smallest singular value of the ray-plane Jacobian ordinarily vanish at
the same rate.  At epsilon = 0 they form a double contact with no material
interval, and on the other side there is no root.  A fixed angular cutoff
therefore deletes legitimate intervals at a representation- and
affine-transform-dependent distance from tangency.  The required behavioral
trend is instead monotone two-root, contact, no-root behavior, with the two
roots retained while their physical chord exceeds the model minimum-segment
tolerance.

The condition-100 and condition-1,000 affine ellipsoid ratchet shows that the
regular corrector needs a separate fold path.  Its first implementation should
be a bounded branch-and-prune procedure, not tolerance inflation:

Before entering that fold path, use a conditioned equation basis to remove
interval dependency that is purely an artifact of the two selected ray-plane
equations.  If F=(f0,f1) is the original Bernstein system, choose a finite 2x2
matrix M from a representative projected Jacobian and form G=M F.  A useful
choice aligns one row with the dominant derivative column and the other row
perpendicular to it.  The transform is only a conditioning hint: F=0 implies
G=0 even if M is inaccurate or singular.  Consequently, an outward
coefficient hull for either component of G that excludes zero safely excludes
the box.  Failure to construct finite derivative or transformed-coefficient
bounds must retain the box.

This operation must propagate both source coefficient errors with outward
interval multiplication and addition for every transformed coefficient.  Its
test gate must compare observed hulls to an exact-dyadic oracle over every
supported surface order, signed mixing matrices, very small and very large
binary scales, and zero matrix entries.  A transformed exclusion is permitted
only when the exact hull excludes zero as well.  The operation is
allocation-free and separately counts attempts, exclusions, retained boxes,
and inconclusive arithmetic.  It can reduce the work presented to the fold
solver, but it does not establish a root or authorize hit publication.

  1. On a terminal ambiguous parameter box, evaluate a candidate point and a
     scaled 2x2 SVD or equivalent rank-revealing factorization of the
     projected Jacobian.  Let p be the well-conditioned right-singular
     direction, q the weak direction, and rotate the two equations by the
     corresponding left-singular basis.  The factorization is a search hint;
     interval bounds remain authoritative.

  2. Contract the regular equation in p for bounded q strips using interval
     Newton/Hansen-Sengupta or Bernstein clipping.  Split an axis-aligned UV
     box along the coordinate with the largest component of q rather than
     representing an unbounded rotated parameter polygon.  Discard a child
     only when outward-rounded Bernstein or interval bounds prove that one of
     the two equations excludes zero.

  3. Along the retained regular-equation corridor, evaluate the weak scalar
     equation on ordered q strips.  Scalar samples and the estimated fold
     point may schedule work, but may not prove root absence.  Search both
     sides of the estimated fold separately so one Newton basin cannot consume
     or hide the neighboring root.

  4. Correct each candidate inside its own bounded box.  A strict-interior
     Krawczyk inclusion continues to certify each regular root away from exact
     contact.  Root boxes must be disjoint in the weak direction or remain an
     explicitly ambiguous cluster.

  5. Account for the complete parent box: every portion must be either
     coefficient-excluded, covered by a certified root box, or retained as
     unresolved.  Two converged candidates alone do not prove that the pair is
     complete.  Exact contact, an uncertifiable singular box, interval
     dependency, or workspace exhaustion uses the conservative fallback.

  6. Once two roots are certified, compare their ray-parameter interval gap
     to rti_tol.dist/|D| with floating-point enclosure.  Publish an
     ENTER/LEAVE interval only if its physical length is tolerance-resolved;
     otherwise hand one ambiguous contact cluster to solid-event resolution.

  7. Use fixed arrays for the strip/box stack, candidate brackets, and SVD
     scratch.  Record high-water marks and an explicit fold-workspace fallback;
     do not allocate during shot.

This path deliberately separates three questions: numerical convergence,
existence/uniqueness certification, and solid classification.  Deflation or
regularized Newton methods for corank-one singular polynomial roots are useful
references for exact-contact diagnostics, but they do not by themselves prove
that all nearby simple roots were found.  The branch-and-prune coverage proof
is still required.

The first bounded fold qualification should be treated as measured design
input, not as authority to weaken these gates.  Conditioned hulls localized
the known condition-100 and condition-1,000 misses to two boxes on opposite
sides of a Bezier-span boundary.  Solving the dominant equation at nine fixed
weak-parameter samples and bisecting a weak-equation sign change recovered all
twelve expected candidates.  Candidate recovery is therefore not the hard
part.

The hard limit is interval precision near the fold.  Candidate-centered
binary64 Krawczyk tests should retain a separate interval for each Bernstein
coefficient; collapsing all coefficients to the largest patch error discards
enough information to prevent certification.  Fused centered-coordinate and
dot-product construction can avoid cancellation error proportional to the
world-coordinate magnitude.  If error-free transforms or an extended
precision tier are used, every operation must have a portable outward-
rounding contract and an independent exact oracle.  Platform `long double`
alone is not such a contract: its mantissa width varies, and a test result on
one ABI does not establish portable certification.

A better completeness proof than tiling the complement is available for a
generic one-sided fold.  In a conditioned equation basis let phi be the
regular equation and psi the weak equation.  On a parent box, prove:

  * the Bernstein hull of d(phi)/d(p) excludes zero;
  * phi has strict opposite signs on its two p boundaries, so exactly one
    continuous regular-equation corridor crosses every retained q; and
  * the Bernstein polynomial for
    det(d(phi,psi)/d(p,q)) is one-signed on that corridor domain.

Then psi restricted to the corridor is monotone because its derivative has
the sign of det(J) divided by d(phi)/d(p).  An existence certificate for one
bracketed root consequently proves that it is the only root in the parent.  A
parent
touching the zero-determinant fold boundary may first remove a thin box whose
root absence is interval-excluded, then apply the same proof to the remaining
root-containing box.  The two equation products in det(J) should be formed in
Bernstein form before subtraction; multiplying independent derivative hulls
is usually too pessimistic.  Exact tangent and any determinant coefficient
whose sign remains uncertain still fall back.

The qualification prototype showed why this structure matters.  Per-control
binary64 intervals certified both condition-100 candidates and part of the
condition-1,000 h/T=1e-6 set; a provisional wider-mantissa interval path
certified the rest of that set but not the condition-1,000 h/T=1e-8 pair.
One-sided monotonicity proved only one of the two span parents in each hard
shot.  Generic complement subdivision exhausted even a 1,024-box diagnostic
budget per parent.  These incomplete paths were reverted.  Future work should
ratchet each failed candidate, certificate, and threshold case in a focused
C/C++ corpus and promote no root until both span parents, physical-event
deduplication, and the complete final interval are qualified.

The first permanent layer of that focused corpus should isolate candidate
search from certification.  Use the analytic tensor-product fold

  phi(u,v) = u - 0.37,
  psi(u,v) = (v - 0.5)^2 - epsilon,

whose positive-epsilon roots have separation 2*sqrt(epsilon), whose zero case
is an exact double contact, and whose negative-epsilon case is a miss.  Sweep
dyadic separations down toward binary precision so the expected coefficients
and roots can be represented without an unrelated decimal-oracle ambiguity.
Apply u/v reversal and exchange, and apply signed equation permutations plus
wide independent power-of-two equation scaling.  Gate root count, one-to-one
location, normalized residual, ordered weak brackets, fixed sample/work
bounds, candidate capacity, and deterministic repetition.  Run this test
without constructing a `.g` file so every difficult arithmetic case can be
added directly to a small C/C++ matrix.

The bounded candidate algorithm may choose a conditioned derivative frame,
solve the regular equation on a fixed set of weak-coordinate samples, and
bisect scalar weak-equation sign changes into separate one-sided candidates.
It must use caller-provided fixed arrays and report exhaustion.  This is
search evidence only.  It neither proves absence between samples nor proves
that a candidate is a unique root, and it must not publish a hit until the
coverage and certification rules above account for the complete parent box.
The initial fixed matrix uses 17 weak samples, no more than 56 regular
bisections per solve, no more than 64 weak bisections per bracket, and four
candidate slots.  These constants are measurable workspace limits, not
geometric assumptions.

Keep equation-conditioning claims precise.  The derivative-aligned
orthogonal frame is naturally covariant under signed equation permutations
and independent power-of-two scales.  It is not, by itself, a proof of
candidate-search invariance under an arbitrary shear: a large multiple of the
weak equation may remain in the nominal regular equation even when the weak
derivative vanishes at the fold.  Non-dyadic equation mixing may also perturb
an exact double root at coefficient-roundoff scale.  Add those cases only
with propagated coefficient-construction errors and a stronger
rank-revealing or coefficient-aware basis.  The conservative transformed-hull
exclusion remains valid because it uses outward bounds and gives a transform
no positive root-publication authority.

The next permanent arithmetic layer should form the corridor determinant in
Bernstein form.  If A has bidegree (m,n) and B has bidegree (p,q), form each
product coefficient as

  C[k,l] = sum A[i,j] B[k-i,l-j]
                 choose(m,i) choose(p,k-i) / choose(m+p,k)
                 choose(n,j) choose(q,l-j) / choose(n+q,l).

Do not multiply only the global derivative hulls; that loses the coefficient
correlation and is substantially wider.  Treat every binomial ratio as an
outward interval even though its integer numerator and denominator are exact.
For input surface orders up to 16x16, the two derivative products and their
difference have order at most 30x30.  Storage and loop limits must reflect
that maximum explicitly, reject overflow/nonfinite arithmetic, and remain
fixed-size.

Audit this operator independently.  An efficient exact oracle can accumulate
the integer numerator of each rational Bernstein product coefficient with
exact binomial weights and apply a dyadic scale only after the sum.  Cover all
input order pairs, signed coefficient patterns, and wide binary scales.  Also
use exact integer intervals on a lower-order but nontrivial matrix to verify
that coefficient-varying source uncertainty is propagated through derivative,
product, and determinant bounds.  A strict observed determinant sign may be
used only when the exact enclosure has the same property.

For root existence, do not confuse a fixed determinant-angle threshold with
the Krawczyk theorem.  The inverse midpoint Jacobian is a point
preconditioner; any finite approximate matrix C is admissible when every
operation in

  K(x,X) = x - C F(x) + (I - C J(X))(X - x)

is outward enclosed.  Poor conditioning will often make K fail strict
containment, which correctly returns inconclusive, but it need not reject a
strict containment merely because a normalized determinant is below 1e-6.
Keep the existing production threshold until the per-coefficient path and its
fallbacks are qualified; exercise the threshold-free rule first through a
private test entry.

The synthetic certification matrix should add parameter shear to the normal
fold form so the two Jacobian columns become nearly parallel without changing
the known roots.  Vary shear and root separation independently.  Narrow each
candidate box enough that its coefficient magnitude and evaluation enclosure
remain commensurate with the local residual; box aspect ratio is part of the
proof strategy, not merely a speed heuristic.  For each side require:

  * strict Krawczyk inclusion in a candidate-centered sub-box;
  * a larger containing corridor where d(phi)/d(p) excludes zero;
  * strict opposite phi signs on the two p boundaries for every q; and
  * one strict Bernstein sign for det(J) throughout the corridor.

The last three facts make the phi=0 corridor single-valued and make psi
strictly monotone along it.  Together with Krawczyk existence they prove
exactly one root in the corridor.  Sweep down well below the old fixed
determinant ratio and retain exact contact as an expected inconclusive case.
This analytic proof matrix is still not production qualification: the real
ray-plane path must preserve individual coefficient intervals through fused
construction and restriction, exclude the remaining strip next to the fold,
prove both adjacent Bezier-span parents, and deduplicate their physical event.

The first real-data qualification must keep that path observable and
non-authoritative.  Preserve every initial ray-plane coefficient interval
through span reparameterization and direct restriction to a candidate box;
do not reconstruct a common patch error after restriction.  Form
`CV-origin*weight` and each ray-plane dot product with fused operations where
available, enclosing the correctly rounded fused result outward by a
representable step.  Audit coefficient-varying restriction independently at
every supported tensor order, several sub-boxes, and wide dyadic scales.  A
nominal candidate corrector may center or size proof boxes, but it must not
shrink the interval coefficients or provide publication authority.

Instrument each localized terminal box with candidate count, restriction
failure, strict Krawczyk attempts/certificates, determinant ratio, and the
normalized amount by which the best Krawczyk image misses strict containment.
This distinguishes search failure from enclosure failure.  If bounded
candidate search recovers both expected roots but the image remains
materially outside the box, do not add samples, increase iterations, or
weaken a determinant/tolerance cutoff.  Introduce a portable higher-precision
enclosure tier instead, preferably error-free `TwoSum`/FMA-residual products
and fixed-size expansions with a rigorously outward conversion back to
binary64.  Apply it only to the localized ambiguous boxes, keep it
allocation-free, and exact-audit fused coefficient construction plus every
restriction stage.  Platform `long double`, compiler excess precision, or a
comparison only against the ordinary binary64 path is not a sufficient
oracle.

The implemented portable tier uses a fixed 128-component binary64 expansion.
`TwoSum` supplies an error-free addition and `std::fma(a,b,-a*b)` supplies the
product residual.  The implementation must remain conservative when a
residual might underflow below the binary64 subnormal range, when a value is
nonfinite, or when the fixed expansion fills: those cases are inconclusive
and retain the fallback.  It must not silently drop a nonzero component.
Conversion back to an interval takes an outward bound around the dominant
component and all remaining magnitudes, and the result is intersected with
the independently outward ordinary interval.  Thus the higher-precision tier
is never used by itself as evidence merely because it is narrower.

Construct an expansion for every represented ray-plane coefficient from the
homogeneous operation `CV-origin*weight` and the two projected dot products.
Be explicit about the scope of exactness: this encloses the exact value of the
stored binary64 inputs.  It does not remove rounding already introduced while
constructing the ray-plane frame.  If later cases show that frame error is the
limiting term, build and propagate an expansion-valued frame or an independent
frame-construction error bound rather than assigning the coefficient tier
authority it does not possess.

Restrict a coefficient net with the Bezier blossom rather than the ordinary
two-split `a/b` formulation.  For an output coefficient at index i, evaluate
the blossom with degree-i copies of the lower endpoint and i copies of the
upper endpoint, in each tensor direction.  Normalize each scalar equation by
an exact power of two before the blossom.  Independent nonzero scaling of the
two equations preserves the root set, avoids many underflow cases, and needs
no division by a non-dyadic subdomain width.  Preserve the full expansion
through the bounded blossom; only compact if an exact audit and real profile
show that the reduced stack/work cost is worthwhile.

Use the same expansion contract for candidate-point evaluation, derivative
hulls, interval endpoint products, and the Krawczyk image.  Every such result
must also intersect its ordinary interval counterpart.  Exact-oracle gates
cover every supported tensor order and wide binary scales.  The current
checkpoint passes 2,250 expansion function checks, 324,000 derivative checks,
45 interval products, 108 ray-plane coefficient equations, and 546,750 local
restrictions.  All 45 products and 108 coefficients contract the ordinary
enclosure; 544,500 restrictions contract.  Arithmetic-gate high-water is four
components, while the real fold probe reaches 17 of 128.  Add explicit
negative gates as well: a product with a residual below the binary64
subnormal range and a mixed-scale normalization that cannot exactly round-
trip a nonzero endpoint must both reject the expansion tier and retain the
ordinary fallback.

This tier is a measured partial success, not yet a production solver.  It
recertifies all 36 analytic fold roots.  A bounded contraction loop now
intersects each unsuccessful Krawczyk image with its current root box,
outward-maps the result into original span coordinates, restricts directly
from the original expansion coefficient net, and retries.  It uses at most
four fixed-array attempts and records applied, empty/degenerate, unchanged,
and arithmetic-failure outcomes.  This raises the real affine expansion
certificate counts from 2, 2, 1, 1, 0, 0 to 2, 2, 2, 2, 0, 0: both roots now
certify in each directed condition-100 h/T=1e-8 and condition-1,000 h/T=1e-6
shot.  The condition-1,000 h/T=1e-8 pair remains uncertified, although its
normalized Krawczyk image excess remains improved from roughly 41 to 11--13.
Doubling symmetric proof depth and adding a second weak-direction anisotropic
mode did not add a certificate.  Keep those negative results in the
continuation record so work does not return to undirected depth or aspect-
ratio sweeps.

Represented exact-contact equations can acquire strict expansion certificates
because preceding affine/frame arithmetic may have split the mathematical
double root.  Such a certificate is numerical existence evidence only.  It
cannot authorize a physical material interval until the minimum-size/contact
classifier, both parent proofs, fold-boundary strips, and cross-span event
identity agree.  Keep expansion telemetry shadow-only until those gates are
complete.  Before production use, also profile the roughly 100-KiB transient
maximum-order blossom stack and place it in an existing bounded shot workspace
if needed.

The implemented one-sided parent theorem separates regular-graph existence
from determinant behavior away from that graph.  In a conditioned equation
frame, designate phi as the regular equation, p as the regular parameter, and
q as the weak parameter.  Require an interval hull for phi_p that excludes
zero with one strict sign, and require strict opposed phi signs on the two p
boundaries for the entire q range.  Those facts prove that phi=0 is one
continuous graph p(q) crossing the parent.  The conditioning matrix and the
choice of p may be selected from nominal candidate/Jacobian information, but
they are hints only: the two boundary tests and derivative test use the
outward Bernstein coefficient intervals after transformation.

Do not require det(J) to have one sign throughout the full two-dimensional
parent if that test is inconclusive.  Along phi=0,

  d psi(p(q), q) / dq = det(J) / phi_p

up to the fixed parameter-orientation convention.  It is therefore enough to
prove one common strict determinant sign on the implicit graph.  Cover q with
a bounded depth-first set of dyadic slabs.  On each slab, restrict the
original equation coefficients and apply a fixed number of interval-Newton
steps in p using phi at p=1/2 and the interval derivative hull.  If phi is
excluded, that slab needs no determinant test.  Otherwise test the determinant
on the resulting graph enclosure.  Every covered slab must obtain the same
strict sign; restriction/arithmetic failure, sign conflict, depth exhaustion,
or workspace exhaustion is inconclusive and uses the existing fallback.

Preserve determinant value scale during this operation.  Form the Bernstein
determinant polynomial on the q slab before contracting p.  Track every local
p contraction back to a rigorously outward interval in the original slab
coordinates, then restrict the already-formed determinant polynomial to that
interval.  Do not recompute derivatives and det(J) after reparameterizing the
equations to the thin p enclosure: that multiplies the p derivative column by
the enclosure width and can destroy usable sign scale precisely when the
contraction succeeds.  Fixed-size determinant restriction supports orders
through 30x30 and has an independent exact-dyadic enclosure audit across
every supported even order in both axes, three quarter-grid intervals, wide
binary scales, and the maximum-order corner.

Prove the remaining fold-boundary strip independently.  Given the regular
phi graph on the full one-sided parent, traverse bounded q slabs, contract p
around phi=0 as above, and require the Bernstein hull of phi or psi to exclude
zero on every contracted enclosure.  This is a coverage proof that the strip
contains no additional system root, not another candidate sampler.  The
current fixed limits are a 16-entry depth-first stack, 511 visited boxes,
depth eight, and four p contractions per slab.  They are engineering work
bounds, not correctness assumptions; exhaustion is observable and falls
back.  Exact contact must remain inconclusive rather than being interpreted
as a root-free material strip.

Do not require two-dimensional Krawczyk inclusion as the only existence
theorem once the regular graph is proved.  The regular derivative and opposed
phi boundary signs establish a unique continuous phi=0 point p(q) at every q.
At each weak boundary q=0 and q=1, contract p around that point with bounded
one-dimensional interval Newton, restricting both expansion-valued curves
after every contraction.  If the Bernstein enclosure of psi on the first
contracted endpoint graph has one strict sign and the enclosure on the second
has the opposite sign, continuity supplies a system root between them.  The
already-proved common sign of det(J) on the graph makes psi strictly monotone
there and therefore makes that root unique.  Evaluate an exact singleton
contraction directly.  A zero endpoint, uncertain sign, arithmetic failure,
or unsupported bound is inconclusive; exact contact cannot become a crossing
merely because it reaches an endpoint.

For each real localized candidate, construct candidate-centered one-sided
parents using the dyadic fractions 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4, and
7/8 between the candidate and the nearest weak boundary.  Always restrict
from the original expansion-valued ray-plane net.  A complete shadow parent
requires all three independently meaningful facts: existence from either a
strict Krawczyk inclusion in a nested root box or opposed endpoint signs on
the regular graph, uniqueness from the regular graph plus determinant sign,
and exclusion of the adjacent fold strip.  This decomposition localizes a
failure instead of encouraging more global depth or weaker tolerances.

The permanent synthetic qualification deliberately makes the whole-parent
determinant unsigned with a coupled term while leaving its graph sign known.
It spans six root separations, two sides, parameter interchange, and equation
scales 2^-100, 1, and 2^100.  All 72 graph determinant cases must certify, all
72 root-free strips must exclude, and exact contact must hit the explicit
depth-inconclusive path.  The same 145 cases independently exercise endpoint
existence: all 72 root-bearing corridors certify, all 72 root-free strips and
the exact-contact case remain non-certifying, and every case reports the
endpoint theorem available.  The corresponding real affine ratchet always
runs all six directed hard shots even after a production improvement removes
a miss.  Both corridors, both fold strips, and both endpoint-existence proofs
now complete in every shot, for real complete-parent counts 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2.
Expansion Krawczyk remains a separately useful floor at 2, 2, 2, 2, 0, 0.
The next solver step at that checkpoint was cross-span physical-event identity
and the minimum-segment/contact policy, not more parent subdivision or an
immediate plane-frame precision rewrite.  That step is now implemented for the
strict same-face, exactly-one-shared-span-boundary case described below; this
historical conclusion remains important because it explains why additional
precision/depth experiments are not the current optimization target.

Do not treat an exact-perpendicular frame as a drop-in change.  A tested
largest-component algebraic basis used a common exact power-of-two direction
scale and normals (-d[a],d[k],0) and (-d[b],0,d[k]) in cyclic coordinates.
It removed the represented exact-contact certificates and modestly improved
one hardest Krawczyk excess, but lost one of two condition-100 certificates in
both directions and changed ordinary selection counts.  The permanent ratchet
correctly rejected it.  Retain this construction as a bounded shadow frame
candidate, but choose frame and candidate box together, require all existing
existence floors to remain monotone, and do not confuse exact orthogonality
with good interval conditioning.

No real fold candidate may become a production hit until both adjacent span
parents have a complete one-sided corridor/fold-strip proof, coincident
observations are deduplicated as one physical event, and the resulting
ENTER/LEAVE interval passes the model minimum-size rule.  Exact contact,
uncertain determinant sign, capacity exhaustion, unsupported weights/order,
or an inconclusive precision tier continues to the conservative dense-tree
fallback.

The first production fold gate now satisfies that rule without broadening it.
Each complete parent stores a fixed-capacity root record and is localized by
24 dyadically shrinking weak-parameter slabs.  Every accepted slab re-proves
the unique regular graph and endpoint existence, contracts the regular
parameter, maps the result outward to the original span, and obtains a
rigorous rational ray-t interval.  No localization step allocates.

Two observations are eligible to pair only when they belong to one face and
two distinct tensor-product Bezier spans sharing exactly one parameter
boundary, with overlapping transverse domains.  The topology graph must make
them mutual degree-one neighbors; corner, many-to-many, and cross-face cases
remain unsupported.  The certified determinant sign supplies orientation:
with the selected ray-plane frame its sign is `(Su x Sv) dot D`, adjusted for
face reversal.  Same-direction overlapping intervals are duplicate span
observations; opposite directions in ray order distinguish ENTER/LEAVE
material from LEAVE/ENTER void.  This avoids using a grazing normal-angle
threshold to decide event identity.

The minimum-size classifier uses only outward intervals.  It computes the
ray-parameter interval for `tol->dist / |D|` and subtracts the localized root
intervals.  A strict lower gap above the minimum upper bound is resolved; a
strict upper gap below the minimum lower bound is a subminimum contact; every
straddling case is ambiguous.  A private 36-case matrix covers those three
classes plus overlapping exact contact under 0.01x/10,000x model scaling and
0.25x/16x ray-direction scaling.  Invalid intervals and invalid directions
reject.  Equality is deliberately ambiguous rather than resolved.

Production remains narrower still: exactly two non-Krawczyk terminal boxes,
two complete parents, one mutually unique resolved material pair, zero proof,
workspace, trim, direction, topology, or classification failure, and two
clean interior trim classifications are required.  Every ordinary local root
must match one certified root interval or be the known ill-conditioned
single observation collapsed between the pair on one of the same two spans.
Any unrelated root or near-trim result falls back.  The pair is published
directly so legacy angle-based grazing cleanup cannot erase the certified
close ENTER/LEAVE interval.  This initial gate restores all six former
condition-100/1,000 tolerance-resolved affine chords, leaves exact contact and
negative clearance as misses, and makes no tree-density change.  General
near-trim/seam authorization and corner or multi-span event topology remain
separate later work.

5.5 Allocation-free ray-time workspace

The new fast path must not call malloc/new or grow STL containers while
shooting.  Initial compile-time bounds should be deliberately finite and
instrumented, for example:

  * supported direct-path surface order up to 16;
  * 64 pending subdivision boxes;
  * 64 raw root candidates; and
  * scratch for two or three maximum-order homogeneous control nets.

These are starting engineering limits, not correctness assumptions.  A span
above the supported order or a full array immediately uses the fallback.
Measure high-water marks on analytic tests and the STEP corpus before tuning
the capacities.

To avoid storing a full control net for every pending box, keep only parameter
subdomains and traversal state on the pending stack.  Use a bounded set of
scratch control nets to extract/subdivide the active subpatch from its
immutable prepared span.  Depth-first traversal bounds scratch memory.  The
extra computation occurs only in ambiguous regions and is preferable to a
large per-ray or per-leaf allocation.

Use BRL-CAD's normal resource-based segment allocation only when emitting the
final segments.  Add a debug/benchmark mode that counts all BREP-path dynamic
allocations after prep so the no-allocation requirement is testable.


6. TRIM, SEAM, AND SOLID-EVENT RESOLUTION
-----------------------------------------

The resolver consumes the immutable certified-root records and produces a new
event array; it does not destructively edit the root array.  Each event stores
its source-root indices, topological identity, outward t interval, net state
change, representative endpoint attributes, classification theorem, and any
authorized seam envelope.  A cluster that has multiple plausible identities
or state changes is unresolved even if deleting one observation would make
the final hit count even.

6.1 Candidate classification

After solving the untrimmed surface, classify the candidate without losing
information:

  * CLEAN_INTERIOR: inside the face and outside every seam envelope.

  * INSIDE_NEAR_EDGE: accepted by the trim but within the local edge envelope.

  * OUTSIDE_NEAR_EDGE: rejected by the trim but close enough in 3D and UV to a
    specific eligible edge.

  * OUTSIDE_TRIM: rejected and not close enough to an eligible edge.

  * SINGULAR_BOUNDARY: a root on a declared surface singularity or degenerate
    trim side.

The final near-edge test uses distance to the lifted trim/shared 3D edge, not
only closesttrim in UV.

6.2 Event clustering

Sort candidates by t_min, then form clusters whose t intervals overlap.
Compatibility requires either the same face/root, the same topological edge,
or an explicitly declared same-surface seam.  Geometrically close candidates
from unrelated topology remain separate.

Within each cluster:

  * discard only candidates proven outside all relevant trims and envelopes;

  * combine duplicate observations of the same face root;

  * retain both sides of a shared-edge observation until local solid state is
    known; and

  * carry the widest justified uncertainty into the resolved event.

6.3 Manifold edge transition

For a normal manifold edge with two incident faces, build a local cross-section
perpendicular to the edge tangent.  The oriented incident face normals and
trim directions define the interior sector.  Evaluate the ray direction just
before and after the clustered event in this oriented 2D cross-section:

  * outside -> inside produces exactly one ENTER event;
  * inside -> outside produces exactly one LEAVE event;
  * unchanged state is a tangent/contact and produces no transition.

This symbolic local perturbation handles a ray exactly on the edge without
 depending on which face Newton happened to find.  It also works for convex
and concave manifold edges.  Vertex clusters use the cyclic fan of incident
faces in the same spirit; unsupported/non-manifold fans fall back initially.

6.3.1 Exact certified interior-edge theorem

The first production realization of Section 6.3 resolves exact crossings of
supported interior two-trim manifold edges.  It is deliberately an exact-line
theorem, not a general permission to merge anything within model tolerance.
The mathematical ray line must first contain a point on the topological edge
within a coordinate-scaled binary64 roundoff envelope.  Only after that
independent witness exists may the authorized edge envelope associate the
redundant incident-surface roots.  This ordering is essential: model tolerance
may explain representational disagreement at a known boundary, but must not
move a ray onto a boundary it did not intersect.

Evaluate every prepared edge in a fresh fixed-capacity observation record and
require all of the following before considering a cluster:

  1. The object is a non-plate solid and all face/span, surface-box, local-root,
     trim, and workspace accounting is complete.  The topological edge has
     exactly two trims, supported positive-weight rational data, certified
     global edge/lift injectivity and orientation, and a completed conservative
     gap proof whose class is strictly `SEAM_GAP_INSIDE`.  Sampled discrepancy
     remains diagnostic and cannot authorize the theorem.

  2. Evaluate the topological edge at its closest observed parameter, project
     that point onto the ray, and require both the evaluated ray/edge distance
     and direct point distance to fit

         max(ON_ZERO_TOLERANCE,
             4096 * DBL_EPSILON * coordinate_scale).

     This is the exact line/edge witness.  Reject an edge point within the
     declared edge envelope of either endpoint; the cyclic vertex theorem or
     whole-ray fallback owns that neighborhood.

  3. Construct the two oriented incident-face frames at the certified edge
     parameter.  Project the ray into the plane normal to the edge tangent and
     classify `-D_cross` as the state immediately before the event and
     `+D_cross` as the state immediately after it.  The full oriented material
     sector handles both convex and reflex/reentrant edges.  Outside-to-inside
     is ENTER, inside-to-outside is LEAVE, and equal states are CONTACT.  A
     direction on either sector boundary, tangent to either incident face, or
     with an invalid frame remains unresolved.

  4. Keep the strict witness tolerance and the wider association tolerance
     separate.  The former is derived only from coordinate roundoff and is
     used to require that every source terminal box actually contains the
     exact topological ray parameter.  The latter is the authorized edge/lift
     envelope converted to ray parameter and may associate corrected roots
     whose representative `t` values differ because the trimmed surface
     representations and iterative solves are not identical.  A nearby root
     whose box does not enclose the exact parameter remains an independent
     ordinary root; it is not swallowed by the edge cluster.

  5. Every associated root must belong to an incident face, lie in the
     authorized spatial and ray-parameter envelope, match a lifted trim frame,
     have a finite non-tangent normal, and agree with the sector direction for
     a transition.  Every matching terminal box must be either an unresolved
     boundary candidate or an already Krawczyk-certified regular box.  A
     regular box may be reclassified as boundary evidence only after the exact
     topological proof succeeds.  Multiple roots and boxes from adjacent
     Bezier spans or from the two trims of a periodic same-surface seam are
     allowed when they independently enclose the same exact parameter and have
     compatible directions.  Duplicate use of one root or box is forbidden.

  6. Collect every disjoint exact edge cluster on the ray before mutation.
     Conflicting observations, overlapping ownership, capacity exhaustion, an
     unowned incident box covering an exact event, or an unowned local root
     invalidates the complete attempt.  CONTACT clusters own their evidence
     but emit no transition.  ENTER/LEAVE clusters append one immutable
     `MANIFOLD_EDGE`/`CRACK_HIT` event at the topological edge parameter, with
     edge identity, canonical root, representative box, total source-box
     count, direction, and the union of source t intervals.

  7. Resolve every remaining ordinary root and terminal box in the same
     temporary transaction.  Several exactly duplicate local roots on one
     face may form one ordinary event only when their face, direction,
     hit/trim class, determinant orientation, and binary64-scale t identity
     agree and every associated box has a regular Krawczyk certificate.
     Clean outside groups own their boxes without emitting an event.  Any
     other remainder invokes fallback.

Only after all clusters and ordinary evidence are owned, the event stream
passes the global {0,1} state machine, outward minimum-segment classifier, and
fixed-workspace hit reconstruction, mark transition boxes
`RESOLVED_BOUNDARY`, contact boxes `RESOLVED_CONTACT`, append the temporary
events, and select the result atomically.  A failed attempt publishes neither
dispositions nor events and runs the complete dense SurfaceTree path.

The permanent directed gate converts an exact L-shaped implicit EXTRUDE to a
BREP and checks three line families in both directions: two disjoint convex
and reentrant edge transitions; a reentrant CONTACT between two outer
transitions; and a mixed ordinary-face/edge stream.  A fourth line lies on an
incident face-sector boundary and must publish no edge event or prepared
partition; either an earlier conservative surface-work gate or the edge
theorem itself may select whole-ray fallback.  Translation, arbitrary
rotation plus translation, 0.01x similarity, and 10,000x similarity plus
large translation repeat all three families with scaled model tolerances.  A
converted rational sphere adds a periodic same-surface seam crossing with four
independently converged incident roots/boxes and an unrelated ordinary exit.
Tests reconstruct edge identity, before/after state, every source root and
box, contact ownership, reversal, event counts, final partition, and analytic
endpoints from immutable trace data rather than trusting production counters.

The exact reentrant-contact line revealed a separate implicit EXTRUDE endpoint
artifact: that primitive reports three hits and discards its last endpoint on
this measure-zero line.  The permanent contact test therefore uses the exact
analytic L-profile interval as its oracle while all transverse cases continue
to compare the implicit EXTRUDE and converted BREP directly.

This theorem does not yet authorize a ray merely near an edge, an arbitrary
gap/overlap cluster whose source boxes do not enclose one exact topological
parameter, a face-tangent sector boundary, a non-manifold edge, or an edge
endpoint/curved-vertex neighborhood.  Those remain separately specified
tolerance-near, overlap, contact, or vertex theorems.  The narrow paired
continuation theorem below also remains independent because its emitted
physical endpoints are not the intermediate topological edge parameter.

6.4 Crack closure

When one or both adjacent lifted surfaces miss the mathematical ray, an
OUTSIDE_NEAR_EDGE candidate may still establish an intended boundary event.
Create a closure transition only if:

  * the evidence refers to the same mated topological edge;

  * the candidate-to-edge and measured face/edge discrepancies are inside the
    authorized model-space envelope;

  * the local manifold sector changes solid state across the event; and

  * no clean incompatible root occupies the same interval.

The reported representative t should be the best-conditioned projection onto
the shared edge/local boundary, with an uncertainty interval covering both
adjacent representations.  Do not edit or snap the BREP itself during a shot.

An overlap uses the same resolver: several same-direction face observations
of one manifold event collapse to one oriented transition.  Opposite
transitions at one t become a contact only when the local sector test confirms
no state change.

6.4.1 Narrow paired seam-continuation theorem

The first production seam path is intentionally narrower than the eventual
general cluster resolver.  It handles the observed one-existing-endpoint,
one-missing-endpoint crack without treating an unresolved near-trim root as an
ordinary root and without editing the cleaned legacy hit array.  Keep it as an
independent event theorem even after general edge clustering exists; its proof
obligations are useful as a low-complexity fast path and as a fallback boundary
for the more general resolver.

This is a two-endpoint grazing chord, not two observations to collapse at one
edge parameter.  The certified edge ray parameter is topological/sector
evidence lying between the endpoints; it is not emitted as either boundary
event.  The existing corrected surface root and the independently certified
continuation root remain the physical ENTER/LEAVE representatives, with their
own outward intervals.

The input is the immutable prepared surface-box/local-root ledger plus a fresh
fixed-capacity evaluation of the prepared manifold edges.  Qualification is:

  1. The object is a non-plate solid; every face/span is supported; all
     candidate/excluded span accounting is complete; and surface, box, local-
     root, trim, and event workspaces report no failure or overflow.

  2. Try each nontrimmed, non-tangent CLEAN_HIT or NEAR_HIT local root as the
     existing endpoint.  The normal sign supplies its provisional direction.
     Do not destructively clean, merge, or relabel any other root while trying
     a candidate.

  3. The candidate must select exactly one supported two-trim edge.  Its ray
     distance is inside the authorized envelope; its cross-section sector is
     the material side; global edge/lift correspondence is certified; the
     adaptive geometric gap proof is strictly INSIDE; and no correspondence,
     gap, or evaluation workspace is exhausted.  Sampled gap measurements are
     diagnostic only.

  4. Map the certified edge parameter to the incident trim geometrically.
     Reflect the existing UV seed across the local trim tangent into the
     bounded Bezier-span extension and run the safeguarded corrector.  Require
     the opposite ENTER/LEAVE direction and the correct ordering about the
     edge observation.

  5. Certify the continuation over a bounded outside-span reparameterization.
     Exactly one continuation candidate and one certified candidate are
     allowed.  Every isolated certificate box must contain the corrected root;
     no certificate box may overlap the existing endpoint; the root must lie
     inside the outward ray-t hull; and its oriented normal must be finite and
     non-tangent.  Store the exact face, Bezier span, UV, adjacent face,
     direction, representative t, and certificate interval produced by this
     proof.

  6. Prove global source ownership before changing a disposition.  Every
     retained box that contains the selected existing root is a boundary
     source; the union of those boxes is its outward t interval.  Every other
     local root must be tangent, incident to the same edge, and within the
     model-space edge tolerance converted to ray parameter.  Every remaining
     box must own at least one such root and is a contact witness.  A box that
     is both or neither is a failure.  Every local root must be owned.

  7. Permit the edge-only witness case only when there is no other local root
     and every retained surface box owns the existing endpoint.  Here the
     independently evaluated topological edge is the seam witness; the absence
     of an additional mathematical surface root is precisely the crack being
     resolved.  This rule must not be generalized to a ray with an unowned box
     or unrelated root.

  8. Only after the complete proof, mark endpoint boxes RESOLVED_BOUNDARY and
     tangent-witness boxes RESOLVED_CONTACT.  Append two immutable events:
     SEAM_EXISTING, sourced from its local root and source-box set; and
     SEAM_CONTINUATION, sourced from the outside-span certificate.  Both carry
     the same edge identity.  Preserve the existing endpoint's CLEAN/NEAR
     state; label the continuation as a trimmed CRACK_HIT with near-edge
     provenance.

  9. Submit those events to the same global state machine and outward minimum-
     segment classifier used by ordinary/fold streams.  Publication requires
     one ENTER followed by one LEAVE, disjoint ordered endpoint intervals, one
     resolved material segment, a final outside state, finite strictly ordered
     representatives, and successful final hit reconstruction.  Any other
     result invokes whole-ray fallback before segment allocation.

The event record therefore needs source kind, certificate kind, edge index,
source root, representative source box plus owned-box count, face/span/UV,
adjacency, hit/trim class, direction, and outward t interval.  Continuation
records use explicit invalid source-box/root sentinels rather than pretending
the extrapolated root came from an in-domain terminal box.  Regular and fold
events explicitly have no edge identity.

The initial Cobb gate contains both forms of the theorem.  Fourteen rays have
only edge evidence and endpoint-owned boxes.  Two rays also have tangent
surface witnesses.  Six candidates with an extra non-tangent or off-corridor
root must remain fallback.  Tests must reconstruct every ownership decision
from the immutable records, check both endpoint intervals against the implicit
sphere, reverse every ray, and require no promotion above the authorized seam
envelope.  General direct contact, incident-surface overlap, mixed
ordinary/crack streams, multiple edge events, non-manifold topology, and cyclic
vertex fans remain separate theorems rather than relaxations of this one.  The
initial vertex theorem is Section 6.4.2; curved and tolerance-near fans remain
future extensions of that theorem.

6.4.1.1 Tolerance-near incident-face contact-lobe subtype

The first tolerance-near extension is a separate subtype of the paired seam-
continuation theorem, not a relaxation of its tangent-witness rule and not a
t-distance merge.  The endpoint-preserving bowed Cobb matrix contains six
inward-displacement rays for which the old narrow theorem found one valid
outer endpoint and its certified continuation, but also retained two clean,
non-tangent roots on the other incident face.  They occur at g/T = h/T = 0.5,
0.9, and 1.0 in both directions.  In increasing ray parameter those two roots
are ENTER then LEAVE, lie inside the intended outer sphere chord, and surround
the projection of the mated topological edge.  They describe a small incident-
surface lobe inside the topological material sector; they are neither tangent
witnesses nor additional solid boundaries.  Treating them as either case
without a distinct proof would be incorrect.

Keep all input and continuation obligations in Section 6.4.1.  After the
ordinary tangent-witness ownership branch rejects an extra non-tangent root,
the contact-lobe subtype may proceed only as follows:

  1. Require a non-exact edge observation.  The measured ray/edge distance
     must be strictly greater than the coordinate-scaled line-roundoff
     envelope used by the exact manifold-edge theorem.  The existing strict
     global edge/lift correspondence and `SEAM_GAP_INSIDE` discrepancy proof
     must already authorize the model corridor.  Both lifted-trim distances
     and the conservatively bounded discrepancy must remain inside that
     independently declared/certified envelope.  Thus model tolerance cannot
     turn an exact edge crossing into this case or authorize itself from the
     observed lobe.

  2. Initially restrict the ray to the edge-normal cross-section,
     `abs(dot(D_unit, edge_tangent)) <= 1e-10`.  This is a deliberately narrow
     production subset.  A future oblique theorem must bound edge-frame
     variation over the complete projected interval rather than increasing
     this angular threshold.

  3. Require exactly two roots other than the selected outer endpoint.  Both
     must belong to the other incident face and the same prepared surface
     span, classify CLEAN_HIT or NEAR_HIT on the retained side of the trim,
     have finite non-tangent normals, and sort as ENTER followed by LEAVE.
     Their representatives must be strictly inside the selected existing/
     continuation endpoint representatives.  Do not demand that binary64
     corrected representatives themselves bracket the edge projection: at
     0.01x scale a representative can move approximately 1.6e-12 past it.
     The outward terminal boxes, not nominal points, carry that proof.

  4. Map the certified edge parameter to the contact face's trim using the
     globally certified geometric edge/trim correspondence.  Do not assume
     equal normalized parameter speed.  Every contact terminal box must own
     at least one of the two roots, have the same face/span, remain an
     unresolved determinant box, contain the mapped trim UV in both
     coordinates, and contain the projected edge ray parameter in its outward
     t interval.  This box rule is representation-invariant under the existing
     rational Möbius trim reparameterization gate.

  5. Give every retained box exactly one role: selected outer-endpoint source,
     old tangent witness, or contact-lobe source.  Give every retained local
     root an owning box.  A box with two roles, no role, the wrong face/span,
     a nonzero determinant certificate, missing UV/t containment, or an
     unowned root aborts the entire subtype before mutation.  The contact box
     t hull must be nonempty and lie strictly between the two outer physical
     endpoint representatives.

  6. Prove the complete contact-box t hull remains in the oriented material
     sector at the certified edge parameter.  Project both hull endpoints
     perpendicular to the edge tangent and reject a segment whose closest
     projected point reaches the coordinate-roundoff neighborhood of the
     edge.  Each nonzero projected segment sweeps a monotone angular interval
     shorter than pi.  Compute its signed minor turn with `atan2`, unwrap the
     second endpoint relative to the first, and require the full minimum/
     maximum angular interval to remain strictly between the two oriented
     sector boundaries.  Checking endpoints alone is insufficient for a
     reflex sector because a chord with two inside endpoints can cross the
     complementary convex cone.

  7. The two contact roots emit no transition.  After the complete proof,
     mark their boxes `RESOLVED_CONTACT`; mark the selected outer source boxes
     `RESOLVED_BOUNDARY`; and publish the same two outer physical endpoints as
     Section 6.4.1 using the distinct paired certificate classes
     `SEAM_CONTACT_EXISTING` and `SEAM_CONTACT_CONTINUATION`.  Record one
     contact pair, the actual number of contact boxes, and exactly two contact
     roots.  The global state/minimum-size machine must still prove one outer
     ENTER/LEAVE interval.  Check event capacity before changing any box
     disposition, so overflow is atomic for both the old and new subtypes.

  8. Production eligibility must require a matched pair of contact-specific
     certificates and contact counters of 1/>0/2, four direction checks, no
     tangent-witness or edge-only counters, and `physical_event_near_trim`
     equal to the contact-box count.  The original seam pair must conversely
     have zero contact counters and retain its original tangent-witness or
     edge-only accounting.  A mixed or partially published pair is invalid.

The independent Cobb validator reconstructs source/root/box ownership from
the immutable ledger, independently evaluates the fixture's exact trim
reparameterization, requires the contact box hull to remain strictly within
the analytic implicit-sphere chord, and checks the final outer endpoints.
The six original cases publish six pairs, twelve contact roots, and twelve
contact boxes.  A second gate repeats one contact case in both directions
under identity, nonlinear trim reparameterization, translation, two arbitrary
rotations plus translation, 0.01x scale, and 10,000x scale with large
translation.  All fourteen cases preserve the analytic normalized chord;
terminal subdivision legitimately produces one or two contact boxes without
changing the physical result.

This contact-lobe subtype does not itself authorize endpoint-moving defects,
arbitrary numbers of overlap roots, roots on several faces or spans, an
oblique edge interval, sector-boundary tangency, a vertex neighborhood, or
non-manifold topology.  Endpoint-moving edge-corridor authorization is the
separate prerequisite in Section 6.4.1.2; it can enable the original narrow
seam theorem without weakening this two-root contact theorem.

6.4.1.2 Topological endpoint closure and representation-independent dispatch

A tolerance-displaced lifted trim endpoint need not have its nearest point on
the 3-D edge at the edge-domain endpoint.  Consequently, a geometric closest-
point map cannot be required to reproduce the trim's exact domain boundary.
That former requirement caused the endpoint-moving Cobb family to report
`SEAM_GAP_UNAVAILABLE` before visiting a bounding cell even though the global
strict-monotonicity correspondence proof succeeded.  Resolve the boundary
pair from BREP topology and reserve closest-point mapping for interior
subdivision:

  1. For edge endpoint e and each of its two trims, select trim endpoint
     `m_bRev3d ? 1-e : e`.  Its `m_vi` must exactly equal `edge.m_vi[e]`.
     Boundary association is therefore topological; distance tolerance may
     validate its geometry but may not manufacture or change its vertex.

  2. Evaluate the 3-D edge endpoint and the surface lift of that exact trim
     endpoint.  Both must lie within that specific topological vertex's point
     by `max(model distance tolerance, valid vertex.m_tolerance)` plus only a
     coordinate-scaled binary64 roundoff allowance.  Do this independently at
     both ends and on both incident trims.  A large edge tolerance or the
     opposite endpoint's vertex tolerance cannot authorize a failed endpoint.

  3. Seed the complete edge/lift discrepancy partition with the two certified
     trim-domain endpoints.  Consecutive edge Bezier spans inherit the previous
     trim endpoint exactly; only an interior span boundary is mapped by the
     geometric solver, constrained to the remaining directed trim interval.
     Adaptive child midpoints remain constrained to their parent trim
     interval.  Thus cells cover both complete loci with no endpoint gap while
     preserving nonlinear trim-reparameterization invariance.

  4. The existing positive-weight rational control-hull bounds still decide
     each cell.  Endpoint closure does not relax the gap envelope: the complete
     maximum Hausdorff upper bound must remain strictly inside the authorized
     edge corridor.  Outside, equality-straddling, unavailable, and exhausted
     results remain fallback.  Record a distinct
     `discrepancy_endpoints_certified` bit and require it explicitly anywhere
     a closure or seam event consumes the discrepancy proof.

  5. Surface-root isolation method is not a solid-boundary completeness
     theorem.  In particular, an interval Krawczyk box can certify the one
     surviving non-tangent surface root of a cracked solid stream.  Before
     dispatch, count clean/near, retained, non-tangent ENTER and LEAVE roots.
     If those directions are imbalanced, allow the existing seam-continuation
     theorem to run even when every box happened to be Krawczyk-qualified.
     The theorem's full edge, continuation, box/root ownership, event, and
     global-state obligations are unchanged; balanced ordinary streams still
     take the regular path without a speculative seam attempt.

The endpoint-moving Cobb family moves the entire rational boundary row while
preserving weights, independently measures the target/adjacent edge and
endpoint-vertex lift discrepancies, and sets every affected declared
tolerance.  Its 60 primary rays cover g/T = 0.25, 0.9, and 1.1, inward and
outward signs, h/T = 2.0, 0.9, 0.25, 0, and -0.25, and reversal.  Every target
now has an independently reconstructed topological endpoint contract and a
complete strict INSIDE gap proof, using at most 1,598 cells without exhaustion.
The original seam theorem certifies the inward g/T=0.9, h/T=0.25 pair in both
directions; its physical event enclosures contain the implicit-sphere
endpoints and its representatives agree within 2.5e-4 T.  Those two repairs
remove both former below-envelope no-partition leaks.  The new baseline is 16
legacy one-hit rays, two certified pairs, ten total prepared selections, 46
implicit/variant differences all inside the declared uncertainty band, zero
outside-band differences, and zero below-envelope leaks.

An additional eight-case gate repeats that certified state in both directions
under translation, arbitrary rotation plus translation, 0.01x scale, and
10,000x scale with large translation.  It reconstructs the endpoint contract,
requires the complete strict gap proof, and validates both immutable event
enclosures and representatives against similarity-scaled implicit-sphere
intervals.  Two in-memory under-declared endpoint metadata controls must be
rejected by the independent endpoint oracle; intentionally invalid BREP
metadata is not sent through full prep.  The registered
`rt_brep_ray_endpoint` process keeps these checks parallel with the main seam
matrix.

This closes the parameter-boundary assumption and a representation-sensitive
dispatch decision.  It does not classify a ray whose relevant edge parameter
lies in a vertex neighborhood, prove a curved vertex fan, authorize arbitrary
endpoint overlap root graphs, or bound an oblique contact interval whose edge
frame varies along the ray.  Those remain separate fail-closed theorems.

6.4.1.3 Bounded oblique contact intervals

The first oblique extension keeps Finding 69's root topology and publication
transaction unchanged but replaces its single frozen edge frame with a
complete interval proof.  It is not an angular-threshold relaxation.  A
nonzero tangent component means that one fixed mapped edge UV and one fixed
sector no longer own the entire unresolved contact lobe.  In particular, a
ray projection can share a t value with an edge point that is physically far
along the seam.  Projection overlap is therefore not a valid box-ownership
theorem.

Prepare the additional proof data as follows:

  1. Preserve every terminal edge/trim pair produced by the strict seam-gap
     partition.  A retained record contains the exact edge-domain interval,
     its constrained trim-domain interval, the prepared rational Bezier edge
     span, and the trim index.  Install these records only after both incident
     trims complete a strict `SEAM_GAP_INSIDE` proof.  Outside, ambiguous,
     unavailable, or budget-exhausted edges retain no production frame
     partition.

  2. Initially support frame intervals only when both trim correspondences
     were certified by the exact isoparametric theorem.  The more general
     injective/oriented correspondence proof remains valid for gap bounding,
     but endpoint UV hulls alone do not yet bound a curved non-isoparametric
     trim.  Such edges keep the old whole-ray fallback.  This was the staged
     Finding 71 boundary; Section 6.4.1.6 lifts it only with complete retained
     rational trim loci and derivatives.

  3. Sort each side's terminal cells by edge parameter and retain their exact
     oriented correspondence.  Cached prep rebuilds these process-local
     records from the BREP and existing cached acceleration data; the cache
     serialization format does not change.

At shot time, consider only the already qualified two-root incident-face
contact subtype.  Preserve the perpendicular branch byte-for-byte in policy:
it still requires the frozen mapped UV/t ownership and the fixed-frame angular
segment proof.  For a finite nonparallel ray with
`1e-10 < abs(dot(D_unit,T_unit)) < 1-1e-10`, require all of the following:

  1. Intersect the two sorted prepared edge partitions and remap every local
     edge subdivision endpoint to each trim within its parent interval.  Use a
     fixed 32-cell stack, maximum local depth 20, and at most 8,192 visited
     cells over the complete contact transaction.  Mapping, interval
     evaluation, sorting, and coverage use fixed storage; any mapping failure,
     unsupported span, nonfinite result, or capacity limit aborts without
     publishing events.

  2. Link each unresolved zero-determinant contact box to a nonempty trim
     interval on that box's own incident face.  Once the exact contact face
     and surface span have been selected from the two qualified roots, also
     admit adjacent rootless unresolved zero-determinant boxes on that same
     face and span to the proof attempt.  These are contact-corridor candidates,
     not accepted hits.  The complete interval UV hull, not just a nominal
     root or ray projection, must be contained in each box's outward UV
     rectangle.  A disjoint cell is skipped; a partially contained cell is
     subdivided.  Every contact box, including every rootless candidate, needs
     a link, and the linked box t intervals must cover the complete contact t
     hull with no gap.  Thus a broad or shifted solver box cannot be silently
     assigned to a distant point on the seam.  Never admit a rootless box on
     the source outer face or on another span: the contact material-sector
     theorem says nothing about an omitted physical surface root there.

  3. Bound the rational edge point and tangent over the edge interval by
     outward Bernstein restriction.  Bound both incident surface partials
     over containing UV neighborhoods and form interval outward normals and
     interior conormals.  Compute the rational quotient-rule numerator
     `X'W-XW'` coefficient by coefficient using Bernstein products before
     taking a convex hull.  Taking independent hull products loses the shared
     control-net cancellation and becomes unbounded after rescaling a small
     parameter interval.  A small containing parameter-width floor prevents
     roundoff amplification; because it only widens the evaluated
     neighborhood, it cannot exclude a real frame.

  4. Reject every interval that approaches either edge vertex within the
     authorized edge tolerance plus coordinate-scaled roundoff.  This is an
     edge-interior theorem, not a vertex-neighborhood policy.  Prove that the
     interval tangent has nonzero length and that its dot with the ray has one
     strict, consistent sign on every box-owning interval.  Exact or interval
     parallelism remains fallback.

  5. Use homogeneous determinant signs rather than normalized vectors or
     angles.  Let A be the topology-oriented edge tangent, C0 and C1 the two
     interior conormals, N0 and N1 their outward normals, and V(t) the ray
     point minus the interval edge point.  Require strict signs for
     `(T x C0).N0` and `(A x C1).N1`.  The sector orientation is
     `A.(C0 x C1)`; the boundary predicates are `A.(C0 x V)` and
     `A.(V x C1)`.  Project the conormal dot test into the edge-normal plane
     with the sign-equivalent expression
     `|T|^2(C0.C1) - (C0.T)(C1.T)`.

  6. For each fixed edge parameter, both boundary predicates are affine in
     ray t.  Strict interval signs at both box-t endpoints therefore prove the
     same sign on the complete segment.  A convex sector requires both
     boundary predicates positive throughout.  A reflex sector requires at
     least one predicate to remain positive throughout; this stronger
     condition prevents the segment from crossing the complementary convex
     cone.  When sector turn straddles zero but the projected conormal dot is
     strictly negative, treat the frame as a smooth pi sector and require both
     predicates.  Any zero-containing determinant remains fallback.

  7. Prefer the local subdivision child containing the already prepared
     closest-edge witness.  This changes search order only.  The same interval
     obligations apply, and failure to find a certificate within the fixed
     bounds remains fallback.  A blind low-parameter-first traversal can
     exhaust the work budget inside a wide UV box before reaching the valid
     local frame.

Only after every contact box is linked and every interval predicate succeeds
may the unchanged contact transaction resolve boxes and publish the two outer
events.  Record one oblique pair, the number of certified frame cells, and the
number of box links.  Prepared production eligibility requires exactly one
oblique pair, at least one frame cell, and a box-link count equal to the
contact-box count.  Perpendicular contact requires all oblique counters to be
zero, as do ordinary seam pairs.  The later global surface-root coverage gate
normally rejects every rootless terminal box.  Its only exception is a box
already atomically marked resolved-contact by this completed oblique
transaction, with the one-pair ledger and complete box-link equality.  This
ordering is essential: proximity, face identity, or determinant zero alone
must not waive root coverage.

The Cobb ratchet uses rays whose direction is the normalized sum of the seam

6.4.1.4 Connected source-root union certificate

An isolator terminal box is coverage evidence, not necessarily a distinct
root.  In the bowed Cobb component-0.1 rays, the outer source surface produced
one ordinary Krawczyk root box plus an adjacent depth-limit box without a
nominal root.  The two boxes overlap in their outward ray-t intervals and
touch along a UV boundary.  Treating the second box as an independent unknown
forces fallback, while accepting it merely because it is adjacent could hide
a second physical surface root.  Resolve this only with a fresh root-count
proof over the complete connected component:

  1. Attempt this extension only inside the already qualified finite,
     nonparallel oblique contact-lobe transaction.  The source root must be on
     a different face/span from the contact pair.  Every terminal box that
     contains the source root must already be a regular Krawczyk box with one
     nonzero, consistent determinant sign.  Thus the extension starts from an
     independently established surface root and does not replace ordinary
     root isolation.

  2. A possible additional member must be on exactly the source face and
     Bezier span, have unresolved disposition and zero determinant sign, and
     contain no local or fold root.  Grow a component from the root-owning
     boxes with fixed boolean arrays.  Two boxes connect only when their closed
     UV rectangles intersect in both coordinates and their outward ray-t
     intervals overlap, allowing only 256-epsilon coordinate roundoff.  Iterate
     to a fixed point.  Disconnected boxes, other roots, faces, or spans remain
     unowned and therefore force whole-ray fallback.

  3. Form the complete rectangular UV hull of the connected component.  Map it
     into the prepared Bezier span's normalized parameter domain and widen its
     two boundaries outward by one binary64 step.  Rebuild the two ray-plane
     Bernstein systems directly from the prepared positive-weight Bezier span,
     restrict both systems to that hull with the audited outward bounded
     restriction arithmetic, and map the existing source-root representative
     into the restricted unit square.  All coefficient and interval storage is
     fixed-size stack data; this proof performs no shot-time allocation.

  4. Require strict interval Krawczyk inclusion over the entire rectangular
     hull.  The inclusion theorem supplies existence and uniqueness of one
     ray/surface root in that hull.  Since a root already exists in the
     root-owning sub-box, it is the same unique root; every connected rootless
     terminal box is therefore redundant isolation coverage and cannot hide a
     second solid transition.  A failed restriction, singular midpoint frame,
     nonfinite value, or non-strict Krawczyk image leaves the boxes unresolved.

  5. Only after the contact-sector proof and all other seam obligations pass
     may the transaction mark the complete source component
     `RESOLVED_BOUNDARY`.  The existing event keeps a root-owning box as its
     representative, records the complete component box count, and takes the
     union of all component t intervals as its outward event interval.  Record
     one source-union certificate, the number of root-owning boxes, and the
     total number of component boxes.  Eligibility requires one certificate,
     `0 < root_boxes < total_boxes`, and `total_boxes` equal to the immutable
     event's source-box count.  All three fields must otherwise be zero.

  6. The global root-coverage gate retains its blanket rejection of rootless
     boxes.  Its only new exception is a zero-determinant box already marked
     resolved-boundary by an eligible seam transaction carrying the complete
     source-union ledger.  Consequently adjacency, t overlap, a numerical
     root, or even a successful contact proof alone cannot waive surface-root
     completeness.

The permanent component-0.1 Cobb trend requires this exact `1/1/2` source
ledger in both ray directions and checks the resulting event boxes and
representatives against the analytic sphere chord.  The similarity matrix now
shoots both component 1e-3 (rootless contact corridor) and component 0.1
(source-root union) forward and backward under identity, nonlinear trim
reparameterization, translation, cyclic and arbitrary-axis rotation, 0.01x
scale, and 10,000x scale.  All 28 classifications and analytic chords must be
invariant.  Because valid transformed isolation topology sometimes produces
only one source box, component 0.1 accepts either an all-zero source-union
ledger or a complete ledger; the identity trend independently forces the

For each identity direction, three copied-trace controls must also be rejected:
one moves the rootless box away from the UV-connected component, one claims
that every union box owns the root, and one makes the rootless box the event
representative.  These controls ratchet graph connectivity, strict ledger

This certificate does not merge boxes across spans, accept a component with
another reported root, prove a fold or tangent root, absorb disconnected
coverage, or authorize an arbitrary regular event stream.  It is deliberately
nested inside the narrow seam-contact theorem until broader physical-event

6.4.1.8 Explicit contact-face closure and one-miss ownership

Moving the non-isoparametric trim from the face opposite the contact roots to
the contact-root face exposes a distinction that the earlier theorem did not
need to make.  The ray/surface solver can still isolate the same three exact
surface roots and the same three terminal boxes, but the face trim classifier
can change one member of the inner two-root lobe from `CLEAN_HIT` to
`CLEAN_MISS`.  Rejecting that mathematical surface root solely because the
lifted trim moved a small authorized amount reopens the intended solid at the
seam.  Conversely, accepting arbitrary misses or using a measured crack to
enlarge its own tolerance would manufacture solid topology.

Separate the tolerance roles before changing root ownership.  Let `T` be the
model tolerance and `D` the valid nonnegative tolerance explicitly stored on
the shared BREP edge.  Ordinary closure, including the legacy repair path and
the final diagnostic classifier, must continue to use the hard limit `T`.
This preserves the existing rule that a modeled discrepancy above the minimum
valid size is not silently repaired just because edge metadata is larger.

Only a provisional candidate local to the prepared contact-event transaction
may use

    C = max(T, D)

and then only when `D` is valid, explicit, and strictly expands `T`.  Before
spending work on the continuation solve, the immutable root ledger must
already contain exactly the intended other-face one-hit/one-miss transverse
ENTER/LEAVE pattern.  The provisional classifier writes only a private
candidate trace.  It cannot alter the legacy result, increment a published
candidate ledger, resolve a box, or emit an event unless the complete theorem
below succeeds.

The provisional path must not use the effective prep tolerance wholesale.
That effective value can also contain vertex metadata or an inferred measured
discrepancy.  In particular, an unset edge tolerance currently lets prep
retain candidate evidence by expanding to the observed gap; that inferred
value must never authorize closure or a trim miss.  Invalid model tolerance is
unavailable, invalid/unset edge tolerance contributes nothing, and every
comparison keeps only a coordinate-scaled binary64 roundoff allowance.  The
strict global edge/trim correspondence and discrepancy-bound transactions
remain separate prerequisites; `C` does not repair an outside, ambiguous,
incomplete, or exhausted prep proof.

Do not collapse the two classifiers.  An implementation that substituted `C`
for `T` in general closure activated 34 additional bowed-seam transactions at
complete discrepancies `g/T=1.1`, `2`, and `10`, producing 31 exhaustive seam
test failures and changing the established pair/selection/event totals from
`22/124/44` to `56/158/112`.  The intended contact-face case is not evidence
that every edge declaration grants solid-repair authority.  The permanent
ratchet therefore requires the old bowed totals and zero ordinary closure
candidates above `T`, alongside the new private one-miss certifications.

The first production extension is deliberately limited to one classifier
miss in the already narrow incident-face contact-lobe theorem:

  1. Preserve all Section 6.4.1 input obligations.  There must be exactly one
     selected clean source root and exactly two other incident-face surface
     roots.  The two contact roots must lie on one other face and one prepared
     surface span, be strictly transverse, form an ordered ENTER/LEAVE pair,
     and lie strictly inside the source/continuation material interval.  No
     t-distance merge, nearest-root selection, or arbitrary count is allowed.
     When ordinary `T`-bounded closure did not qualify, require exactly one
     trim hit and one trim miss both in the pre-continuation screen and again
     in the authoritative post-continuation theorem.

  2. Each contact member must be either a mathematical trim hit
     (`CLEAN_HIT`/`NEAR_HIT` with non-miss trim state) or a mathematical trim
     miss (`CLEAN_MISS`/`NEAR_MISS` with miss trim state).  Require at least
     one hit and permit at most one miss.  The root already exists because the
     ray/surface solve returned it; this rule never creates, moves, reflects,
     or changes the ray distance of a surface root.

  3. Map the observed shared-edge parameter to the contact trim through the
     globally certified edge/trim correspondence.  Evaluate the shared 3D
     edge point `E`, the contact surface at the corresponding trim UV `L`, and
     the same contact surface at the miss-root UV `R`.  The miss is eligible
     only when both

         |R-E| <= C + roundoff
         |R-L| <= C + roundoff.

     These are two distinct model-space checks.  Proximity to the edge alone
     cannot excuse an unrelated trim miss, and proximity to the trim alone
     cannot pull an interior root into the seam corridor.  A nonfinite point,
     invalid face, missing surface, or second miss fails the whole ray closed.

  4. Treat the point test only as the cheap association screen.  Before it can

     require that whole image inside both the `E` and `L` tubes.  The detailed
     fixed-expansion contraction and rational image theorem below is
     mandatory; nominal-point proximity alone is not authority.

  5. The local root/tube proof grants no boundary event by itself.  Every source
     box, every contact-root box, and every rootless determinant-corridor box

     proof, rational trim-cell ownership, full contact t-hull coverage,
     outward material-sector frame signs, monotone edge sweep, event order,
     and minimum-segment checks remain unchanged.  Publish the two outer
     source/continuation events and the miss ledger atomically only after the
     complete transaction succeeds.  The inner contact lobe remains hidden
     evidence and is never emitted as a solid boundary.

  6. Record the number of admitted classifier misses independently from the
     two contact roots.  A noncontact certificate requires zero contact and
     miss ledgers.  A contact certificate requires exactly two contact roots
     and at most one miss.  Existing opposite-face, isoparametric, oblique,
     bowed-seam, and contact-trend tests require zero misses, so the extension
     cannot silently take over their successful classifications.

box evaluator to all three difference polynomials and to `W` over the
certified root box, including the portion beyond `[0,1]^2`.  Require a
strictly positive lower weight bound.  Divide each difference enclosure by
the weight enclosure and combine their outward squares to obtain a Euclidean
surface-image displacement bound `B`.  The analytic extension is eligible
only when

    |P-E| + B <= C + coordinate_roundoff
    |P-L| + B <= C + coordinate_roundoff,

where `E` and `L` are the same certified shared-edge and lifted-trim points
used by the earlier association test.  Thus every possible root in the
unique contraction box remains inside both declared model-space tubes.  No
parameter-domain extrapolation receives authority merely from proximity, and
positive weight, uniqueness, edge proximity, and trim proximity are
independent fail-closed obligations.

Run this proof only for the explicit-declaration one-hit/one-miss transaction.
It performs no allocation during ray tracing and records attempts,
availability, uniqueness, support-boundary extension, image bounds, and tube
failure separately.  Publication additionally requires exactly one attempt,
one available unique root, no tube failure, and every unchanged box,
material-sector, order, and minimum-segment obligation described above.
Ordinary model-tolerance contacts and all zero-miss paths retain their prior
logic and must report no local-certificate ledger.

The permanent C/C++ arithmetic corpus begins with three affine boundary roots
and three nearby quadratic root pairs at equation scales `2^-100`, `1`, and
`2^100`, plus multi-root, singular, uncertain-coefficient, invalid-input, and
explicit tube-containment rejection controls.  Its next conditioning layer
contains a degree-elevated bi-affine order-9-by-9 system, a coupled
degree-15-by-7 system whose root lies just beyond the support boundary, and
two nearly singular affine systems whose Jacobian determinants are `2^-20`
and `2^-40`.  These make thirteen classified systems: ten unique-root
certificates and three deliberate fail-closed results.  All positive cases
must certify in one radius attempt without capacity exhaustion.

Exercise the rational model-image half of the theorem through actual prepared
surfaces as well as extracted coefficient arrays.  A rational bilinear face
with equal weights, the same face with a `2^20` control-weight ratio and
reversed ray, and that weighted face after `10,000x` scale plus large
translation must all certify a boundary-crossing root, a positive weight
interval, and a finite complete model-image bound.  This is a conditioning
test, not authority for numerically singular parameterizations.  A permanent
`2^400` weight-ratio control makes OpenNURBS midpoint-normal evaluation
unavailable during SurfaceTree construction, before the local certificate is
called.  The complete BREP prep must reject that surface without a process
abort, partial BVH, leaked ownership, or attempted ray solve.  Keep this prep
failure separate and do not weaken surface regularity or root-proof
obligations to admit it.

The real Cobb replay extracts the actual prepared projected coefficient
intervals for all sixteen similarity/reversal cases.  Every real miss
certifies in one radius attempt, crosses the analytic span boundary, excludes
the neighboring root, retains positive rational weight, and bounds the full
image inside both tubes.  Keep all of these difficult systems as a ratchet
before generalizing the local theorem to other seam, vertex, or grazing
policies.  Register the arithmetic and weighted-surface corpus as
`rt_brep_ray_local_root`, include it in the exhaustive interval and unsharded
compatibility processes, and keep the real production replay in
`rt_brep_ray_contact_trim` so CTest can schedule both independently.

The generic sphere grazing bridge in Section 7.7 is the first real close-root
extension of this corpus.  It reuses actual roots and prepared rational
coefficients rather than manufacturing a pair of affine equations.  Keep it
separate from the declared-contact production replay: successful local
certificates establish only isolated simple roots, while a general grazing
transaction still needs complete parent coverage and event/material proofs.

6.4.2 Cyclic planar vertex-fan theorem

A BREP vertex can be one physical boundary crossing even though three or more
incident faces independently report roots, or numerical conditioning causes
one of those redundant face solves not to report a root.  Do not select one
incident normal, delete an even number of observations, or infer a transition
from retained-hit parity.  Prepare the oriented topological link of the vertex
and classify the solid state on the unit sphere of ray directions.

The first production theorem is deliberately restricted to closed manifold
planar fans.  It is broad enough to cover convex and reentrant polyhedral
vertices and exact implicit-to-BREP extrusions, while leaving curved,
non-manifold, tolerance-near, and tangent cases on the complete legacy path.
The restrictions are proof obligations, not heuristics:

  1. Create one immutable prep record per BREP vertex.  Every incident edge
     must be unique, have exactly two trims, have supported positive-weight
     rational edge data, pass the global edge/lift injectivity and orientation
     theorem, and have a conservative geometric gap decision that authorizes
     its lifted representations.  Any exhaustion or unavailable proof makes
     the complete vertex record unsupported.

  2. Require a valence of at least three and no greater than the fixed local-
     root/event workspace capacity.  Every incident face must occur on exactly
     two incident edges.  Directed edge/face incidences must assemble into one
     connected cycle; reject boundary, repeated, disconnected, or
     inconsistently oriented components.  This is a sufficient closed-manifold
     test, not an attempt to repair malformed topology.

  3. Evaluate a finite outgoing edge tangent at the vertex endpoint.  Use the
     edge endpoint, trim direction, trim reversal, and face reversal to orient
     the pair of edge tangents bounding each material face sector.  Require
     every outgoing tangent to lie in the corresponding face plane and reject
     degenerate corners.

  4. Preserve each face sector's complete directed sweep.  The two endpoint
     vectors do not identify whether the material sector is the minor or major
     arc: a reentrant 270-degree face sector has the same endpoint directions
     as a convex 90-degree complement.  Store the signed clockwise sweep
     selected by the oriented trim/face cycle, including reflex sweeps.

  5. Require every incident surface to be planar only within a coordinate-
     scaled floating-point roundoff envelope.  The implemented sufficient
     bound is `4096 * DBL_EPSILON * coordinate_scale`.  It is intentionally
     independent of model tolerance: exact-zero planarity is not invariant
     under evaluated rotations, while model tolerance would incorrectly
     authorize genuinely curved surfaces.  A later curved theorem must bound
     differential variation rather than relax this test.

At shot time, first establish that the mathematical ray line contains the
stored topological vertex within a coordinate-scaled binary64 roundoff bound.
This exact-locus witness is distinct from later clustering.  A line merely
within model tolerance of the vertex does not qualify and must retain the
ordinary/edge/fallback behavior.  After the witness is established, nearby
redundant local-root observations may be associated with the vertex using a
bounded fraction of model tolerance.

Classify the crossing by the oriented spherical link.  For unit ray direction
`D`, the closed link winding obeys

    W(D) / (2 pi) = I(+D) - I(-D),

where `I` is the local solid indicator.  Consequently winding +1 is ENTER,
winding -1 is LEAVE, and zero is no transition.  For each prepared face arc:

  * divide its stored full sweep into no more than eight pieces, each no larger
    than pi/4;

  * rotate successive directions in the face plane with Rodrigues' formula;

  * project the endpoints perpendicular to `D`; and

  * accumulate the oriented `atan2` angle in long double.

Require the ray to be transverse to every incident face and the sum to enclose
exactly plus or minus `2 pi` within a scale-aware arithmetic bound.  A ray on a
face plane, a vanishing projection, zero winding, or an ambiguous winding is
not resolved by this theorem.  In particular, exact face-tangent contact is a
future contact theorem, not an ENTER/LEAVE guess.

Before appending an event, prove complete source ownership for the whole ray:

  1. At least one non-tangent local root and one retained terminal box must
     supply numerical provenance for each physical vertex cluster.  Pure
     topological line coincidence with no local surface evidence is not enough
     for production publication.

  2. Every local root in the vertex association interval must belong to an
     incident face, be the sole root for that face in the cluster, have a
     finite non-tangent direction equal to the winding result, and not be an
     unrelated `CRACK_HIT`.

  3. Each observed root owns every terminal box that contains it.  Every
     terminal box on an incident face whose outward t interval contains the
     exact vertex parameter must have a matching observed root and be owned.
     A box with no root is incomplete and invokes fallback.

  4. The cyclic topology may account for an incident face whose redundant
     surface solver root is absent only if no terminal box for that face
     covers the event.  This permits transform-dependent redundant-root
     omission without overlooking any actual prepared work.  It must never be
     used to excuse an unowned root or box.

  5. Permit several disjoint vertex clusters on one ray, and permit ordinary
     roots outside those clusters, only when all ownership sets are disjoint
     and the remaining ordinary theorem succeeds.  Reject overlapping source
     intervals, multiply owned boxes/roots, or capacity exhaustion.

After every cluster and remaining event succeeds, mark vertex-owned boxes
`RESOLVED_BOUNDARY` and append one immutable `VERTEX_FAN` event at the exact
topological vertex parameter.  The event carries the vertex identity, an
invalid edge identity, `VERTEX_FAN` source kind and certificate, `CRACK_HIT`
hit class, direction, canonical source root, representative source box,
source-box count, and an outward ray-parameter interval.  All other event
classes carry an invalid vertex identity.  Maintain independent telemetry for
prepared/supported records, attempts, candidates, winding checks and
ambiguity, certifications, events, owned boxes, and owned roots.

No box disposition or event count may change during a failed attempt.  Only
after the complete event stream passes the normal {0,1} solid-state machine,
outward minimum-segment proof, and fixed-workspace hit reconstruction may it
replace the dense SurfaceTree answer atomically.  Prep may own variable-sized
immutable fan records; ray traversal, winding, ownership, and event assembly
must use fixed-capacity storage and perform no allocation.

The permanent directed gate has four parts:

  * a converted convex ARB8 with exact vertex-plus-regular rays in both
    directions, independently checking three incident roots/boxes, event
    provenance, one material interval, exact face-tangent fallback, and rays
    displaced 0.25 model tolerance from the vertex;

  * an exact concave L-shaped implicit EXTRUDE converted to BREP, with one line
    crossing a reentrant vertex and a convex vertex, two disjoint fan events,
    and endpoint comparison against the implicit primitive;

  * translation, arbitrary rotation, 0.01x scale, and 10,000x scale plus large
    translation, with scaled length tolerances and both ray directions; and

  * independent reconstruction of incident faces, every observed root, every
    terminal box, directions, source counts, disjoint ownership, event
    reversal, and the final production partition from the immutable trace.

Do not require the number of redundant incident-face roots to be identical
under similarity transforms; require at least one root/box per physical vertex
and complete ownership of everything the prepared solver actually retained.
The analytic/implicit oracle and final physical transition must remain
invariant.  Curved incident faces, tolerance-near intended vertex closure,
non-manifold fans, and face-tangent contact require separately specified
theorems and corpus gates before they acquire publication authority.

6.5 Solid assembly

Process resolved events in t order with oriented winding/state, assuming the
line begins outside at negative infinity.  ENTER increments the local solid
state, LEAVE decrements it, and CONTACT changes nothing.  Emit segments over
the intervals in which the state is inside.

This replaces "the first sign is entering" and even-hit-count repair.  A final
nonzero state, a negative state, an unresolved non-manifold cluster, or
incompatible interval ordering is a structured failure and invokes the old
path during rollout.

For a well-defined oriented BREP volume, winding must remain nonnegative.
Disjoint shells return to zero between intervals.  Overlapping outward shells
may raise winding above one while their union remains material; the first
zero-to-one ENTER and final one-to-zero LEAVE are the published union
boundaries.  An inward-oriented cavity produces a LEAVE to zero followed by
an ENTER back to one.  A negative state, an unresolved final nonzero state, or
an event whose identity/order is ambiguous is a structured failure.  Do not
repair it by deleting an arbitrary hit.

This nonzero-winding contract is now independently gated by one BREP primitive
containing two appended overlapping converted-sphere shells and an implicit
CSG-union oracle.  It is distinct from tolerance-local duplicate-event
coalescence: same-direction seam observations inside the model-resolution
envelope resolve to one event, whereas certified shell boundaries farther
apart each change winding.  The current prepared selector still restricts
publication to its already-certified alternating {0,1} event classes and
falls back on general overlap.  Extend prepared authority to winding above one
only after complete event ownership proves those shell crossings; the legacy
fallback's parameter-local rule is not that proof.

Do not decide interval existence from nominal endpoint separation.  Subtract
the outward event t intervals and compare them with an outward bound for
`rti_tol.dist / |D|`.  A strict material gap forms a segment, a strict
subminimum gap becomes zero-material contact, and a straddling result is
unresolved.  This is the same policy already gated for cross-span fold pairs
and must become the sole minimum-size classifier for all event classes.

Before segment emission, apply the minimum-valid-interval rule consistently
with rti_tol.dist and rt_boolfinal.  Preserve the original face/UV/normal data
selected for segment endpoints so rt_brep_norm, curvature, UV reporting, and
shading remain meaningful.

6.6 Plate mode and invalid topology

Plate mode is a distinct contract.  A valid but non-solid BREP currently
defaults to zero-thickness plate mode, and each accepted surface hit becomes a
plate interval.  Do not apply solid crack closure or winding assembly to such
objects unless plate semantics explicitly request it.

Keep the legacy unsewn Cobb sphere as a plate-mode regression.  The new sewn
Cobb fixture described below is the solid crack oracle.  Invalid and
non-manifold BREPs should produce diagnostics and use the conservative legacy
behavior until a separate policy is specified.


7. COBB-SPHERE CONTROLLED TEST FRAMEWORK
----------------------------------------

7.1 Why the Cobb sphere is useful

src/proc-db/brep_cobb.cpp implements J. E. Cobb's rational Bezier sphere
tiling.  It creates six 5x5 rational Bezier surfaces, rotated into cube-face
positions.  It is ideal for this work because:

  * the underlying analytic oracle is a sphere;
  * it has twelve long patch seams and eight three-face vertices;
  * every patch is non-planar;
  * seam and vertex rays can be constructed exactly; and
  * homogeneous control points can be perturbed reproducibly.

However, the current generator calls NewFace independently six times.
Inspection of its generated database reports:

  Valid: YES
  Solid: NO
  Plate mode: YES, zero thickness
  Faces: 6
  Edges: 24
  Vertices: 24
  Trims: 24

Each face owns four boundary edges; matching geometric edges are not shared.
That form must not be treated as the solid oracle.

7.2 Shared fixture support

Move the duplicated Cobb face construction in brep_cobb.cpp and
brep_invalid.cpp into internal reusable test support.  Preserve the existing
command's output for compatibility, then add constructors for:

  1. cobb_unsewn: the current six independent faces, used for plate-mode and
     legacy rendering tests.

  2. cobb_sewn: the same six surfaces with cube topology: eight shared
     vertices, twelve shared 3D edges, twenty-four trims, two mated trims per
     edge, six oriented outer loops, and consistent face orientation.

  3. cobb_variant: a deep copy of cobb_sewn with one deterministic
     perturbation specification applied.

The sewn constructor must pass IsValid, IsSolid, and IsManifold with oriented
true, boundary false, and exactly two trims on every edge.  Before using it as
an exact oracle, sample every patch against the unit sphere and record maximum
radial error.  The expected result is roundoff-level disagreement; tests must
use the measured baseline rather than assuming zero.

7.3 Perturbation representation

Define a test-only perturbation specification containing:

  family
  target face/edge/vertex
  signed requested amplitude in model units
  declared edge/trim/vertex tolerance policy
  topology-preserving or topology-breaking flag
  scale and translation
  UV domain transform
  deterministic seed only for selecting a target, never for solver behavior

When moving a rational control point, preserve its weight.  Convert the
homogeneous CV to Euclidean P, apply delta, and store (P + delta) * w with the
same w.  Calibrate perturbations by measuring the resulting maximum 3D seam
discrepancy; a control-point displacement is not generally equal to maximum
curve displacement.

7.4 Required perturbation families

A. Boundary-row gap/overlap

Move the boundary row of one patch along the local outward radial or
co-normal direction while leaving the adjacent patch unchanged.  Test both a
gap and overlap.  Provide endpoint-preserving and endpoint-moving variants.

B. Bowed seam

Leave the two edge endpoints fixed and move the three interior boundary CVs.
This creates zero corner error and a maximum discrepancy near the edge
middle, defeating endpoint-only tolerance checks.

C. Vertex defect

Perturb one cube-corner control point or the boundary ends of one incident
face.  Sweep rays through and around the three-face vertex fan.

D. Surface-only disagreement

Move surface boundary CVs but keep the shared 3D edge, 2D trims, and topology
metadata unchanged.  This directly exercises ON_BrepEdge tolerance semantics.

E. Trim-only disagreement

Offset or bow one 2D trim inward/outward while retaining the surface and 3D
edge.  Test anisotropic u and v errors separately.  This exercises trim
parameter tolerances and 3D lifted-distance verification.

F. Edge-only disagreement

Perturb the shared 3D edge curve without changing either surface lift.  This
tests whether edge tolerance is used as metadata/evidence without incorrectly
moving the actual mathematical surface hits.

G. G1 discontinuity without a crack

Keep the common boundary CV row identical and move only the first interior CV
row.  Position remains continuous, but normals differ.  Shoot grazing rays to
ensure a normal discontinuity is not interpreted as a gap or duplicate solid
boundary.

H. Exact geometry-preserving representation changes

Insert knots, elevate degree where supported, split one patch into multiple
faces with shared topology, reverse a parameter direction, and apply extreme
affine domain scalings such as [0,1e6] by [0,1e-6].  These variants must give
the same result as pristine Cobb.  A rational linear-fractional
reparameterization may be added if OpenNURBS can construct it exactly.  Do
not label an approximate refit as geometry-preserving.

I. Combined trim and surface error

Move a surface boundary and its trim in different directions with separately
declared tolerances.  This represents realistic imported geometry and tests
that UV and model-space errors are accumulated conservatively rather than
compared as one scalar.

Implementation checkpoint (2026-08-09): the first isolated E and F fixtures
are now implemented in src/librt/tests/brep_ray.cpp and registered as
rt_brep_ray_defect.  The trim-only case bows one incident 2-D trim while both
surfaces and the shared 3-D edge remain exact.  It measures U and V changes
separately and records the resulting anisotropic trim metadata.  The edge-only
case bows only the interior rational 3-D edge controls, preserving its
endpoints, both trims, and both surface lifts.  Dense independent samples
calibrate each requested 3-D discrepancy; neither generator uses a requested
control displacement as the oracle.

For these defects, a tangent-centered line is the wrong isolation tool: its
two roots lie O(sqrt(R*h)) away from the seam.  The permanent ray constructor
instead pins one analytic sphere endpoint to the seam midpoint and chooses the
ray direction from the desired endpoint separation L.  It tests L/T = 100,
10, 2, 1.1, and exact contact, toward both incident faces and in both ray
directions.  With g/T = 0.25 and 0.9, the initial matrix contains 40 valid
trim-only and 80 valid edge-only rays.  All 120 final results match the
implicit ELL and analytic endpoints within 7.08e-8, while all 120 prepared
attempts conservatively select whole-ray fallback.  This is the intentional
pre-theorem baseline.

The trace localizes rather than conceals the missing authority.  At a seam
endpoint, one incident face may contribute a trim miss while its mate
contributes a tolerance-near hit at the same physical event.  The adaptive
surface isolator also retains duplicate unresolved terminal boxes on either
side of an internal subdivision boundary.  Trim-only cases reach the exact
manifold-edge resolver but cannot own those boxes; edge-only cases no longer
possess an exact geometric edge witness and reach declared-tolerance seam
resolution.  The production extension must prove a complete connected
terminal-box component has exactly the intended root, certify one orientation
over its enclosure, bind the actual root to the authorized trim/edge tube, and
publish the full event stream atomically.  Do not resolve this baseline by
merging nearby hits or by accepting a point sample as proof of box/root
completeness.

7.5 Perturbation sweep

For every family, adapt the requested CV/trim change until the measured
maximum 3D mismatch g reaches these normalized targets relative to the
effective authorized seam envelope T:

  g/T = 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 2, 10

Independently test tolerance metadata with:

  * correct declared tolerance;
  * ON_UNSET_VALUE, requiring computation;
  * explicit zero;
  * declared tolerance half the actual discrepancy; and
  * declared tolerance twice the actual discrepancy.

The threshold behavior is part of the result:

  * At or below the authorized envelope, the solid interpretation must not
    leak and should match the implicit sphere outside the explicit uncertainty
    band.

  * Above the envelope, the implementation must report excessive discrepancy
    or fall back.  It must not silently increase tolerance until the sphere
    happens to close.

  * An over-declared tolerance may authorize a large disagreement because it
    explicitly records modeling accuracy, but it must be visible in
    diagnostics and can only affect the mated topology that declares it.

Run the matrix at radii well below, near, and well above one model unit, and at
large translations from the origin.  Equal g/T cases must behave consistently
under scale and translation within floating-point limits.

7.6 Directed Cobb rays

Generate deterministic families rather than relying on image pixels:

  * axes through patch centers;
  * exact rays through the north and south pole;
  * rays through every seam midpoint, normal and oblique to the local seam;
  * rays exactly along each seam tangent;
  * parallel offset sweeps across each seam on both sides;
  * rays through all eight vertices and small angular/positional fans around
    each vertex;
  * exact tangent rays at face interiors, seams, and vertices;
  * near-tangent angular sweeps on both sides of tangency;
  * rays whose two sphere roots approach each other toward tangency;
  * reversed versions of every ray; and
  * rays beginning inside as well as outside the sphere.

For a unit sphere, analytic line/sphere roots provide expected t values and
whether the ray is a miss, contact, or interval.  Compare the full ordered
partition, not only hit count.

7.7 Grazing-limit behavior study

Near-tangent behavior needs its own convergence study, not a handful of
special rays.  The study must approach tangency from both the hit and miss
sides and record how raw roots, final solid intervals, numerical conditioning,
and local-refinement work change.

For a sphere of radius R, use a unit-direction line whose perpendicular
distance from the center is b.  Define signed inward clearance:

  h = R - b

For h > 0 the analytic root separation/chord is:

  delta_t = 2 * sqrt(R^2 - b^2)
          = 2 * sqrt(2*R*h - h^2)

and, close to tangency:

  delta_t ~= 2 * sqrt(2*R*h)

At either root:

  |N dot D| = sqrt(R^2 - b^2) / R = delta_t / (2*R)

Thus h, delta_t, and |N dot D| give three independent ways to verify that the
test is approaching the same geometric limit.  At h = 0 there is one double
root/contact and no material interval.  At h < 0 there is no real root.

Use a logarithmic signed sweep rather than linear pixel offsets.  Include:

  * h/R = 2^-k for k spanning ordinary chords down to the representable
    floating-point/tolerance limit;
  * cases selected so delta_t/rti_tol.dist is 100, 10, 2, 1.1, 1, 0.9,
    0.5, 0.1, and smaller;
  * exact h = 0 constructed analytically rather than by an accumulated camera
    transform; and
  * matching negative h values on the miss side.

Run every case against the implicit sphere, its normal ft_brep conversion, the
pristine sewn Cobb sphere, and selected perturbed Cobb seams.  Repeat at face
interiors, seam midpoints, and vertices; reverse the ray; rotate the geometry;
use non-unit ray directions; and repeat over object scale and translation.

Capture two levels of result:

  1. Raw primitive observations before rt_boolfinal: isolated parameter boxes,
     mathematical candidate roots, residuals, t intervals, normal dot values,
     solver outcome, subdivision depth/count, and fallback use.

  2. Normal librt partitions after Boolean tolerance processing: miss,
     contact/zero-thickness event, or material interval and its endpoints.

The expected trend from the hit side is:

  * delta_t decreases monotonically according to the analytic square-root
    law;

  * the two raw simple roots remain present and ordered while their isolating
    intervals can be separated;

  * absolute endpoint error stays bounded even though relative error in a
    vanishing chord necessarily grows;

  * refinement effort and conditioning warnings may grow, but failure status
    remains deterministic and fallback is explicit;

  * once delta_t enters the rti_tol.dist regime, final partition behavior
    matches the implicit oracle and rt_boolfinal policy; and

  * at exact tangency the candidate is a CONTACT_TANGENT with zero winding
    change, followed by a stable miss on the negative-h side.

Keep a focused certificate bridge independent of the full signed sweep.  At a
generic sphere point away from seams and surface-span boundaries, use radial
direction `(2,3,6)/7` and tangent direction `(3,-2,0)/sqrt(13)`.  Construct
chords whose separations are `100`, `10`, `2`, `1.1`, `1`, `0.9`, `0.5`,
`0.1`, and `0.01` times `rti_tol.dist`, in both ray directions.  Match the two
raw local roots to the analytic endpoints and replay their actual prepared
face, span, and UV values through the fixed-expansion local-root certificate.
Require both roots to occupy one analytic span, remain strictly inside it,
retain positive rational weight, and certify again with a radius cap strictly
below one quarter of their normalized UV separation.  Root separation and
`|N dot D|` must follow the analytic monotone trend, and certification must not
restart after becoming unavailable.  Exact tangency must refuse a unique-root
certificate, and the mirrored miss cases must produce neither local roots nor
solid intervals.

This bridge tests a theorem about already located simple roots.  It is not a
coverage proof for the parent surface region, does not prove absence of other
roots, and does not authorize a general grazing partition.  In particular,
the exact double root remains outside the unique-contraction theorem.  Keep
candidate completeness, physical-event pairing, and minimum-segment policy as
separate obligations before using these certificates in the production
grazing dispatcher.  Register the bridge as `rt_brep_ray_grazing_root` so its
small difficult-system matrix runs in parallel with the full directed,
interval, and seam sweeps.

Do not impose a fixed grazing-normal cutoff as the expected transition.  The
meaningful transition is governed by root isolation, analytic geometry, and
the final minimum-segment policy.  Plot or tabulate classification, measured
delta_t, endpoint error, iterations, subdivisions, and fallback rate against
both h/R and delta_t/rti_tol.dist.  There must be no non-monotone islands in
which a sequence approaching tangency alternates between ordinary hit, miss,
odd hit, and hit again.

After the sphere is correct, repeat the limit study on curved geometries with
different local behavior:

  * ellipsoids at high- and low-curvature principal directions;
  * torus outer and inner tangencies, including close pairs among four roots;
  * a cylinder tangent in its curved direction and rays nearly parallel to
    its zero-curvature direction;
  * converted cone/quadric surfaces near caps and patch boundaries; and
  * G1-discontinuous but position-continuous Cobb seams.

For a regular surface with normal clearance h and normal curvature kappa in
the ray direction, the local quadratic root separation is proportional to
sqrt(h/kappa).  Use that prediction to normalize non-spherical trends, while
using the implicit primitive's actual ray results as the authoritative oracle.
Parabolic, singular, and multi-root cases should be labeled separately rather
than forced into the quadratic model.

8. GENERAL IMPLICIT-TO-BREP CORRECTNESS TESTING
-----------------------------------------------

This is the primary correctness program.  The STEP corpus is valuable for
coverage and performance, but it generally lacks an independent exact answer.
In contrast, BRL-CAD can raytrace a known CSG model with mature implicit
primitive code, convert that same model to BREP, and compare two
representations of the same intended solid.  No BREP solver change should be
promoted on corpus images alone if it fails this paired ground truth.

8.1 Existing foundation

src/librt/tests/primitive_brep.cpp already checks that several ft_brep
conversions produce valid, solid, oriented manifolds with paired edges.  It
does not currently shoot the converted geometry.  Extend this testing in a
new focused executable rather than overloading topology-only assertions.

Start with primitives that have reliable implicit raytracers and analytic
area/volume formulas:

  * sphere/ellipsoid;
  * box/ARB8;
  * right circular cylinder;
  * truncated cone;
  * torus; and
  * additional converted quadrics after the basic harness is stable.

Keep more complicated EPA/EHY/HYP/PART/extrude conversions in the topology
suite and add them to paired ray testing when suitable ground truth is
available.

Add a second tier of actual CSG regions converted to BREP.  The original CSG
tree remains the raytracing oracle even when a compact analytic area formula
is unavailable.  The initial set should include:

  * disjoint union, producing several material intervals on one line;
  * overlapping sphere union;
  * two-sphere intersection lens;
  * sphere or cylinder subtraction producing an internal cavity;
  * box minus cylinder, producing planar/non-planar trimmed faces;
  * nested union/subtraction with multiple entry and exit events; and
  * shells and thin walls at several multiples of rti_tol.dist.

The first overlap gate is now implemented one level below Boolean BREP
conversion so it isolates ray assembly: convert two implicit spheres, append
the two closed BREP shells into one valid solid BREP primitive, and compare its
directed axis interval with the original implicit CSG union in both ray
directions.  This fixture distinguishes union `[12,28]` from the historical
intersection `[18,22]` without involving STEP import, trimming, tree depth, or
a BREP Boolean implementation.  Keep it permanently, then add an evaluated
topology-identical BREP union as the stronger later hierarchy case.

Begin with operands separated from coincident and sub-tolerance degeneracies
so the conversion has one clear intended result.  Then introduce deliberate
near-coincident faces and thin features as a separate tolerance-policy suite.
Do not mix conversion ambiguity into the baseline solver gate.

8.2 Paired ray comparison

For each primitive construct the implicit object and its ft_brep conversion in
separate prepared rt_i instances.  Use the identical ray origin and direction
against both.  Record all returned partitions after normal librt Boolean
processing.

For CSG-region cases, prepare the original CSG tree in one rt_i and the
converted BREP result in the other.  Use the same region semantics, ray
minimum/maximum range, rti_tol, overlap policy, and explicitly shared sampling
bounding box.  Compare both raw primitive segments where practical and final
partitions; the latter are the solid behavioral contract.

Compare:

  * miss versus hit;
  * number and order of material intervals;
  * entry and exit t within model-space tolerance;
  * entry/exit orientation;
  * forward/reverse symmetry;
  * finite point, normal, curvature, and UV outputs; and
  * solver/fallback status.

Classify rays inside a defined analytic tangency band separately.  They must
remain deterministic and safe, but an exact tangent should not be used to
claim a statistically meaningful inside interval.

The paired harness should report the first differing ray with enough data to
replay it as a single directed test.  Aggregate-only pass/fail is insufficient:
opposite errors in crossing count or chord length can cancel in a Crofton
estimate.

Generally speaking the CSG intersections should be reliable, but it is possible
that grazing behaviors or limit cases might hit CSG issues.  When we have a
disagreement between a CSG ray and a brep ray, it is reasonable to double check
the CSG answer if it seems to be confounding the analysis.

8.3 Cauchy-Crofton verification

src/librt/primitives/crofton.cpp implements:

  SA = 4*pi*R^2 * crossings / (2*rays)
  V  = pi*R^2 * total_chord / rays

with a fixed RNG seed.  src/librt/tests/crofton.c already checks analytic
primitives and convergence behavior.

For implicit/CSG-to-BREP comparison, generate each deterministic line once
from one explicitly shared bounding sphere and feed it to both
representations.  Do not allow small differences in prepared bounding boxes
to change the sample.  For each line retain the complete ordered partitions,
crossing count, and total chord for both representations.  Compute per-line
differences as well as independent aggregates.

Implement this without weakening the production Crofton API: extract or
reproduce its fixed-seed line generator in a librt test helper, materialize a
read-only array of line origins/directions, and shoot that array through two
prepared rt_i instances with a comparison callback that copies each final
partition into bounded test records.  Feed the same per-line crossing and
chord values into the existing Crofton formulas.  The ordinary
rt_crofton_shoot entry point should remain the production estimator; the
paired harness is an exact A/B validation tool.

Use progressive sample levels such as 2,000, 8,000, 32,000, and 128,000.
Compute batch/block estimates so the test reports standard error rather than
using only successive-estimate stability.  Acceptance should require:

  * analytic area and volume inside a preselected confidence band;
  * BREP and implicit paired estimates inside that band;
  * no persistent bias as sample count increases; and
  * directed ray tests passing independently, since random Crofton rays will
    almost never hit an exact seam, pole, or tangent.

In addition, report:

  * fraction of lines with different hit/miss classification;
  * fraction with a different number of material intervals;
  * crossing-count difference distribution;
  * total absolute and signed chord-length difference;
  * the contribution of differing lines to area and volume error; and
  * disagreement stratified by grazing angle, trim proximity, and solver
    fallback status.

For baseline analytic and nondegenerate CSG conversions, the target is no
unexplained paired partition difference outside the explicit numerical and
tangent uncertainty bands.  Merely obtaining an area/volume estimate within a
few percent is not sufficient.  Crofton then supplies a powerful integrated
check that the remaining enormous ray population has no systematic bias.

Volume is generally the more stable solid test near minimum-size slivers.
Surface area is more sensitive to representation and angular survival of thin
features, as documented in include/rt/func.h.  Report both; do not dismiss a
stable area disagreement as sampling noise merely because volume agrees.

8.4 Later-stage hierarchy duality checks

After the individual primitive and small controlled CSG gates are stable, add
two more stressful comparisons.  These are valuable qualification targets,
but they must not replace the simpler fixtures that localize a failure to leaf
intersection, Boolean assembly, or conversion.

The first comparison preserves an existing implicit CSG hierarchy exactly and
replaces every leaf having an exact ft_brep conversion with a BREP leaf.  Keep
all union/intersection/subtraction operators, matrices, region attributes,
tolerances, ray bounds, and overlap policy unchanged.  Do not evaluate the
tree into one BREP for this test: the purpose is to compare the mature implicit
leaf solvers against NURBS leaf solvers under identical librt Boolean weaving.
Build the original and substituted databases in memory, shoot the identical
directed and Crofton lines through both, and retain ordered final partitions.
Classify unsupported leaves explicitly and begin with fully replaceable trees;
mixed trees can follow once the all-BREP-leaf case is understood.

Implementation checkpoint (2026-08-09): the first topology-identical nested
fixture is now in src/librt/tests/brep_ray.cpp.  It defines one radius-5 ELL
leaf, reuses it under translations to -8 and +8 on X in a nested union
combination, and subtracts a translated radius-2 ELL from the left component.
The comparison database replaces only the two leaf definitions with their
exact ft_brep results; it preserves the combination nesting, reuse, member
matrices, region subtraction, tolerance, and ray set.  This exercises a
hollow left component plus a solid right component, including three ordered
material intervals along the X axis.  Four analytic directed rays check the
forward and reverse three-interval answers and transverse shell/solid answers.

Its exact independent measures are 216*pi surface area and (968/3)*pi volume.
On the fixed 8,000-line shared Crofton sample the implicit and BREP-leaf trees
have zero differing lines and zero tangent-band exceptions; maximum paired
endpoint error is 3.84e-8.  Both independent estimators pass the confidence
bands around the analytic measures.  This satisfies the first nondegenerate
nested/reused/transformed BREP-leaf hierarchy gate.  It does not yet qualify
near-coincident members, thin walls near rti_tol.dist, partially replaceable
mixed trees, arbitrary region attributes/overlap policies, or the reverse
BREP-to-CSG recognizer.  Those remain separate tiers so a failure can still be
localized to leaf solving, Boolean assembly, tolerance policy, or recognition.

The second comparison runs in the opposite direction.  BRL-CAD's experimental
BREP shape recognizer, exposed by the libged `brep csg` command, calls
brep_to_csg() and may reconstruct either a complete implicit CSG tree or a
partial tree retaining residual BREP pieces.  Apply it to simple controlled
BREPs first and then to the NIST example solids expected to be substantially
or fully recognizable.  Compare the original BREP against the reconstructed
tree with the same shared-line partition harness.  Record whether each result
is:

  * fully implicit, providing the strongest independent intersection oracle;
  * mixed implicit/BREP, which still stresses recognition, transformations,
    tree semantics, and the recognized leaves but is not fully independent;
  * unsupported without mutation; or
  * conversion-failed/invalid, which is a conversion result and must not be
    misreported as a ray-solver discrepancy.

For both directions, serialize the first disagreeing model and ray into a
small replayable C/C++ fixture or database artifact.  Preserve passing cases
permanently.  A conversion improvement may reduce a documented disagreement,
but never encode the old incorrect tree or partition as the expected answer.
These dual conversions are later qualification tiers because they exercise
more code and make root-cause attribution harder than the analytic primitive,
Cobb, and direct BREP-leaf CSG corpora.


9. DRAWING IMPROVEMENTS AND TESTING
-----------------------------------

Drawing work should follow the ray correctness foundation but use the same
analytic and perturbed fixtures.

9.1 Wireframe edges

Replace endpoint-distance-only adaptive decisions with a criterion combining:

  * view-derived point spacing;
  * model-space chord deviation at midpoint/extrema;
  * tangent-angle change; and
  * mandatory subdivision at curve knots and discontinuities.

Always terminate with explicit depth/segment limits and report limit hits in
debug mode.  Evaluate points and tangents for success and finiteness before
adding vlist entries.

9.2 Surface indication

The default edge-only view is ambiguous for closed or sparsely bounded
surfaces.  Provide a surface indication independent of the ray SurfaceTree:

  * use trimmed adaptive isocurves for lightweight wireframe display; or
  * derive display lines from the existing CDT representation when a
    tessellated view is requested.

Do not special-case only ON_SumSurface and ON_RevSurface.  Any interior
isocurve must be clipped against trim loops using parameter-space topology and
must handle closed parameter seams.

9.3 Drawing acceptance

For pristine and perturbed Cobb variants verify:

  * finite points only;
  * all declared boundary edges represented;
  * no long spikes from failed evaluation or seam wrapping;
  * no missing patch at a pole or closed surface seam;
  * stable output under equivalent UV domain scaling; and
  * monotonic refinement as view/model tolerance tightens.

Image regression can supplement, but geometric vlist invariants should be the
primary automated tests.


10. IMPLEMENTATION WORK PACKAGES AND PROMOTION GATES
----------------------------------------------------

Execution should use parallelism at boundaries that do not change solver
semantics.  Build independent translation units with the build system's
parallel mode.  Run deterministic focused executables or modes for interval
arithmetic, fold proofs, affine grazing, Crofton, Cobb/seams, and directed
partitions as separate processes under CTest `-j`.  The current
`--directed-only`, `--crofton-only`, `--seam-only`, `--interval-only`,
`--fold-only`, and `--affine-only` modes are registered as separate CTest
entries and pass when run concurrently.  The no-argument executable retains
the complete corpus.  This prevents the exhaustive interval oracle, which
dominates sanitizer wall time, from serializing short correctness gates.  On
the current host the first split reduced isolated optimized wall time from
about 238 seconds to about 136 seconds.  Further arithmetic-matrix sharding is
lower priority than establishing correct production root and event semantics.

OpenNURBS/model construction merely to accelerate validation.  That requires

assignment, callback state, and deterministic telemetry.  The allocation-free
local proof should give identical results independent of scheduling before a
shared-process parallel benchmark is meaningful.  Existing face-parallel prep

11. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
-----------------------

Correctness:

  * A selected prepared ray accounts for every potentially intersected
    face/span and every terminal subdivision box as excluded or completely
    resolved.  No unresolved/capacity/unsupported state is converted to a
    miss, and no partial prepared partition is mixed with fallback hits.

  * Each published endpoint is owned by a certified physical ENTER or LEAVE
    event with preserved face, UV, orientation, topology, and uncertainty
    provenance.  Hit parity and cleanup pass order have no publication
    authority.

  * Implicit and BREP ordered partitions agree for all non-ambiguous directed
    analytic rays within rti_tol.dist.

  * Original CSG regions and their converted BREPs have no unexplained paired
    partition difference on the shared deterministic Crofton line set outside
    explicit numeric/tangent uncertainty bands.

  * Later-stage topology-identical implicit-leaf and BREP-leaf CSG hierarchies
    agree on ordered partitions.  Fully reconstructed BREP-to-CSG NIST cases
    likewise agree; mixed reconstructions report their residual BREP content
    and are not claimed as fully independent oracles.

  * Paired Crofton crossing and chord differences converge without systematic
    bias, and independent surface-area and volume estimates agree with
    analytic values wherever such formulas are available.

  * Exact sphere pole shots succeed deterministically from both directions.

  * Tangent contacts do not create material intervals; nearby two-root cases
    remain distinct when their interval is meaningful at BRL-CAD tolerance.

  * In the signed grazing sweep, raw root separation follows the analytic
    trend from two roots through a double contact to no roots.  Final librt
    classification matches the analytic oracle and has at most one contiguous
    tolerance-controlled transition band, with no odd-hit or alternating
    hit/miss islands.  The implicit primitive agrees outside its measured
    floating-point contact uncertainty; exact transformed-discriminant
    artifacts are reported rather than used as ground truth.

  * Sewn Cobb seam/vertex rays do not leak for g <= T.

  * g > T never triggers silent closure.

  * Equivalent UV reparameterizations do not change hit classification.

  * Nonadjacent faces are never joined based only on proximity.

  * Plate-mode behavior remains separately correct.

  * Crofton area and volume converge to analytic and paired implicit values
    within measured statistical confidence, with no increasing-sample bias.

Robustness:

  * All surface/derivative/normal failures become structured outcomes.

  * No NaN or infinity reaches a hit, segment, vlist, bbox, or serialized
    cache.

  * Workspace overflow, unsupported order/weights, non-manifold topology, and
    excessive seam discrepancy use an explicit fallback or diagnostic.

  * Parallel prep and shots are repeatable and sanitizer-clean.

Performance:

  * The new shot fast path performs no general heap allocation.

  * Prepared BBNode/trim-list counts and serialized cache size fall
    substantially when density is reduced.

  * FRAME.g prep wall time and peak RSS improve materially from the recorded
    approximately 33.4 seconds and 3.05 GB baseline.

  * Total render performance, including local refinement, is no worse on
    ordinary rays and is measured separately for deliberately grazing rays.

  * Fallback frequency and workspace high-water marks are low and stable on a
    representative corpus subset before the new path becomes default.


12. RISKS AND REQUIRED SAFEGUARDS
---------------------------------

False exclusion in local bounds
  This is the most serious algorithmic risk.  Use Bernstein/projective bounds
  only when their preconditions hold.  Unsafe weights or arithmetic states
  fall back.  Tests must contain high-degree and extreme-weight surfaces.

Tolerance inflation
  Automatically using measured error as permission can heal arbitrarily bad
  geometry.  Only ON_UNSET_VALUE authorizes inferred topology tolerance;
  explicitly set values remain authoritative.  Always report large envelopes.

UV singularities
  Inverse surface metrics can explode at a pole.  UV is only a broad phase;
  final proximity is in 3D.  Use one-sided evaluation and bounded queries.

Close roots versus crack observations
  Never merge solely by t distance.  Require topology, isolated-root, and
  local-state evidence.  Preserve ambiguity until solid-event resolution.

Fixed workspace
  Fixed capacity must not imply fixed correctness.  Every overflow is an
  observable fallback.  Corpus high-water metrics determine later tuning.

Legacy invalid geometry
  The current heuristics accommodate unsolid geometry.  Do not remove the old
  path until fallback telemetry identifies what real corpus cases still need
  it.  Do not let those cases weaken the well-defined solid path.

Cache compatibility
  Version all new serialized data.  Reject incomplete or old records and
  rebuild.  Exercise repeated load/free cycles under memory checking.

Crofton interpretation
  Dense sampling cannot repair a systematic raytracing bias or a
  representation-dependent sub-tolerance sliver.  Always inspect paired ray
  differences and block variance, not only the final scalar estimate.

14. RECOMMENDED FIRST CONCRETE CHANGE SET
-----------------------------------------

The first implementation change should deliberately avoid changing tree
density or seam semantics.  It should contain only:

  1. Prepared ownership repair and cache-deserialization ownership.

  2. SurfaceTree root/CurveTree transfer with immediate destruction of the
     temporary builder and its SurfaceSplitCache.

  3. Removal of unused m_face_user.p storage.

  4. Structured solver outcomes, initial-residual success, deterministic
     singular handling, finite checks, and safe normal evaluation.

  5. Directed sphere pole, boundary, seam, and reverse-ray tests.

  6. Baseline counters needed to compare later phases.

This produces an independently reviewable correctness and memory-management
improvement.  The Cobb seam framework and tolerance resolver should be the
next change set, followed by local Bezier isolation.  Only then should the
default surface-tree depth be reduced.


15. STATUS-2 SURFACES AND LOCAL GRAZING REFINEMENT CHECKPOINT
------------------------------------------------------------

The rational torus work identified two production requirements which were not
visible in the initial sphere-only stages.

OpenNURBS NURBS-form status

  ON_Surface::GetNurbForm does not return a Boolean.  Status 1 means the NURBS
  form has the original parameterization; status 2 means it has the same
  geometric locus with a parameter map.  ON_RevSurface commonly returns 2.
  Rejecting every value except 1 omits ordinary analytic surfaces produced by
  STEP conversion, not merely exotic representations.

  Prepared face records therefore retain the exact status.  Coefficient
  construction, subdivision boxes, Krawczyk proofs, determinant proofs, and
  root ownership remain in the returned NURBS parameter space.  Before using
  a root with the original BREP topology, map it with
  GetSurfaceParameterFromNurbFormParameter and require:

    * a finite mapped parameter;
    * a successful inverse
      GetNurbFormParameterFromSurfaceParameter round trip;
    * round-trip agreement at a scale-aware floating-point bound; and
    * agreement between the original-surface point and certified NURBS point
      at the numeric model-distance bound.

  Evaluate the original surface normal and trim classifier only after that
  point check.  Publish original-surface UV, never the private NURBS-form UV.
  Apply the same 3D locus-agreement check to regular corrector roots and
  singular-fold representatives; a successful parameter round trip alone is
  not evidence that the two evaluated points agree at raytracing precision.
  This is a point-map theorem, not an interval-map theorem.  Status-2 seam,
  edge, vertex, or near-trim events must continue to fail closed until an
  enclosure of the parameter map proves their complete source-box and trim
  corridor relationships.

  `brep-audit --surface-trees-only` now reports each integer status and object
  totals.  Product_step46_item.s in non_grabcad/0353/0353-10-12.g has 175
  faces: 33 status-1 and 142 status-2.  Its dense trees have 98,758 leaves and
  individual 4,096-leaf faces.  This concrete sample makes status-2 support
  central to the STEP corpus: the old equality-to-1 check omitted 81.1 percent
  of those faces from the prepared solver.

Bounded exact local refinement

  At a tube-direction torus chord only 2T long, depth-24 isolation retained
  three boxes.  Two allocation-free local correctors found the analytic
  endpoints, and expansion Krawczyk proved two local root neighborhoods, but
  the ordinary isolation net represented every coefficient with the worst
  error of its equation.  This error floor kept a rootless box and a box
  spanning the nearby fold neighborhood.  The dense legacy SurfaceTree path
  visited 24 leaves yet returned only one physical root in either ray
  direction.

  Reuse the exact per-coefficient expansion intervals already built for the
  equation coefficients.  The first production gate enables this tier only
  for status-2 faces and only permits its clean interior regular/fold results
  to publish.  This preserves the already-qualified status-1 seam, edge, and
  vertex source-box transactions until dedicated repartition-invariance tests
  exist for them.  When ordinary isolation reaches its depth budget:

    1. Restrict every interval Bernstein coefficient to the terminal box with
       exact dyadic blossoms and outward expansion bounds.

    2. Apply both individual equation hull exclusion and an invertible
       conditioned two-equation transform.  Form every transformed coefficient
       with error-free expansions before testing its Bernstein hull.  A
       transformed hull strictly separated from zero excludes the box without
       assuming that either original equation is separately signed.

    3. Try the tight expansion-valued Krawczyk inclusion and require a strict
       determinant sign before treating a root as a regular crossing.

    4. If the exact rotated hull retains an unresolved box, split only that
       box.  Use the existing fixed 64-entry stack, at most eight extra levels
       per box, and at most 64 exact refinements over the ray.  Exhaustion is
       explicit fallback; it is never permission to publish.

    5. If a regular proof appears but its outward ray-parameter interval is
       still wider than 0.01*T/|D|, continue the same bounded local refinement.
       This makes event separation, rather than mere point convergence, the
       termination objective.  Reprove both children; do not assume a parent
       root belongs to one child based on the floating candidate alone.

  Exact coefficient-hull recovery for an unresolved box runs only after the
  old ordinary depth budget.  The event-separation rule in step 5 may begin
  earlier when ordinary Krawczyk proves uniqueness before that depth but leaves
  a ray interval wider than 0.01T.  Both cases use the same fixed refinement
  count and stack and allocate no memory.  They do not change the prepared
  SurfaceTree or its serialization.  The 2T torus case uses the rotated hull
  to remove the false retained box.  At 1.1T, further local splits isolate two
  signed regular boxes tightly enough that the outward event gap is strictly
  greater than T.  Both ray directions now publish the analytic interval at
  100T, 10T, 2T, and 1.1T.

Status-2 similarity qualification

  Pointwise NURBS-form mapping and close-root separation must be invariant
  under the same similarities already required of status-1 sphere and seam
  logic.  A focused torus matrix therefore applies arbitrary rotation and
  translation at scales 1, 0.01, and 10,000, scaling T with the geometry.  It
  shoots the exact-tier tube-section cases at 2T and 1.1T in both directions.
  Every one of the 12 rays must:

    * observe exactly one status-2 face;
    * select a complete two-event prepared material interval;
    * match the analytic circular section within T;
    * exercise exact expansion availability, exclusion, and refinement; and
    * avoid arithmetic failure, fixed-workspace exhaustion, or fallback.

  This matrix passes.  It also clarifies the fold-localization contract.  The
  localization routine tries many nested optional contractions and records a
  failure for each level that cannot improve the box.  A failed optional level
  does not invalidate a complete fold proof.  Mixed fold/regular publication
  consumes the actual stored fold interval: the best certified contraction if
  one exists, otherwise the complete parent interval.  Its strict outward-gap
  classifier then proves or rejects separation from the regular box.  Do not
  use a zero localization-failure counter as a substitute for that interval
  proof.

  The large transformed implicit TOR returns two quartic segments on all four
  positive analytic section rays in this matrix.  Record those as oracle
  artifacts and continue to use the analytic circle for the grazing boundary;
  transform invariance must not train the BREP solver to reproduce them.

Minimum-size boundary behavior

  Use three categories, not a binary hit/miss expectation:

    * gap.minimum > T.maximum: RESOLVED material interval;
    * gap.maximum < T.minimum: SUBMINIMUM contact/nonmaterial; and
    * overlapping/equal bounds: AMBIGUOUS whole-ray fallback.

  Equality at exactly 1T must not be called resolved.  The status-2 torus
  stream marks it ambiguous.  The unchanged status-1 sphere path reaches its
  prior UNCERTIFIED fallback before the new tier.  Legacy fallback sometimes
  returns a tolerance-sized segment and sometimes a miss, so the ratchet
  records that result without claiming a resolved certificate.  Below T the
  prepared stream may find two surface roots, but it must not publish a
  material interval.

  Exact tangency likewise may produce one or more contact candidates but must
  not receive a simple-root material certificate.  Analytic outside lines
  must not publish BREP material even if a candidate corrector lands within a
  sub-tolerance numerical neighborhood.

Oracle qualification

  The implicit TOR quartic currently emits a small segment on both exact
  tangent directions and on 16 of 18 directed analytic outside samples for
  each tested normal section.  Therefore use the analytic circle at the
  grazing boundary.  Keep the implicit TOR as a positive-clearance endpoint
  comparison and record its tangent/outside artifacts as a separate solver
  defect.  Do not train BREP acceptance to reproduce that defect.

Rejected proof path

  Joining the opposite root box to a connected rootless source component did
  prove complete component ownership, but ordinary and expansion Krawczyk both
  rejected the union because it contained the singular fold neighborhood.
  That is the correct mathematical result.  The experimental publication path
  had no positive controlled case and was removed rather than retained as OBE
  production work.  Candidate roots still never substitute for complete box
  coverage.

Current deterministic gate

  `rt_brep_ray --grazing-root-only` covers sphere plus both torus normal
  sections, both directions, nine signed chord scales, exact tangency, and
  mirrored outside clearances.  For each torus section, all eight directed
  100T/10T/2T/1.1T cases must select the prepared production stream and publish
  one complete material interval.  The four tube-section 2T/1.1T cases must
  also record exact expansion availability, a rigorous exclusion, and bounded
  local refinement with no arithmetic failure or budget exhaustion.  This
  prevents a later legacy solver success from concealing loss of the new
  production theorem.  The interval, fold, and local-root partitions
  independently exercise the expansion arithmetic and proof primitives.  Keep
  these gates green before changing the base depth, exact-refinement budgets,
  trim policy, or SurfaceTree fallback.
2026-08-09 IMPLEMENTATION NOTE: DEGENERATE SURFACE POLES

This design note records the observation that led to the implemented
"CERTIFIED COLLAPSED POLE EVENTS" checkpoint near the start of this document.
The checkpoint states the narrower final authority and validation results.

Controlled SurfaceTree depths show that sphere-pole behavior is not monotonic:
the directed suite fails at depth two, passes at three, returns invalid pole
normals at four, misses the large translated pole at five and six, and passes
at seven.  The failing traces expose the same geometric pole as a continuum of
UV roots along a collapsed parameter boundary.  Tree density merely changes
which UV representatives and ill-conditioned derivative normals survive.

Treat a smooth collapsed boundary as one geometric event, not as many ordinary
surface roots.  For a ray through the pole, both homogeneous ray-plane
Bernstein residuals have the collapsed-boundary factor.  A proposed certified
path is to remove that known factor, use the existing outward interval and
rotated-hull machinery to exclude every distinct interior root on each
touching span, and remove the corresponding factor from the residual
Jacobian determinant to certify a constant crossing orientation.  Publication
requires complete ownership of all spans forming the singular fiber,
consistent pole point and oriented sign, a scale-aware line-to-pole roundoff
or modeling-tolerance qualification, and a valid alternating solid event
stream.  Ambiguous singularities, inconsistent limiting orientation, or an
unexcluded deflated root fail closed to the existing path.  This construction
avoids arbitrary UV normal selection, dynamic allocation, and dependence on
SurfaceTree depth.  A focused C/C++ pole corpus must ratchet base, reversed,
inside-origin, small translated/scaled, and large translated/scaled sphere
cases before this theorem is generalized.
