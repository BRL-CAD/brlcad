=================================================================

Status: Detailed engineering proposal
Study date: 2026-08-07
Repository studied: /home/cyapp/brlcad

1. PURPOSE AND SUCCESS CRITERIA
-------------------------------

BRL-CAD's BREP primitive must interpret a trimmed NURBS boundary as the
boundary of the solid the modeler intended.  This is subtly different from
reporting every mathematical intersection with each underlying surface.
Imported patches and their trims may disagree by small amounts, adjacent
surface parameterizations may have radically different scales, and a ray may
pass through the uncertainty region at a seam.  A mathematically literal
per-face answer can therefore leak through a nominally closed solid.

At the same time, the raytracer must not bridge unrelated geometry or merge
nearby, legitimate roots.  Near-tangent rays are particularly difficult:
several roots can be close in model space, ordinary Newton iteration becomes
ill-conditioned at a tangent, and a coarse starting guess can converge to the
wrong root or to no root.  The current response has been to build very dense
surface trees.  That improves the guesses but makes prep extremely expensive
in time and memory.

The target result has all of the following properties:

  * Correct solid partitions for well-formed BREPs, including rays through
    poles, surface seams, trim boundaries, vertices, and near-tangent regions.

  * Tolerance-aware closure of cracks between topologically adjacent faces.
    A sub-tolerance disagreement must not turn an intended solid ray into a
    miss.  Geometry beyond the authorized tolerance must not be silently
    healed.

  * Explicit separation of numerical convergence tolerance, UV trim
    uncertainty, model-space topology tolerance, ray-distance clustering,
    and BRL-CAD's minimum meaningful segment thickness.

  * A much smaller prepared acceleration structure.  Difficult regions are
    refined locally during a shot, using bounded, allocation-free workspace.

  * Deterministic behavior independent of thread scheduling.  Prep and shot
    must not use stale surface-specific data or random perturbations.

  * Measurable correctness against analytic implicit solids before relying on
    converted STEP geometry whose true answer is not independently known.

  * Original CSG raytracing is the primary behavioral oracle for every
    implicit-solid-to-BREP conversion.  Identical rays must produce identical
    ordered material intervals, and identical Cauchy-Crofton line samples must
    converge to the same crossing and chord statistics.  This paired test is
    the strongest promotion gate for the NURBS BREP solver, not an optional
    aggregate smoke test.

  * Predictable limiting behavior as a secant ray approaches a one-point
    graze.  The two roots must approach each other continuously, remain
    resolvable while their interval is meaningful at BRL-CAD tolerance, become
    one zero-transition contact at exact tangency, and become a miss outside
    the surface.  There must be no alternating hit/miss bands or odd-hit leaks
    near the limit.

  * Drawing behavior that does not depend on constructing the raytracer's
    dense surface tree and that remains useful for trimmed, closed, and highly
    curved surfaces.

The implementation should be staged.  The current dense-tree route remains a
correctness oracle and bounded fallback until the replacement passes directed
ray, Cauchy-Crofton, and corpus tests.


2. CURRENT IMPLEMENTATION: OBSERVATIONS AND IMPLICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Surface-tree construction and the recent split-cache change

The relevant change is commit:

  23eabb2c056fc7d0c4ccc6c5d9299f72eedbb432
  "Add libbrep changes from STEP work"

The old SurfaceTree::hasSplit implementation kept file-static surface
pointers, domains, span counts, and allocated span vectors indexed by
bu_parallel_id().  This was unsafe for two independent reasons documented in
the new code:

  * bu_parallel_id() is not a unique identity for arbitrary C++ worker
    threads used by STEP conversion.

  * A cached ON_Surface pointer survived destruction of its surface.  If the
    allocator later reused that address, stale knot data could be selected
    for a different surface.

The new implementation puts a SurfaceSplitCache on each SurfaceTree.  It owns
the two surface domains and std::vector span arrays and dies with the tree.
This is the correct ownership boundary for construction and must not be
replaced with the old static scheme.  The useful optimization is instead to
make SurfaceTree a temporary builder and release the split cache immediately
after its prepared products are transferred to brep_specific.

Current source locations:

  * include/brep/surfacetree.h
  * src/libbrep/opennurbs_ext.cpp, SurfaceSplitCache,
    SurfaceTree::SurfaceTree, SurfaceTree::~SurfaceTree, and
    SurfaceTree::hasSplit
  * include/brep/defines.h, BREP_MAX_FT_DEPTH (currently 8)

SurfaceTree subdivides at surface knots and according to sampled flatness and
straightness.  A maximum depth of eight can produce a large number of BBNodes
per face.  CurveTree data and per-leaf trim lists add additional memory.  In
particular, BBNode::Stl contains a std::vector of children and a std::list of
trim nodes above the leaf.  Dense subdivision magnifies the cost of all of
these structures, not just bounding boxes.

2.2 Prepared-object ownership problems

brep_build_bvh constructs one SurfaceTree per face in parallel.  It adds each
SurfaceTree root as a child of the top-level BVH, stores the SurfaceTree in
ON_BrepFace::m_face_user.p, frees only the temporary pointer array, and
contains the explicit comment that the SurfaceTrees are never destroyed.

That leak currently masks an ownership conflict:

  * SurfaceTree::~SurfaceTree deletes both m_root and m_ctree.

  * The top-level BVH also treats the transferred root as its child and
    recursively deletes BBNodes.

  * BBNodes refer to the CurveTree but do not own it.

  * brep_specific_delete deletes only the ON_Brep and top-level BVH.

  * Cache deserialization constructs one CurveTree per face and does not
    retain an owner for those trees.

No reader of m_face_user.p was found in the repository.  It should not be
used as an implicit ownership mechanism.

The fix should make brep_specific a C++ owner of:

  * the prepared ON_Brep;
  * the top-level BVH and its recursively owned BBNode roots;
  * one CurveTree per prepared face;
  * immutable Bezier-span and seam metadata proposed below; and
  * any diagnostic/prep statistics retained for reporting.

SurfaceTree should expose explicit transfer/release operations, or be
replaced internally by a builder-result object that transfers its root and
CurveTree exactly once.  The temporary builder and its SurfaceSplitCache can
then be destroyed.  Deserialization must populate the same owner containers
as ordinary prep.  Delete the BVH before deleting CurveTrees so no BBNode can
observe an already destroyed tree during teardown.

The prep cache in rt_brep_prep_serialize is currently version 0.  It writes a
CurveTree followed by a BBNode tree for every face.  Any new span, tolerance,
or adjacency data requires a version bump.  Old cache entries must be rejected
and rebuilt, not partially interpreted.

2.4 Current ray/surface solve

rt_brep_shot asks the master BBNode hierarchy for every intersected leaf using
std::list, then calls utah_brep_intersect for each leaf.  The intersection
solver represents the ray as the intersection of two planes perpendicular to
the ray.  For a surface S(u,v), it solves:

  f(u,v) = (S(u,v) - O) dot P1 = 0
  g(u,v) = (S(u,v) - O) dot P2 = 0

where O is the ray origin and P1/P2 span the plane normal to the ray.  The
Jacobian is:

          [ Su dot P1   Sv dot P1 ]
      J = [ Su dot P2   Sv dot P2 ]

The code explicitly inverts the 2x2 determinant.  It normally starts from the
leaf midpoint.  If the leaf midpoint normal has |normal dot ray| < 0.2, it
also tries the four corners.  It can return at most five roots from a leaf.

Important correctness and robustness issues are visible in the current code:

  * It evaluates the initial residual but does not accept an already
    converged seed before testing the Jacobian determinant.  An exact root at
    a surface singularity can therefore be rejected before success is
    considered.

  * The determinant "jitter" at a singular initial seed is
    uv += 0.1 * random * (uv0 - uv).  At the first iteration uv == uv0, so the
    perturbation is exactly zero.  It can repeat for all 100 iterations.

  * drand48 makes the solve nondeterministic and has undesirable cross-platform
    and threading properties.

  * The return value of Ev1Der is not checked.  Intermediate values and
    normals are not checked for finiteness, and the result of normal Unitize
    is not validated.

  * A fixed absolute determinant threshold of 1e-6 is not scale- or
    parameterization-invariant.

  * UV is clamped back into the leaf after every step.  Clamping can turn
    divergence into a stalled boundary point and obscure a root in the
    neighboring leaf.

  * The first-step corner direction rejection is heuristic and can discard a
    valid path on a curved or badly parameterized patch.

  * Step damping is limited to three halvings, followed by four non-improving
    iterations, for no geometric reason.

  * Duplicate detection is first performed in UV.  The same model-space root
    can have different UV coordinates on a seam, and similar UV coordinates
    do not imply similar model-space points under arbitrary parameterization.

  * A fixed five-root array assumes the dense leaves have isolated the
    surface sufficiently.  It is not a general root-count guarantee.

  * A fixed ROOT_TOL of 1e-7, BREP_INTERSECTION_ROOT_EPSILON of 1e-6,
    BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE of 5e-3, BREP_SAME_POINT_TOLERANCE of 1e-6,
    and BREP_GRAZING_DOT_TOL of 1.7453e-5 mix several unrelated meanings.

The reported sphere-pole failure is therefore plausible even when a particular
prepared sphere happens to succeed because another leaf seed approaches the
pole.  An axial test of the current local build did produce the expected
two-unit interval for one implicit-to-BREP sphere, but that does not remove
the exact-seed bug above.  It means only that the current subdivision and seed
set happened to route around it in that case.

libbrep already contains useful singularity-aware routines:

  * surface_EvNormal in include/brep/util.h and PullbackCurve.cpp
  * IsAtSingularity in include/brep/pullback.h and PullbackCurve.cpp

surface_EvNormal attempts one-sided normal evaluation at singular boundaries.
The ray solver should reuse or generalize this behavior instead of building a
normal solely as Unitize(CrossProduct(Su,Sv)).

2.5 Current trim and solid-hit repair

After Newton returns a UV point, utah_brep_intersect calls
BBNode::isTrimmed(..., BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE).  It creates:

  * CLEAN_HIT for an interior point away from a trim;
  * NEAR_HIT for an accepted point close to a trim; and
  * NEAR_MISS for a trimmed-away point close to a trim.

The associated BRNode supplies an adjacent face index when CurveTree prep can
identify one.  rt_brep_shot then runs several ordered heuristic passes:

  * remove near misses next to same-direction accepted hits;
  * turn adjacent-face pairs of same-direction near misses into CRACK_HIT;
  * remove opposite-direction near-miss pairs;
  * collapse same-direction clean hits on adjacent faces;
  * remove near misses from the ends of an odd list;
  * perform similar collapsing for NEAR_HIT;
  * discard trimmed, out-of-bounds, and grazing candidates;
  * merge same-distance candidates, deleting both if their normal signs
    disagree;
  * remove consecutive entries or exits according to an assumed sign; and
  * discard a final hit in some remaining odd-list cases.

The final solid path emits segments only when the remaining hit count is even.
This logic encodes valuable experience about real cracks, but it loses the
geometric evidence needed to make principled decisions.  It also depends on
pass order, assumes the first surviving sign identifies entry, and removes all
grazing hits before determining whether a close pair represents a thin but
valid interval.

The replacement should preserve near-trim candidates and their uncertainty,
then resolve them as local topological events.  It should not discard them
early or infer solid state from hit-count parity alone.

2.6 Tolerance-unit conflation

include/brep/defines.h describes BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE as critical to solid
raytracing and trimming and notes that it may need model-scale adaptation.  It
is currently 0.005.  The same number is used in contexts with incompatible
units:

  * as a distance from a trim in surface parameter space; and
  * as a model-space amount used to grow surface-leaf bounding boxes.

This cannot be invariant under UV rescaling or distorted parameterizations.
A surface domain of [0,1]x[0,1] and an equivalent domain of
[0,1000000]x[0,0.000001] must not get different crack behavior.

OpenNURBS already distinguishes topology tolerances:

  * ON_BrepVertex::m_tolerance is a model-space bound between the vertex and
    applicable edge or lifted trim endpoints.

  * ON_BrepEdge::m_tolerance is the maximum model-space distance from the 3D
    edge curve to the surface of any face using that edge.

  * ON_BrepTrim::m_tolerance[0] and [1] are u- and v-parameter accuracy of the
    2D trim relative to a closest surface parameter corresponding to the edge.

ON_UNSET_VALUE means the value needs to be computed; zero has a specific
near-machine-tolerance meaning.  These values must be validated and used in
their native units.

BRL-CAD's default rti_tol.dist is 0.0005 mm (BN_TOL_DIST).  rt_boolfinal
forces intervals whose entry and exit differ by this amount to zero thickness
and fuses nearby partitions.  include/rt/func.h contains an extensive Crofton
discussion showing that sub-tolerance slivers can appear or disappear by ray
angle.  BREP shot semantics must be designed with this downstream behavior in
mind; otherwise BREP may manufacture distinctions that librt later erases or
may inconsistently preserve them only at oblique angles.

2.7 Current drawing behavior

The ordinary rt_brep_plot path is primarily an edge plot.  It uses
ON_Curve_PolyLine_Approx for edges and adds special surface-tree isocurves only
for selected closed ON_SumSurface and ON_RevSurface cases.  The adaptive plot
path uses view-derived point spacing, but its edge subdivision is driven
mostly by endpoint distance rather than a complete chord-error/curvature test.

The shaded/tessellated path is separate and uses brep_facecdt_plot.  Drawing
should remain separate from the ray acceleration structure.  Requiring a
dense ray SurfaceTree to obtain a usable visual would preserve the prep cost
we are trying to remove.


3. REQUIRED SEMANTICS: FIVE DIFFERENT TOLERANCE JOBS
----------------------------------------------------

The implementation should introduce an internal BrepTolerancePolicy prepared
from rti_tol, BREP topology metadata, local surface metrics, and ray scale.
The following quantities must remain separate even if some share a numerical
lower bound.

3.1 Numerical solve tolerance

This answers: "Is the candidate sufficiently close to the mathematical ray
and surface to be a usable geometric observation?"

For the two-plane formulation use the model-space line residual:

  residual_line = hypot(f, g)

when P1 and P2 are orthonormal.  This has model-space units.  Accepting a root
also requires a suitably small model-space change in S, or a parameter change
interpreted through the local first fundamental form:

  G = [ Su.Su  Su.Sv ]
      [ Su.Sv  Sv.Sv ]

  step_model^2 = delta_uv^T G delta_uv

Numerical tolerance should be a conservative fraction of the geometric
acceptance tolerance, with a floor based on floating-point scale.  It must not
be enlarged merely to heal a seam.  Solving and seam healing are separate
operations.

3.2 UV trim-query tolerance

This answers: "Which trim segments might be relevant to this UV candidate?"
It is a broad-phase parameter-space envelope, not the final crack decision.

Use ON_BrepTrim::m_tolerance[0/1] when set.  Add a local conversion of the
model-space uncertainty through the surface Jacobian.  The natural shape is a
metric ellipse delta_uv^T G delta_uv <= T^2; an axis-aligned UV rectangle may
conservatively bound that ellipse for CurveTree lookup.  Near singular G, do
not permit the inverse metric to expand without bound.  Query a bounded UV
region and make the final decision by closest distance in 3D to the lifted
trim or edge.

3.3 Model-space seam envelope

This answers: "How far may two topologically mated representations disagree
while still representing one intended boundary?"

For each topological edge prepare an authorized envelope from:

  * rti_tol.dist as the BRL-CAD minimum model-space scale;
  * a valid ON_BrepEdge::m_tolerance;
  * valid endpoint ON_BrepVertex tolerances; and
  * the 3D lift of valid trim parameter tolerances where needed.

If a tolerance is ON_UNSET_VALUE, compute it with OpenNURBS facilities or an
adaptive edge/trim comparison during prep and mark it as inferred.  Do not
treat an explicitly set zero tolerance as missing.

Also measure the actual discrepancy g between the shared 3D edge curve and
each adjacent lifted trim/surface boundary.  The measured discrepancy is
evidence, not permission: a set tolerance authorizes closure only when g is
within it.  An inferred tolerance may be based on g because OpenNURBS
explicitly says an unset value needs computation.  Record unexpectedly large
declared or inferred envelopes for diagnostics.

Only faces joined by the same topological edge, or the two sides of a declared
surface seam, are eligible for automatic solid closure.  Geometric proximity
alone must not join unrelated faces.  Non-manifold edges, edges with missing
adjacency, and discrepancies beyond the envelope are unresolved and use the
fallback during the staged rollout.

3.4 Ray-parameter clustering tolerance

This answers: "Could two candidate observations describe the same uncertain
physical event along this ray?"

Each candidate carries a ray-parameter interval, not just one scalar t.  For
ray direction D and model-space uncertainty T, the base conversion is:

  delta_t = T / |D|

with additional solver/bounding uncertainty included.  Candidates form a
cluster when their intervals overlap and their topology/model-space evidence
is compatible.  Do not cluster solely with BREP_SAME_POINT_TOLERANCE and do
not assume ray directions are normalized internally.

3.5 Minimum valid solid interval

This answers: "Is a resulting material interval meaningful to BRL-CAD's
Boolean evaluator?"

rti_tol.dist is authoritative.  BREP must preserve genuinely separate roots
until solid assembly, then handle intervals consistently with rt_boolweave and
rt_boolfinal.  A tangent contact produces no transition.  An interval below
the minimum solid thickness is represented according to the same zero-
thickness/fusion rules used downstream, not discarded earlier because its
normal happened to satisfy a fixed grazing threshold.


4. PROPOSED PREPARED DATA MODEL
-------------------------------

4.1 BrepPreparedFace

For every face retain:

  * face and surface indices;
  * an owned CurveTree;
  * a coarse BBNode hierarchy;
  * a list of immutable rational Bezier spans covering the surface;
  * mapping from coarse leaves to candidate spans;
  * trim/edge adjacency identifiers;
  * face orientation; and
  * prep statistics such as nodes, leaves, spans, and measured errors.

Each Bezier span stores its surface domain, order, positive-weight status,
homogeneous control points or an immutable reference to packed prepared
control points, a conservative model-space bound, and references to trims
whose UV bounds overlap it.

For ordinary positive rational weights, the dehomogenized control points obey
the rational convex-hull property.  If weights are non-positive, cross zero,
or are otherwise unsafe for conservative rational bounds, mark the span for
the existing dense-tree fallback.  Correct fallback is preferable to a false
exclusion.  Support for projective interval bounds can be added later.

4.2 BrepPreparedEdge

For every edge retain:

  * edge and endpoint vertex indices;
  * the two trim and face indices for a manifold mated edge;
  * trim orientations and face reversal state;
  * edge parameter domains and maps to each trim;
  * declared and inferred topology tolerances;
  * measured maximum edge-to-lift discrepancy and where it occurs;
  * a conservative model-space seam envelope; and
  * flags for seam, singular, boundary, non-manifold, invalid, or excessive
    discrepancy states.

The maximum discrepancy must be conservatively estimated.  Begin with all
curve knots/endpoints and adaptive midpoint sampling.  Subdivide until the
curve/trim lift chord bound is below the measurement tolerance.  This work is
done at prep, not during shots.

4.3 BrepHitCandidate

Replace the information-poor brep_hit during solving with an internal record
containing at least:

  * face, surface, span, leaf, trim, edge, and adjacent-face indices;
  * UV point and a UV uncertainty region;
  * model-space point and normal when well-defined;
  * scalar t and [t_min,t_max] uncertainty interval;
  * line residual, last model-space step, and a conditioning measure;
  * signed model-space distance to the relevant trim/edge when available;
  * face orientation and provisional enter/leave/contact classification;
  * inside-trim, outside-trim-near-edge, clean-interior, singular, and
    out-of-domain flags; and
  * solver outcome and refinement provenance.

Do not throw away an outside-near-trim candidate.  It is essential evidence
when the neighboring face misses the ray because of a crack.

4.4 Structured solver outcomes

Use a result enum rather than a Boolean converged flag.  Required outcomes
include:

  CONVERGED_REGULAR
  CONVERGED_SINGULAR
  CONTACT_TANGENT
  OUTSIDE_TRIM
  OUTSIDE_DOMAIN
  NO_ROOT_BOUND_PROVEN
  JACOBIAN_SINGULAR
  STALLED
  DIVERGED
  NONFINITE_EVALUATION
  UNSUPPORTED_WEIGHTS
  WORKSPACE_EXHAUSTED
  FALLBACK_USED

Keep the best residual and candidate box even on failure so the local
refinement and diagnostics have useful evidence.


5. PROPOSED INTERSECTION ALGORITHM
----------------------------------

5.1 Broad phase

Keep a shallow surface/face hierarchy for rejecting rays far from a patch.
Initially preserve the existing depth-eight hierarchy while the new solver is
shadow-tested.  Once correctness gates pass, lower the default depth in
measured steps.

Add an allocation-free hierarchy traversal overload.  It should use a fixed
array stack and write leaf/span references into a fixed candidate array or
invoke a callback immediately.  The current std::list-based traversal and
std::list hit collection allocate during every shot and must not be part of
the final fast path.

If a fixed traversal or candidate array fills, set WORKSPACE_EXHAUSTED and
invoke the current path.  Never silently truncate candidates.

5.2 Conservative local Bezier exclusion

For each candidate rational Bezier span, choose orthonormal P1 and P2
perpendicular to D.  Express the rational surface in homogeneous coordinates
(X,Y,Z,W).  Roots of the ray-plane functions are roots of the polynomial
Bezier numerators:

  F1(u,v) = (XYZ(u,v) - O * W(u,v)) dot P1
  F2(u,v) = (XYZ(u,v) - O * W(u,v)) dot P2

The Bernstein coefficient ranges of F1 and F2 provide conservative exclusion:
if either coefficient hull is strictly positive or strictly negative beyond
the numerical interval error, the parameter box contains no ray intersection.

For boxes that cannot be excluded:

  1. Apply Bezier clipping when coefficient geometry yields a safe domain
     contraction.

  2. Otherwise split along the parameter direction with the larger predicted
     model-space extent or poorer bound.

  3. Use de Casteljau subdivision, preserving conservative coefficient bounds.

  4. Traverse near boxes first according to ray t range, but retain all boxes
     whose bounds may contain roots.

  5. Stop subdividing when the box is isolated in both ray t and model space,
     or when the safeguarded corrector has converged.

This supplies a root-isolation mechanism that does not rely on one Newton
basin.  It is especially important for two close grazing roots.

5.3 Safeguarded corrector

Use the current two-plane equations but replace explicit determinant inversion
with a conditioned 2x2 solve (scaled pivoting or a small QR/SVD-style solve).
The algorithm is:

  1. Evaluate position and derivatives and check the return status and all
     values for finiteness.

  2. Test line residual before forming the Jacobian.  This fixes exact roots
     at poles and other singular seeds.

  3. If the regular Jacobian is usable, compute a Newton step and apply a
     trust-region/line search that requires sufficient residual decrease.

  4. Interpret step size with the local surface metric, not raw UV magnitude.

  5. Do not clamp an arbitrary step to a leaf boundary.  Restrict it to the
     active isolated parameter box; a step that wants to leave is evidence for
     subdivision or a neighboring box.

  6. If the Jacobian is rank-deficient or the normal is singular, retain the
     converged positional candidate if appropriate and use one-sided
     surface_EvNormal/IsAtSingularity logic for a normal.  Otherwise return a
     singular outcome to subdivision.

  7. Use deterministic alternate seeds: box center, corners inset by a
     representable amount, edge midpoints, and the best point predicted by
     coefficient clipping.  Do not use randomness.

  8. Deduplicate in model space and overlapping t intervals, with topology
     awareness for distinct faces.  UV is never the sole identity test.

5.4 Tangencies and close roots

Do not classify a hit as disposable merely because |N dot D| is small.  That
quantity signals conditioning; it does not by itself determine the solid
event.

The local isolator must distinguish:

  * an even-multiplicity tangent contact, which has no inside/outside change;
  * two nearby simple roots enclosing a thin interval;
  * coincident observations of one edge/vertex crossing; and
  * a gap between intended adjacent patches.

Use the isolated t intervals and a local sign/winding test on either side of
the event.  If two root intervals remain inseparable at the model tolerance,
return one ambiguous cluster to the topology resolver rather than deleting
both based on normal signs.

5.5 Allocation-free ray-time workspace

The new fast path must not call malloc/new or grow STL containers while
shooting.  Initial compile-time bounds should be deliberately finite and
instrumented, for example:

  * supported direct-path surface order up to 16;
  * 64 pending subdivision boxes;
  * 64 raw root candidates; and
  * scratch for two or three maximum-order homogeneous control nets.

These are starting engineering limits, not correctness assumptions.  A span
above the supported order or a full array immediately uses the fallback.
Measure high-water marks on analytic tests and the STEP corpus before tuning
the capacities.

To avoid storing a full control net for every pending box, keep only parameter
subdomains and traversal state on the pending stack.  Use a bounded set of
scratch control nets to extract/subdivide the active subpatch from its
immutable prepared span.  Depth-first traversal bounds scratch memory.  The
extra computation occurs only in ambiguous regions and is preferable to a
large per-ray or per-leaf allocation.

Use BRL-CAD's normal resource-based segment allocation only when emitting the
final segments.  Add a debug/benchmark mode that counts all BREP-path dynamic
allocations after prep so the no-allocation requirement is testable.


6. TRIM, SEAM, AND SOLID-EVENT RESOLUTION
-----------------------------------------

6.1 Candidate classification

After solving the untrimmed surface, classify the candidate without losing
information:

  * CLEAN_INTERIOR: inside the face and outside every seam envelope.

  * INSIDE_NEAR_EDGE: accepted by the trim but within the local edge envelope.

  * OUTSIDE_NEAR_EDGE: rejected by the trim but close enough in 3D and UV to a
    specific eligible edge.

  * OUTSIDE_TRIM: rejected and not close enough to an eligible edge.

  * SINGULAR_BOUNDARY: a root on a declared surface singularity or degenerate
    trim side.

The final near-edge test uses distance to the lifted trim/shared 3D edge, not
only closesttrim in UV.

6.2 Event clustering

Sort candidates by t_min, then form clusters whose t intervals overlap.
Compatibility requires either the same face/root, the same topological edge,
or an explicitly declared same-surface seam.  Geometrically close candidates
from unrelated topology remain separate.

Within each cluster:

  * discard only candidates proven outside all relevant trims and envelopes;

  * combine duplicate observations of the same face root;

  * retain both sides of a shared-edge observation until local solid state is
    known; and

  * carry the widest justified uncertainty into the resolved event.

6.3 Manifold edge transition

For a normal manifold edge with two incident faces, build a local cross-section
perpendicular to the edge tangent.  The oriented incident face normals and
trim directions define the interior sector.  Evaluate the ray direction just
before and after the clustered event in this oriented 2D cross-section:

  * outside -> inside produces exactly one ENTER event;
  * inside -> outside produces exactly one LEAVE event;
  * unchanged state is a tangent/contact and produces no transition.

This symbolic local perturbation handles a ray exactly on the edge without
depending on which face Newton happened to find.  It also works for convex
and concave manifold edges.  Vertex clusters use the cyclic fan of incident
faces in the same spirit; unsupported/non-manifold fans fall back initially.

6.4 Crack closure

When one or both adjacent lifted surfaces miss the mathematical ray, an
OUTSIDE_NEAR_EDGE candidate may still establish an intended boundary event.
Create a closure transition only if:

  * the evidence refers to the same mated topological edge;

  * the candidate-to-edge and measured face/edge discrepancies are inside the
    authorized model-space envelope;

  * the local manifold sector changes solid state across the event; and

  * no clean incompatible root occupies the same interval.

The reported representative t should be the best-conditioned projection onto
the shared edge/local boundary, with an uncertainty interval covering both
adjacent representations.  Do not edit or snap the BREP itself during a shot.

An overlap uses the same resolver: several same-direction face observations
of one manifold event collapse to one oriented transition.  Opposite
transitions at one t become a contact only when the local sector test confirms
no state change.

6.5 Solid assembly

Process resolved events in t order with oriented winding/state, assuming the
line begins outside at negative infinity.  ENTER increments the local solid
state, LEAVE decrements it, and CONTACT changes nothing.  Emit segments over
the intervals in which the state is inside.

This replaces "the first sign is entering" and even-hit-count repair.  A final
nonzero state, a negative state, an unresolved non-manifold cluster, or
incompatible interval ordering is a structured failure and invokes the old
path during rollout.

Before segment emission, apply the minimum-valid-interval rule consistently
with rti_tol.dist and rt_boolfinal.  Preserve the original face/UV/normal data
selected for segment endpoints so rt_brep_norm, curvature, UV reporting, and
shading remain meaningful.

6.6 Plate mode and invalid topology

Plate mode is a distinct contract.  A valid but non-solid BREP currently
defaults to zero-thickness plate mode, and each accepted surface hit becomes a
plate interval.  Do not apply solid crack closure or winding assembly to such
objects unless plate semantics explicitly request it.

Keep the legacy unsewn Cobb sphere as a plate-mode regression.  The new sewn
Cobb fixture described below is the solid crack oracle.  Invalid and
non-manifold BREPs should produce diagnostics and use the conservative legacy
behavior until a separate policy is specified.


7. COBB-SPHERE CONTROLLED TEST FRAMEWORK
----------------------------------------

7.1 Why the Cobb sphere is useful

src/proc-db/brep_cobb.cpp implements J. E. Cobb's rational Bezier sphere
tiling.  It creates six 5x5 rational Bezier surfaces, rotated into cube-face
positions.  It is ideal for this work because:

  * the underlying analytic oracle is a sphere;
  * it has twelve long patch seams and eight three-face vertices;
  * every patch is non-planar;
  * seam and vertex rays can be constructed exactly; and
  * homogeneous control points can be perturbed reproducibly.

However, the current generator calls NewFace independently six times.
Inspection of its generated database reports:

  Valid: YES
  Solid: NO
  Plate mode: YES, zero thickness
  Faces: 6
  Edges: 24
  Vertices: 24
  Trims: 24

Each face owns four boundary edges; matching geometric edges are not shared.
That form must not be treated as the solid oracle.

7.2 Shared fixture support

Move the duplicated Cobb face construction in brep_cobb.cpp and
brep_invalid.cpp into internal reusable test support.  Preserve the existing
command's output for compatibility, then add constructors for:

  1. cobb_unsewn: the current six independent faces, used for plate-mode and
     legacy rendering tests.

  2. cobb_sewn: the same six surfaces with cube topology: eight shared
     vertices, twelve shared 3D edges, twenty-four trims, two mated trims per
     edge, six oriented outer loops, and consistent face orientation.

  3. cobb_variant: a deep copy of cobb_sewn with one deterministic
     perturbation specification applied.

The sewn constructor must pass IsValid, IsSolid, and IsManifold with oriented
true, boundary false, and exactly two trims on every edge.  Before using it as
an exact oracle, sample every patch against the unit sphere and record maximum
radial error.  The expected result is roundoff-level disagreement; tests must
use the measured baseline rather than assuming zero.

7.3 Perturbation representation

Define a test-only perturbation specification containing:

  family
  target face/edge/vertex
  signed requested amplitude in model units
  declared edge/trim/vertex tolerance policy
  topology-preserving or topology-breaking flag
  scale and translation
  UV domain transform
  deterministic seed only for selecting a target, never for solver behavior

When moving a rational control point, preserve its weight.  Convert the
homogeneous CV to Euclidean P, apply delta, and store (P + delta) * w with the
same w.  Calibrate perturbations by measuring the resulting maximum 3D seam
discrepancy; a control-point displacement is not generally equal to maximum
curve displacement.

7.4 Required perturbation families

A. Boundary-row gap/overlap

Move the boundary row of one patch along the local outward radial or
co-normal direction while leaving the adjacent patch unchanged.  Test both a
gap and overlap.  Provide endpoint-preserving and endpoint-moving variants.

B. Bowed seam

Leave the two edge endpoints fixed and move the three interior boundary CVs.
This creates zero corner error and a maximum discrepancy near the edge
middle, defeating endpoint-only tolerance checks.

C. Vertex defect

Perturb one cube-corner control point or the boundary ends of one incident
face.  Sweep rays through and around the three-face vertex fan.

D. Surface-only disagreement

Move surface boundary CVs but keep the shared 3D edge, 2D trims, and topology
metadata unchanged.  This directly exercises ON_BrepEdge tolerance semantics.

E. Trim-only disagreement

Offset or bow one 2D trim inward/outward while retaining the surface and 3D
edge.  Test anisotropic u and v errors separately.  This exercises trim
parameter tolerances and 3D lifted-distance verification.

F. Edge-only disagreement

Perturb the shared 3D edge curve without changing either surface lift.  This
tests whether edge tolerance is used as metadata/evidence without incorrectly
moving the actual mathematical surface hits.

G. G1 discontinuity without a crack

Keep the common boundary CV row identical and move only the first interior CV
row.  Position remains continuous, but normals differ.  Shoot grazing rays to
ensure a normal discontinuity is not interpreted as a gap or duplicate solid
boundary.

H. Exact geometry-preserving representation changes

Insert knots, elevate degree where supported, split one patch into multiple
faces with shared topology, reverse a parameter direction, and apply extreme
affine domain scalings such as [0,1e6] by [0,1e-6].  These variants must give
the same result as pristine Cobb.  A rational linear-fractional
reparameterization may be added if OpenNURBS can construct it exactly.  Do
not label an approximate refit as geometry-preserving.

I. Combined trim and surface error

Move a surface boundary and its trim in different directions with separately
declared tolerances.  This represents realistic imported geometry and tests
that UV and model-space errors are accumulated conservatively rather than
compared as one scalar.

7.5 Perturbation sweep

For every family, adapt the requested CV/trim change until the measured
maximum 3D mismatch g reaches these normalized targets relative to the
effective authorized seam envelope T:

  g/T = 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 2, 10

Independently test tolerance metadata with:

  * correct declared tolerance;
  * ON_UNSET_VALUE, requiring computation;
  * explicit zero;
  * declared tolerance half the actual discrepancy; and
  * declared tolerance twice the actual discrepancy.

The threshold behavior is part of the result:

  * At or below the authorized envelope, the solid interpretation must not
    leak and should match the implicit sphere outside the explicit uncertainty
    band.

  * Above the envelope, the implementation must report excessive discrepancy
    or fall back.  It must not silently increase tolerance until the sphere
    happens to close.

  * An over-declared tolerance may authorize a large disagreement because it
    explicitly records modeling accuracy, but it must be visible in
    diagnostics and can only affect the mated topology that declares it.

Run the matrix at radii well below, near, and well above one model unit, and at
large translations from the origin.  Equal g/T cases must behave consistently
under scale and translation within floating-point limits.

7.6 Directed Cobb rays

Generate deterministic families rather than relying on image pixels:

  * axes through patch centers;
  * exact rays through the north and south pole;
  * rays through every seam midpoint, normal and oblique to the local seam;
  * rays exactly along each seam tangent;
  * parallel offset sweeps across each seam on both sides;
  * rays through all eight vertices and small angular/positional fans around
    each vertex;
  * exact tangent rays at face interiors, seams, and vertices;
  * near-tangent angular sweeps on both sides of tangency;
  * rays whose two sphere roots approach each other toward tangency;
  * reversed versions of every ray; and
  * rays beginning inside as well as outside the sphere.

For a unit sphere, analytic line/sphere roots provide expected t values and
whether the ray is a miss, contact, or interval.  Compare the full ordered
partition, not only hit count.

7.7 Grazing-limit behavior study

Near-tangent behavior needs its own convergence study, not a handful of
special rays.  The study must approach tangency from both the hit and miss
sides and record how raw roots, final solid intervals, numerical conditioning,
and local-refinement work change.

For a sphere of radius R, use a unit-direction line whose perpendicular
distance from the center is b.  Define signed inward clearance:

  h = R - b

For h > 0 the analytic root separation/chord is:

  delta_t = 2 * sqrt(R^2 - b^2)
          = 2 * sqrt(2*R*h - h^2)

and, close to tangency:

  delta_t ~= 2 * sqrt(2*R*h)

At either root:

  |N dot D| = sqrt(R^2 - b^2) / R = delta_t / (2*R)

Thus h, delta_t, and |N dot D| give three independent ways to verify that the
test is approaching the same geometric limit.  At h = 0 there is one double
root/contact and no material interval.  At h < 0 there is no real root.

Use a logarithmic signed sweep rather than linear pixel offsets.  Include:

  * h/R = 2^-k for k spanning ordinary chords down to the representable
    floating-point/tolerance limit;
  * cases selected so delta_t/rti_tol.dist is 100, 10, 2, 1.1, 1, 0.9,
    0.5, 0.1, and smaller;
  * exact h = 0 constructed analytically rather than by an accumulated camera
    transform; and
  * matching negative h values on the miss side.

Run every case against the implicit sphere, its normal ft_brep conversion, the
pristine sewn Cobb sphere, and selected perturbed Cobb seams.  Repeat at face
interiors, seam midpoints, and vertices; reverse the ray; rotate the geometry;
use non-unit ray directions; and repeat over object scale and translation.

Capture two levels of result:

  1. Raw primitive observations before rt_boolfinal: isolated parameter boxes,
     mathematical candidate roots, residuals, t intervals, normal dot values,
     solver outcome, subdivision depth/count, and fallback use.

  2. Normal librt partitions after Boolean tolerance processing: miss,
     contact/zero-thickness event, or material interval and its endpoints.

The expected trend from the hit side is:

  * delta_t decreases monotonically according to the analytic square-root
    law;

  * the two raw simple roots remain present and ordered while their isolating
    intervals can be separated;

  * absolute endpoint error stays bounded even though relative error in a
    vanishing chord necessarily grows;

  * refinement effort and conditioning warnings may grow, but failure status
    remains deterministic and fallback is explicit;

  * once delta_t enters the rti_tol.dist regime, final partition behavior
    matches the implicit oracle and rt_boolfinal policy; and

  * at exact tangency the candidate is a CONTACT_TANGENT with zero winding
    change, followed by a stable miss on the negative-h side.

Do not impose a fixed grazing-normal cutoff as the expected transition.  The
meaningful transition is governed by root isolation, analytic geometry, and
the final minimum-segment policy.  Plot or tabulate classification, measured
delta_t, endpoint error, iterations, subdivisions, and fallback rate against
both h/R and delta_t/rti_tol.dist.  There must be no non-monotone islands in
which a sequence approaching tangency alternates between ordinary hit, miss,
odd hit, and hit again.

After the sphere is correct, repeat the limit study on curved geometries with
different local behavior:

  * ellipsoids at high- and low-curvature principal directions;
  * torus outer and inner tangencies, including close pairs among four roots;
  * a cylinder tangent in its curved direction and rays nearly parallel to
    its zero-curvature direction;
  * converted cone/quadric surfaces near caps and patch boundaries; and
  * G1-discontinuous but position-continuous Cobb seams.

For a regular surface with normal clearance h and normal curvature kappa in
the ray direction, the local quadratic root separation is proportional to
sqrt(h/kappa).  Use that prediction to normalize non-spherical trends, while
using the implicit primitive's actual ray results as the authoritative oracle.
Parabolic, singular, and multi-root cases should be labeled separately rather
than forced into the quadratic model.

7.8 Machine-readable limit report

The full sweep tool should emit CSV or JSON containing:

  fixture and perturbation family
  target topology
  object scale and translation
  UV transform
  requested displacement
  measured gap/overlap
  declared and inferred tolerances
  normalized g/T
  ray family and angle
  signed tangent clearance h and h/R
  analytic and measured root separation
  delta_t/rti_tol.dist and |N dot D|
  implicit and BREP ordered intervals
  maximum endpoint error
  candidate and resolved-event counts
  solver/refinement outcomes
  seam closure decision
  fallback reason
  allocation/workspace high-water marks
  Crofton area and volume estimates with uncertainty

The report should make it possible to plot exactly when a particular
perturbation stops behaving like the implicit sphere.


8. GENERAL IMPLICIT-TO-BREP CORRECTNESS TESTING
-----------------------------------------------

This is the primary correctness program.  The STEP corpus is valuable for
coverage and performance, but it generally lacks an independent exact answer.
In contrast, BRL-CAD can raytrace a known CSG model with mature implicit
primitive code, convert that same model to BREP, and compare two
representations of the same intended solid.  No BREP solver change should be
promoted on corpus images alone if it fails this paired ground truth.

8.1 Existing foundation

src/librt/tests/primitive_brep.cpp already checks that several ft_brep
conversions produce valid, solid, oriented manifolds with paired edges.  It
does not currently shoot the converted geometry.  Extend this testing in a
new focused executable rather than overloading topology-only assertions.

Start with primitives that have reliable implicit raytracers and analytic
area/volume formulas:

  * sphere/ellipsoid;
  * box/ARB8;
  * right circular cylinder;
  * truncated cone;
  * torus; and
  * additional converted quadrics after the basic harness is stable.

Keep more complicated EPA/EHY/HYP/PART/extrude conversions in the topology
suite and add them to paired ray testing when suitable ground truth is
available.

Add a second tier of actual CSG regions converted to BREP.  The original CSG
tree remains the raytracing oracle even when a compact analytic area formula
is unavailable.  The initial set should include:

  * disjoint union, producing several material intervals on one line;
  * overlapping sphere union;
  * two-sphere intersection lens;
  * sphere or cylinder subtraction producing an internal cavity;
  * box minus cylinder, producing planar/non-planar trimmed faces;
  * nested union/subtraction with multiple entry and exit events; and
  * shells and thin walls at several multiples of rti_tol.dist.

Begin with operands separated from coincident and sub-tolerance degeneracies
so the conversion has one clear intended result.  Then introduce deliberate
near-coincident faces and thin features as a separate tolerance-policy suite.
Do not mix conversion ambiguity into the baseline solver gate.

8.2 Paired ray comparison

For each primitive construct the implicit object and its ft_brep conversion in
separate prepared rt_i instances.  Use the identical ray origin and direction
against both.  Record all returned partitions after normal librt Boolean
processing.

For CSG-region cases, prepare the original CSG tree in one rt_i and the
converted BREP result in the other.  Use the same region semantics, ray
minimum/maximum range, rti_tol, overlap policy, and explicitly shared sampling
bounding box.  Compare both raw primitive segments where practical and final
partitions; the latter are the solid behavioral contract.

Compare:

  * miss versus hit;
  * number and order of material intervals;
  * entry and exit t within model-space tolerance;
  * entry/exit orientation;
  * forward/reverse symmetry;
  * finite point, normal, curvature, and UV outputs; and
  * solver/fallback status.

Classify rays inside a defined analytic tangency band separately.  They must
remain deterministic and safe, but an exact tangent should not be used to
claim a statistically meaningful inside interval.

The paired harness should report the first differing ray with enough data to
replay it as a single directed test.  Aggregate-only pass/fail is insufficient:
opposite errors in crossing count or chord length can cancel in a Crofton
estimate.

Generally speaking the CSG intersections should be reliable, but it is possible
that grazing behaviors or limit cases might hit CSG issues.  When we have a
disagreement between a CSG ray and a brep ray, it is reasonable to double check
the CSG answer if it seems to be confounding the analysis.

8.3 Cauchy-Crofton verification

src/librt/primitives/crofton.cpp implements:

  SA = 4*pi*R^2 * crossings / (2*rays)
  V  = pi*R^2 * total_chord / rays

with a fixed RNG seed.  src/librt/tests/crofton.c already checks analytic
primitives and convergence behavior.

For implicit/CSG-to-BREP comparison, generate each deterministic line once
from one explicitly shared bounding sphere and feed it to both
representations.  Do not allow small differences in prepared bounding boxes
to change the sample.  For each line retain the complete ordered partitions,
crossing count, and total chord for both representations.  Compute per-line
differences as well as independent aggregates.

Implement this without weakening the production Crofton API: extract or
reproduce its fixed-seed line generator in a librt test helper, materialize a
read-only array of line origins/directions, and shoot that array through two
prepared rt_i instances with a comparison callback that copies each final
partition into bounded test records.  Feed the same per-line crossing and
chord values into the existing Crofton formulas.  The ordinary
rt_crofton_shoot entry point should remain the production estimator; the
paired harness is an exact A/B validation tool.

Use progressive sample levels such as 2,000, 8,000, 32,000, and 128,000.
Compute batch/block estimates so the test reports standard error rather than
using only successive-estimate stability.  Acceptance should require:

  * analytic area and volume inside a preselected confidence band;
  * BREP and implicit paired estimates inside that band;
  * no persistent bias as sample count increases; and
  * directed ray tests passing independently, since random Crofton rays will
    almost never hit an exact seam, pole, or tangent.

In addition, report:

  * fraction of lines with different hit/miss classification;
  * fraction with a different number of material intervals;
  * crossing-count difference distribution;
  * total absolute and signed chord-length difference;
  * the contribution of differing lines to area and volume error; and
  * disagreement stratified by grazing angle, trim proximity, and solver
    fallback status.

For baseline analytic and nondegenerate CSG conversions, the target is no
unexplained paired partition difference outside the explicit numerical and
tangent uncertainty bands.  Merely obtaining an area/volume estimate within a
few percent is not sufficient.  Crofton then supplies a powerful integrated
check that the remaining enormous ray population has no systematic bias.

Volume is generally the more stable solid test near minimum-size slivers.
Surface area is more sensitive to representation and angular survival of thin
features, as documented in include/rt/func.h.  Report both; do not dismiss a
stable area disagreement as sampling noise merely because volume agrees.

8.4 Later-stage hierarchy duality checks

After the individual primitive and small controlled CSG gates are stable, add
two more stressful comparisons.  These are valuable qualification targets,
but they must not replace the simpler fixtures that localize a failure to leaf
intersection, Boolean assembly, or conversion.

The first comparison preserves an existing implicit CSG hierarchy exactly and
replaces every leaf having an exact ft_brep conversion with a BREP leaf.  Keep
all union/intersection/subtraction operators, matrices, region attributes,
tolerances, ray bounds, and overlap policy unchanged.  Do not evaluate the
tree into one BREP for this test: the purpose is to compare the mature implicit
leaf solvers against NURBS leaf solvers under identical librt Boolean weaving.
Build the original and substituted databases in memory, shoot the identical
directed and Crofton lines through both, and retain ordered final partitions.
Classify unsupported leaves explicitly and begin with fully replaceable trees;
mixed trees can follow once the all-BREP-leaf case is understood.

The second comparison runs in the opposite direction.  BRL-CAD's experimental
BREP shape recognizer, exposed by the libged `brep csg` command, calls
brep_to_csg() and may reconstruct either a complete implicit CSG tree or a
partial tree retaining residual BREP pieces.  Apply it to simple controlled
BREPs first and then to the NIST example solids expected to be substantially
or fully recognizable.  Compare the original BREP against the reconstructed
tree with the same shared-line partition harness.  Record whether each result
is:

  * fully implicit, providing the strongest independent intersection oracle;
  * mixed implicit/BREP, which still stresses recognition, transformations,
    tree semantics, and the recognized leaves but is not fully independent;
  * unsupported without mutation; or
  * conversion-failed/invalid, which is a conversion result and must not be
    misreported as a ray-solver discrepancy.

For both directions, serialize the first disagreeing model and ray into a
small replayable C/C++ fixture or database artifact.  Preserve passing cases
permanently.  A conversion improvement may reduce a documented disagreement,
but never encode the old incorrect tree or partition as the expected answer.
These dual conversions are later qualification tiers because they exercise
more code and make root-cause attribution harder than the analytic primitive,
Cobb, and direct BREP-leaf CSG corpora.


9. DRAWING IMPROVEMENTS AND TESTING
-----------------------------------

Drawing work should follow the ray correctness foundation but use the same
analytic and perturbed fixtures.

9.1 Wireframe edges

Replace endpoint-distance-only adaptive decisions with a criterion combining:

  * view-derived point spacing;
  * model-space chord deviation at midpoint/extrema;
  * tangent-angle change; and
  * mandatory subdivision at curve knots and discontinuities.

Always terminate with explicit depth/segment limits and report limit hits in
debug mode.  Evaluate points and tangents for success and finiteness before
adding vlist entries.

9.2 Surface indication

The default edge-only view is ambiguous for closed or sparsely bounded
surfaces.  Provide a surface indication independent of the ray SurfaceTree:

  * use trimmed adaptive isocurves for lightweight wireframe display; or
  * derive display lines from the existing CDT representation when a
    tessellated view is requested.

Do not special-case only ON_SumSurface and ON_RevSurface.  Any interior
isocurve must be clipped against trim loops using parameter-space topology and
must handle closed parameter seams.

9.3 Drawing acceptance

For pristine and perturbed Cobb variants verify:

  * finite points only;
  * all declared boundary edges represented;
  * no long spikes from failed evaluation or seam wrapping;
  * no missing patch at a pole or closed surface seam;
  * stable output under equivalent UV domain scaling; and
  * monotonic refinement as view/model tolerance tightens.

Image regression can supplement, but geometric vlist invariants should be the
primary automated tests.

11. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
-----------------------

Correctness:

  * Implicit and BREP ordered partitions agree for all non-ambiguous directed
    analytic rays within rti_tol.dist.

  * Original CSG regions and their converted BREPs have no unexplained paired
    partition difference on the shared deterministic Crofton line set outside
    explicit numeric/tangent uncertainty bands.

  * Later-stage topology-identical implicit-leaf and BREP-leaf CSG hierarchies
    agree on ordered partitions.  Fully reconstructed BREP-to-CSG NIST cases
    likewise agree; mixed reconstructions report their residual BREP content
    and are not claimed as fully independent oracles.

  * Paired Crofton crossing and chord differences converge without systematic
    bias, and independent surface-area and volume estimates agree with
    analytic values wherever such formulas are available.

  * Exact sphere pole shots succeed deterministically from both directions.

  * Tangent contacts do not create material intervals; nearby two-root cases
    remain distinct when their interval is meaningful at BRL-CAD tolerance.

  * In the signed grazing sweep, raw root separation follows the analytic
    trend from two roots through a double contact to no roots.  Final librt
    classification matches the implicit oracle and has at most one contiguous
    tolerance-controlled transition band, with no odd-hit or alternating
    hit/miss islands.

  * Sewn Cobb seam/vertex rays do not leak for g <= T.

  * g > T never triggers silent closure.

  * Equivalent UV reparameterizations do not change hit classification.

  * Nonadjacent faces are never joined based only on proximity.

  * Plate-mode behavior remains separately correct.

  * Crofton area and volume converge to analytic and paired implicit values
    within measured statistical confidence, with no increasing-sample bias.

Robustness:

  * All surface/derivative/normal failures become structured outcomes.

  * No NaN or infinity reaches a hit, segment, vlist, bbox, or serialized
    cache.

  * Workspace overflow, unsupported order/weights, non-manifold topology, and
    excessive seam discrepancy use an explicit fallback or diagnostic.

  * Parallel prep and shots are repeatable and sanitizer-clean.

Performance:

  * The new shot fast path performs no general heap allocation.

  * Prepared BBNode/trim-list counts and serialized cache size fall
    substantially when density is reduced.

  * FRAME.g prep wall time and peak RSS improve materially from the recorded
    approximately 33.4 seconds and 3.05 GB baseline.

  * Total render performance, including local refinement, is no worse on
    ordinary rays and is measured separately for deliberately grazing rays.

  * Fallback frequency and workspace high-water marks are low and stable on a
    representative corpus subset before the new path becomes default.


12. RISKS AND REQUIRED SAFEGUARDS
---------------------------------

False exclusion in local bounds
  This is the most serious algorithmic risk.  Use Bernstein/projective bounds
  only when their preconditions hold.  Unsafe weights or arithmetic states
  fall back.  Tests must contain high-degree and extreme-weight surfaces.

Tolerance inflation
  Automatically using measured error as permission can heal arbitrarily bad
  geometry.  Only ON_UNSET_VALUE authorizes inferred topology tolerance;
  explicitly set values remain authoritative.  Always report large envelopes.

UV singularities
  Inverse surface metrics can explode at a pole.  UV is only a broad phase;
  final proximity is in 3D.  Use one-sided evaluation and bounded queries.

Close roots versus crack observations
  Never merge solely by t distance.  Require topology, isolated-root, and
  local-state evidence.  Preserve ambiguity until solid-event resolution.

Fixed workspace
  Fixed capacity must not imply fixed correctness.  Every overflow is an
  observable fallback.  Corpus high-water metrics determine later tuning.

Legacy invalid geometry
  The current heuristics accommodate unsolid geometry.  Do not remove the old
  path until fallback telemetry identifies what real corpus cases still need
  it.  Do not let those cases weaken the well-defined solid path.

Cache compatibility
  Version all new serialized data.  Reject incomplete or old records and
  rebuild.  Exercise repeated load/free cycles under memory checking.

Crofton interpretation
  Dense sampling cannot repair a systematic raytracing bias or a
  representation-dependent sub-tolerance sliver.  Always inspect paired ray
  differences and block variance, not only the final scalar estimate.

14. RECOMMENDED FIRST CONCRETE CHANGE SET
-----------------------------------------

The first implementation change should deliberately avoid changing tree
density or seam semantics.  It should contain only:

  1. Prepared ownership repair and cache-deserialization ownership.

  2. SurfaceTree root/CurveTree transfer with immediate destruction of the
     temporary builder and its SurfaceSplitCache.

  3. Removal of unused m_face_user.p storage.

  4. Structured solver outcomes, initial-residual success, deterministic
     singular handling, finite checks, and safe normal evaluation.

  5. Directed sphere pole, boundary, seam, and reverse-ray tests.

  6. Baseline counters needed to compare later phases.

This produces an independently reviewable correctness and memory-management
improvement.  The Cobb seam framework and tolerance resolver should be the
next change set, followed by local Bezier isolation.  Only then should the
default surface-tree depth be reduced.
