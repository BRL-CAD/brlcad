ESTABLISHED FINDINGS
--------------------

1. Commit 23eabb2c056fc7d0c4ccc6c5d9299f72eedbb432 correctly replaced the
   unsafe file-static SurfaceTree::hasSplit cache with a per-SurfaceTree
   SurfaceSplitCache.  Do not restore the static cache.

2. brep_build_bvh currently leaks each SurfaceTree.  This leak prevents
   SurfaceTree::~SurfaceTree from double-deleting its root after that root is
   attached to the master BVH.  CurveTrees likewise lack an owner after cache
   deserialization.  No repository reader of ON_BrepFace::m_face_user.p was
   found.

3. The current Newton solver fails to accept an initially converged root before
   checking its Jacobian.  Its singular "jitter" is identically zero on the
   first iteration and uses drand48.  Ev1Der/finite/normal results need checks.

4. The current fixed BREP_EDGE_MISS_TOLERANCE is used in incompatible UV and
   model-space roles.  This will be addressed only after Phase 1 behavior is
   isolated and tested.

5. The historical Cobb generator is a valid but non-solid six-face patch set
   with 24 independent edges.  libbrep now provides both that representation
   and a valid, closed, oriented variant with 8 shared vertices, 12 shared
   edges, and 24 trims.  The support surfaces are an exact sphere to sampled
   roundoff.  Controlled seam/trim perturbations are still required.

6. Primary correctness validation will pair original implicit/CSG raytracing
   with converted BREP raytracing on identical lines, including Crofton
   crossing/chord statistics.  Grazing tests will sweep signed clearance and
   verify the two-root -> tangent-contact -> no-root limit.

7. The old native ID_BSPLINE ray path dates to the 1991 Release 4.0 NURB
   module.  It was compile-time unreachable after ID_BSPLINE began converting
   to BREP in 2009, and it could not compile if re-enabled after its hit-list
   helpers were deleted in 2010.  Its live nmg_nurb_intersect engine projects
   a surface onto two ray planes, applies control-hull clipping, and recursively
   subdivides.  Those concepts are useful precedents, but the implementation
   allocates and copies surfaces during recursion, has fixed-size/order
   assumptions, stops on a UV-only tolerance, has no trim support, and the old
   wrapper fabricated a 0.01-unit exit for odd hit counts.  Do not port it.

8. The first R=10 mm grazing sweep found a deterministic but incorrect
   baseline.  As h -> 0+ the converted BREP chord stopped converging and
   approached about 0.001812758 mm.  At exact tangency it reported that false
   interval, and it reported false intervals for small negative h out to
   between approximately -7.45e-8 mm and -1.49e-7 mm.  The conditioned root
   test described in Finding 11 has repaired this baseline.  Keep the history
   as evidence for why the permanent exact-tangent and negative-h gates exist.

9. Solver work follows a corpus-first ratchet rule learned from the STEP work:
   reproduce difficult cases in C/C++ with analytic or paired implicit
   oracles, keep permanent passing gates, permit documented defects to improve
   without freezing their bad answer, and promote each repaired defect to a
   hard gate.  Avoid isolated changes tested only against their motivating
   model.

10. A ray starting exactly on the +X sphere seam and pointing outward is a
    raw miss in rt_sph_shot, which explicitly rejects an outside/on-surface
    origin pointing away.  Current BREP shot instead returns the wholly
    nonpositive interval [-2R, 0].  The in-memory corpus permits that known
    result to disappear, but requires determinism and forbids any forward
    material.  Once BREP applies the normal half-ray/partition policy, promote
    this case to exact implicit parity.

11. A fixed perpendicular line residual is not a sufficient root criterion at
    tangency.  For a unit surface normal N and ray direction D, residual divided
    by |N dot D|/|D| is the first-order error along the ray.  Root acceptance
    now requires that estimate to satisfy the numerical root tolerance, with a
    1e-13 residual floor for exact/double contact.  Residuals are evaluated
    from S-ray_origin instead of a global plane offset, avoiding cancellation
    on million-unit translated fixtures.  This retained the singular north
    pole, small/large scale, and translated cases while repairing the grazing
    limit.  This is solver conditioning, not the final contact or seam policy.

12. The correct first CSG stress test preserves the Boolean hierarchy and
    replaces only exactly convertible implicit leaves with BREP leaves.  It
    does not collapse the tree through ON_Boolean.  Disjoint and overlapping
    sphere unions, a sphere-intersection lens, a concentric subtraction
    cavity, and box-minus-cylinder all agree on every directed and shared line
    in this form.

13. A diagnostic experiment that did collapse the same binary trees to single
    evaluated BREPs separated Boolean-conversion defects from ray-solver
    defects.  Disjoint sphere union and concentric cavity happened to match,
    but overlapping union, intersection lens, and box-minus-cylinder were
    geometrically wrong before raytracing.  At 8,000 lines, overlapping union
    produced BREP area/volume about 313.2/527.7 versus implicit 497.6/937.5;
    the lens produced about 265.4/469.5 versus 121.9/105.5; and the drilled
    box produced zero BREP chord volume with invalid partitions.  Treat this
    as a later ON_Boolean/conversion qualification problem, not evidence
    against the leaf BREP intersection solver.

14. Two later-stage duality tiers are now specified in the plan: replacing
    exactly convertible leaves throughout larger existing CSG databases while
    preserving their trees, and using the experimental `brep csg`/
    brep_to_csg shape recognizer to reconstruct full or partial implicit trees
    from controlled and NIST BREP examples.  Fully implicit reconstructions
    are independent oracles; mixed reconstructions must disclose residual
    BREP leaves and make a weaker claim.

15. The openNURBS expert NewFace documentation says its vertex indices are
    ordered (SW, SE, NW, NE), but NewOuterLoop in the implementation consumes
    them in boundary order (SW, SE, NE, NW) and relates edge i to vertices i
    and (i+1)%4.  Reusing two adjacent Cobb edges exposed the inconsistency.
    Cobb construction follows the implementation order and the topology tests
    make this dependency observable.

    not currently spread to h greater than the measured gap; that is now a
    one-way ratchet, while any repaired cases are allowed to disappear.

    cannot gate endpoint error directly by g.  It must distinguish geometric
    support uncertainty, root conditioning, interval existence/winding, and
    final minimum-segment policy.

    fixtures.

40. A provisional interval Krawczyk test now supplies the first adaptive
    early-termination rule for ordinary prepared surface spans.  Given a
    bounded corrector root x in local parameter box X, it computes

      K(X) = x - C F(x) + (I - C J(X)) (X - x),

    where F is the pair of homogeneous ray-plane Bernstein polynomials, J(X)
    is bounded by the convex hulls of their derivative Bernstein
    coefficients, and C is the inverse of the midpoint interval Jacobian.
    Only strict containment of K(X) in the interior of X qualifies.  A failed
    corrector, singular preconditioner, failed inclusion, tangent Jacobian, or
    root on a subdivision/patch boundary says nothing and continues the
    existing subdivision path to depth 24.  Small UV, model-space, or ray-t
    width and numerical convergence alone never terminate a box.

    The implementation uses fixed arrays and performs no ray-time allocation.
    Coefficient uncertainty is propagated into function and derivative
    intervals; interval endpoints receive deliberately conservative error
    enlargement and nextafter expansion.  Corrector attempts/convergences,
    successful Krawczyk boxes and their depths, isolated-box ray-t widths,
    subdivision work, overflow, and high-water marks are observable.  The
    continuation certificate deliberately disables this new rule, preserving
    its prior depth-24 behavior.

    partition with endpoints within model tolerance.  They produce 12

42. The function/Jacobian arithmetic layer of the provisional Krawczyk test
    now has an independent enclosure audit.  Scalar double evaluation of
    F(x) plus a post hoc magnitude estimate has been removed.  Each uncertain
    Bernstein coefficient begins as an outward interval, and tensor de
    Casteljau evaluation uses outward interval scaling and addition at every
    level.  Derivative Bernstein coefficients are likewise formed as
    intervals before their convex hulls are taken.  Krawczyk matrix products
    use the same outward interval multiplication.

    Primitive interval operations now expand each computed endpoint by one
    `nextafter` step instead of adding a fixed `128 epsilon * max(1, |x|)`
    margin.  The old absolute floor was conservative but destroyed binary
    scale covariance for small coefficients.  One outward representable step
    around each finite IEEE operation encloses its exact result regardless of
    whether the active rounding mode rounded that operation up or down.  All
    nonfinite results remain rejection paths.

    A private librt test hook publishes only the computed function, Jacobian,
    and product intervals.  The independent C++ oracle represents generated
    coefficients as exact signed dyadics, evaluates their tensor midpoint by
    exact integer addition and exponent adjustment, and computes derivative
    control coefficients exactly.  It then verifies that production
    intervals contain the nominal value plus the complete declared
    coefficient uncertainty.  This avoids relying on the implementation's
